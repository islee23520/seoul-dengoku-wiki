## Common Forms and Variants

The Cryo-Spore Film was a gray-white composite membrane clinging thinly to freezer panels and the seams of transport boxes and low-temperature piping. Its surface looked like matte vinyl, but its cross-section repeated hydrophilic polymer lattices, silica hollow fiber, and dormant spore layers. Its normal thickness was only a few millimeters, and its moisture-laden edges could swell to nearly three times that.

The Cryo-Spore Film's common ecology responded passively to facility surfaces and the local environment. The G23E identifiers marked biomechanical variants whose movement and work functions had differentiated within that environment; they were not treated as one individual with the common film or the whole installation.

- **Role · body:** The Frost-Breath Filter Pouch was a biomechanical filter contracting its wrinkled lung-pouch to draw in the cold-store air and catch spores in the frost layer. It sensed micro-vibration and organic smells, and could not tell blocked doors or fully sealed vessels apart.
- **Origin:** After the collapse, spores and filter fibers combined in the S07·Jamsil Line refrigerated corridor and the Cryo-Spore Film formed. A Seoul creative origin of cold-store spore-film formation; the outer pressure coming from XT03 was recorded only as a variable of the colony's movement.
- **Habitat:** The ventilation intake of the Wangsimni coalition's freezer was its core habitat. In dry seasons it moved to the Jamsil Line's food-storage corridor, and when XT03 hot wind or pressure waves strengthened it flattened itself onto floor ducts.
- **Needs:** It needed rotting organics and cooled airflow. It competed with HP09's food-residue recovery, but supplied with high-grade spoiled waste in sealed bins it never approached preservable food.
- **Life · maintenance:** It followed the five stages emergence–spread–stagnation–negotiable–dissolution, and its spore-film area never exceeded the ceiling of organics and cooling capacity. When the lung-pouch's condensate drain clogged, inner ice tore and it lost its filtering, dissolving into a thin film.
- **Behavior · hierarchy:** One controller called the breath-hider core matched the breathing of several filter pouches by pressure pulsation. Even when F07 reported high contamination, on clash with HP09's sample tests it slowed its breathing and waited for the human verdict.
- **Escalation:** Stage one warned with cold breath and spore smell; stage two lowered ventilation to hamper store operation; stage three inflated the pouches to block entrances. It rose when sealed waste bins were opened or spoiled food was left near the core nest.
- **Engagement · counter:** Irregular noise broke the breathing beat, and modest extra cooling slowed spore activity before section-sealing the vents. Organic bait could be sent down a separate duct to change its route; flame destroyed the refrigeration plant and stored food together.
- **Negotiation · moral choice:** HP09 could trade regular supply of waste food and vent-use times. Removing the colony restored airflow at once but raised the risk that the mold spores it had caught would spread into citizens' stores.
- **Scenario links:** In G23-SC1 it became the biological index separating contaminated from preserved food. In G23-SC2 the breath-hider core's waste supply had to be agreed before opening the blocked vent, and in G23-SC3 it took the scene verifying F07's excess alarms through sample tests.

## Habitat Record

The Cryo-Spore Film was a gray-white composite membrane clinging thinly to freezer panels and the seams of transport boxes and low-temperature piping.

Its surface looked like matte vinyl, but its cross-section repeated hydrophilic polymer lattices, silica hollow fiber, and dormant spore layers.

Its normal thickness was only a few millimeters, and its moisture-laden edges could swell to nearly three times that.

The film's core action lay not in producing or absorbing heat but in changing its paths of movement.

As the temperature difference across its faces grew, some capillaries contracted and the share of in-plane paths through connected hollow fibers rose.

The result was less frontal heat leakage, but heat crowded into frames and bolt-adhesive boundaries, making unforeseen cold points and hot layers.

Where moisture was present a different reaction occurred.

The hydrophilic edges drew in condensate and swelled, quickly sealing small gaps.

Seen as airtightness alone it was useful, but when water behind the seal could not escape, thin ice pouches grew and pushed panel surfaces and packaging from inside.

Sensor divergence was the first sign to check when handling this film.

An infrared camera showed only the cold flat surface, while contact thermocouples read the heat riding the frames, and fiber optics inside the wall caught delayed changes behind the ice layer.

The three values differing did not at once mean one was broken; a map including sensor depth and heat-pulse arrival time had to be redrawn.

Defrosting ran in zones at low output.

Heating the surface all at once made the polymer skin and inner fiber layers expand at different speeds, making fine cracks.

When cracks opened, dormant spores fell as dry powder, so local exhaust, sealed particle collectors, and respiratory protection were needed.

Vibration made a damage path separate from thermal shock.

Repeated shear transmitted from compressors, portable freezers, stage lifts, and transport carts fatigued the viscoelastic adhesive layer from its edges.

Each vibration turned the lifted parts into air paths opening and closing, swinging sensor values and inviting the automatic controllers' excess cooling and needless defrosting.

In the field an aluminum decoy cooling plate, colder than the actual preserved goods, was set on the far side of the work.

Condensate and fallen spores were guided to gather on that plate first, then a dry anhydrous break of fixed width was made with molecular-sieve desiccant and dry nitrogen.

The cooling plate was replaced into a sealed vessel before saturating, and when the break's dewpoint rose, incision stopped.

When opening sealed packaging, the contents were not warmed to peel the film off.

The whole package went into a low-temperature glove tent; holding the inner preservation temperature, a thin fluororesist barrier plate was slid between outer skin and film to limit cutting depth.

Partially thawed moisture refreezing could press specimen vessels and tissue with ice lenses, so temperature history, more than the cut line, became the stopping criterion.

Management procedures presupposed no intent or promise of the film.

When the work leader adjusted heat-source placement, humidity, vibration transmission, exhaust volume, and sensor position, the film reacted passively to those physical conditions.

Safe recovery depended not on the surface looking quiet but on whether hidden heat layers had been checked, the anhydrous break held, and spore collection and freezing temperatures kept to the end.
