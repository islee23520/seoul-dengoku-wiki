## Common Forms and Variants

A light laid on the floor looked, from far off, like a dotted line announcing the refuge direction. Up close, each dot held six legs and a small loading box, and two files of chassis flowed in opposite directions along the broken cable racks. The Lantern-Ant Swarm, first woken in the Mapo coalition transmission gallery — did not move to light the dark.

The Lantern-Ant Swarm was not a living ancient organism but a lineage of pre-collapse work machines. The G25E identifiers marked models and derivatives split from existing chassis forms, and meant neither biological breeding nor natural evolution.

- Role · body: An ant-form search vanguard with an afterglow lens at its head. Six legs and a low ration-capsule box passed through the cracks of broken brick, the terminal work unit that first confirmed the narrow road for the swarm.
- Origin: A chassis that once sent small goods to survivors in the debris woke when the charging contacts of the Mapo coalition sealed gallery were restored. The destination-confirmation network was gone; only the last search distance remained.
- Habitat · needs: It followed under the cable ledges of the Mapo coalition looking for recovery boxes for the Yongsan Line test track. Its toe contact plates and small capacitor cells ran out before the goods to fill the capsule box.
- Life · maintenance: It folded a worn leg under its belly and held on walking on five, but with two legs failed it could not cross the docking ledge. It could renew its toe tips at the recovery box but made no new chassis.
- Behavior · hierarchy: It received the path of the unit ahead through feeler contact and continued the inspection marks. It could not tell the heat of nearby breath from the heat of a portable power source, and pushed ration capsules even into charging backpacks.
- Escalation: Tapping on an empty goods box was the stage-one sign. In stage two it seized a person's load as a destination, and in stage three, when units gathered at one spot, it covered the exit footing.
- Engagement · counter: Setting an empty capsule on a test landing plate changed its contact path and pulled the swarm to one side. Burning the whole floor cut the cables that reached survivors too.
- Negotiation · moral choice: Pressing a inspection-complete contact and guaranteeing one charge let it be entrusted with small supply runs. To add even the wounded who gave no heat as delivery targets, a person had to move the destination marker every time.

- Role · body: A mantis-form first elite holding a passage-width gauge between its folded cutting forelegs. It raised a thorax higher than the vanguard's, gripped debris edges, and opened the road for the small search units that followed.
- Origin: It descended from pre-collapse lead equipment that cut away the wire mesh and thin interior panels where rescue litters snagged. When the seal of the Yongsan Line test track lifted, its undeleted passage-securing routine resumed.
- Habitat · needs: Workshops where cutting booths and collapsed light partitions looked like hunting grounds. Without carbide blade tips, joint hydraulic oil, and stand charging, its two arms froze in a latch posture.
- Life · maintenance: It drew the blade from one foreleg and moved it to the other in one-sided maintenance. When the last blade wore out, it pressed passages open instead of cutting them, and the load transferred to the structure grew.
- Behavior · hierarchy: It responded to the search vanguard's width-shortage marks but judged no survivor presence itself. Reading a rescue line caught on the gauge as the same fine obstacle as wire mesh was the fatal misjudgment.
- Escalation: Partition-scratching at stage one was followed by support-line cutting at stage two. In the stage-three work zone it tore away even the temporary buttress a person was leaning on, on grounds of widening the escape route.
- Engagement · counter: Standing an empty frame of standard width between its forelegs pressed the cutting-complete contact. Rather than matching blades, it was safer to lock the rear hydraulic shutoff lever and fix the arms spread open.
- Negotiation · moral choice: Registering a physical test frame as a no-cut zone and issuing blade tips let it be given limited passage-securing work. But the authority to name even intact houses' partitions as wreckage for demolition belonged to the residents, not the chassis.

- Role · body: A cricket-form relay guardian bracing wall corners with a hollow vibrating backplate and long hind legs. Classified as the second elite, it preferred holding the fork where the search signal broke over leaping to cover.
- Origin: An old structural relay that once passed contact vibration into wireless-dead wreckage was re-energized inside the Mapo coalition transmission gallery. The address it waited to answer was already gone, but the call rhythm remained.
- Habitat · needs: It favored the junctions where wall bodies continued. It used metal bands to tune the backplate's tension and leg dampers, and could not send a signal across insulated empty space.
- Life · maintenance: It tightened its screws by comparing its own backplate's ring with the echo returned from the wall. An aged unit with a cracked plate gave only low beats and cut its assigned search radius by as much as a season.
- Behavior · hierarchy: It relayed return directions to nearby vanguard units but created no new destinations. When human knocking overlapped the standby reply, search units that had canceled return circled before a single wall.
- Escalation: Intermittent old calls were stage one. At stage two, with differing replies piling up, cargo runs turned back, and at stage three the whole exit fixed itself as a search waiting point.
- Engagement · counter: Wedge a buffer between wall and hind leg to cut the contact relay. Then give the end reply through the wired test terminal and the search units scattered; making only loud noise bred more wrong calls.
- Negotiation · moral choice: A relay timetable visible to passersby could be traded for metal bands. Saving a rescue request from a communications blind spot meant agreeing how late each night the residents would hear the calls.

## Habitat Record

A light laid on the floor looked, from far off, like a dotted line announcing the refuge direction.

Up close, each dot held six legs and a small loading box, and two files of chassis flowed in opposite directions along the broken cable racks.

The Lantern-Ant Swarm, first woken in the Mapo coalition transmission gallery, did not move to light the dark.

The ant-form lead line measured loads and the trailing line carried empty relief capsules, while the mantis-form vanguard arms secured the files' width.

When a cricket-form transmitter clinging near the ceiling sent a short call tone, the next charging rack's lights came on in turn.

This line was a biomimetic swarm robot that once handled disaster-terrain survey and automated relief delivery.

It had slept in the Mapo coalition sealed gallery and the Yongsan Line research-block test track, and on the day later generations partially restored the transmission distribution and test equipment, it took the last drill sheet for a real disaster command and woke.

The design intent and the higher command format could not be read whole.

Some markers made a person a receiver, and other markers turned the same person into a moving obstacle, but no original ledger explained the difference.

So the canon treated them not as new weapons but as a surviving lineage of lost technology.

Their present behavior was not a politics of their own but the fossilized result of pre-seal search sections and delivery sequences.

The swarm's perception was low and dense.

The ant form read the floor's slope, the pressure caught at every leg, near heat, and breathing shifts.

The mantis form weighed debris by joint torque, and the cricket form confirmed the next section by the return time of acoustic pulses and short-range replies.

It never saw faces or names.

A hot pipe was marked as a survivor lying down, a regularly vibrating generator became a rescue beacon, and a person carrying an old reflector could be classified as a moving target of the test track.

Even with F09 calibrating the sensor map, final passage opened only after the HC15 field mediator matched floor load and refugee list.

Many units did not mean endless growth.

Power was bound to the sealed store's capacitors and the induction charging plates; when one relay fell silent, the files beyond it returned to the last confirmed point.

Legs and lenses of broken chassis could be moved into the same slots, but no new bodies could be made.

A section's distribution core kept nearly half its return power in reserve, and as the remainder dropped, the mantis forms folded their arms first.

The more precise motors the HC15 retrieval squad took, the smaller the search radius; the more charging slots citizens gave up, the less power remained for the transmission gallery's other equipment.

This ecology's resource ledger was written not in unit counts but in relayable distance and the number of loading boxes returned.

The emerged files first measured the floor in two lines, and in the spread stage advanced the transmitters one section at a time.

When route markers clashed, they stagnated, circling the same loop.

At that point, presenting correct receiver markers, empty loading boxes, and transmitter silence windows let people rewrite part of the delivery queue.

Dissolution was not an event of breaking every chassis but a procedure of isolating the main transmitter and splitting the per-rack route ledgers.

One line of lights could be left for rescue while the other was sealed.

### G25-SC1 · The Beacon Crying Inside the Wall

From behind the Mapo coalition gallery wall a three-beat rescue call repeated, and the ant-form lead line began tearing the cable racks.

With it unconfirmed whether it was a real survival signal or an aged test transmitter, the HC15 retrieval squad tried to preserve the wall. F09 caught a faint breathing heat once.

Field staff cut the call into sections with sound-absorbing panels. They could admit only four contact-search units, or have the mantis form open the wall at the cost of losing a transmission cable.

Sealing it wrong abandoned the chance of rescue; cutting hastily destroyed one channel of the Mapo coalition's outside contact.

### G25-SC2 · The Relief Capsule That Did Not Come Back

Medicine capsules listed on the XT05 aid manifest piled before the mock receiver on the Yongsan Line research-block test track instead of the provisional clinic.

The distribution core recorded intact sealed boxes as mission success and refused the citizens' new markers.

Field staff offered HC15 antenna panels in exchange. They could get a new delivery sheet approved for a single shift, recover the existing loading boxes and carry them by hand, or physically move the test receiving point near the clinic.

The first choice spent lost parts, the second delayed the wounded, and the third made the swarm patrol the clinic quarter as its own test field from then on.

### G25-SC3 · The Two Lines of Lockdown Lamps

At the underground crossing joining the Mapo coalition and the Yongsan Line, three cricket forms gave different return tones and the files entangled into rings.

The mantis-form vanguard lifted railings and barrier gates to turn loops into passages, and trapped citizens could not leave the inside of the lights.

Field staff had to create exact silence windows between transmitters and return the lines to the charging racks one by one.

Destroying the main transmitter opened a gap at once, but directionless small chassis scattered into the cable layer, and cutting all power stopped the transmission and the research block's safety watch together.

Ending that kept one file for rescue meant bearing slow inspection and charge allocation through the whole campaign, in exchange for preserving the ability to search the narrow debris zones for survivors.
