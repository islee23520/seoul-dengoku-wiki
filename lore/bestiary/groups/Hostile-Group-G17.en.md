## Common Forms and Variants

The Surveillance-Orbit Swarm was an ecology of heads on the Yeouido waterfront masts and the Records Bureau rooftop of Seoul Station, short transfer rails, and transmission swarms turning an old threshold table. It was no omnipotent surveillance net tracking people, and it could not merge signals from inside buildings or beyond the mast rows. It mistook cooling-tower steam for fire, metal beds for armed movement, and high-tide railings for intrusion.

The mast rows onto which the threshold sheet was reloaded were the base, and from the Amber-Red Threshold Warden to the West-Sea Salt-Haze Watch Chief the individuals were machine and facility subtypes split by function like thermal domes, tide staffs, flag masts, and rooftop rail nets. The Total-Red Orbit Ring was no biological king but a battlefield strongpoint where sluices and rooftop orbits were bound by a circular relay. No new satellite could be launched, and numbers were kept only by putting abandoned heads onto nearby rails.

## Units and Battle Formation

F01 the Cooling-Tower Keeper erased steam records but could not lower a flag, and only the HP03 waterfront duty comparing the medical-transfer roster before the heads brought red down to amber. The senses were low-resolution thermal imaging, metal reflection, near-range optics, wireless bursts, and column vibration alone — it identified no faces or affiliations, and erased video when the shift ended. Masts that hit the accumulator floor or dome fouling froze at amber and fell from the transmission row.

The player gave units movement, guard, engagement, withdrawal, and specialist work orders. The counters below were the acts of units executing those orders, not a skill list for directly controlling heroes. The Yeouido waterfront masts seized the metal railings of a medical bed in the fog as an intrusion target.

When the red flag rose, the transfer road beside the Water Compact's sluice closed, and the Seoul Station Records Bureau rooftop heads, receiving the same threshold sheet, locked the corridor as far as central Seoul Station.

When XT02 sea noise strengthened, the southwest radius widened, but it could not merge signals from inside buildings or beyond the mast rows.

Low-resolution thermal imaging, metal reflection, near-range optics, wireless bursts, and column vibration were the whole of its senses.

It mistook cooling-tower steam for fire, metal beds for armed movement, railings shaking at high tide for intrusion.

It identified no faces or affiliations, and it erased video when the alarm shift ended.

F01 the Cooling-Tower Keeper recorded steam times and temperatures, erasing false alarms, but held no authority to lower a flag.

Only the HP03 waterfront duty comparing the medical-transfer roster before the heads brought red down to amber.

Energy came from mast solar trickle, rooftop accumulators, and a small share of sluice distribution.

When gimbal bearings, optical domes, band filters, and flag actuators wore, a head watched the same direction for long.

Masts that hit the accumulator floor or dome fouling froze at amber and fell from the transmission row.

It could launch no new satellite or connect heads city-wide, and it kept numbers only by putting abandoned heads onto nearby rails.

Emergence happened when the threshold sheet was reloaded onto the masts, and spread when neighboring heads received the same alarm sheet.

Fouling and accumulator exhaustion made stagnation, and when the whitelist was disclosed it became a negotiable state.

Dissolution was fixing the red-flag rails and sending the false-alarm heads to amber waiting.

Coexistence held only if the alarm channel was separated while leaving the water-level gauging channel, and it was tested again whether a real alarm rang at the next high tide.
