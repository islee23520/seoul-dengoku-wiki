## Common Forms and Variants

The dead siding of the Gangnam Line's Changdong depot hummed first, and a few seconds later a metal knee slowly unfolded from deep inside the maintenance shed. Even after the dust settled, the bipedal heavy walker did not walk straight off. It pressed the floor with the pressure grid of its soles, lowered the counterweight behind its body, and proclaimed its work radius with three low-frequency roars.

The Fossil-Roar Swarm was not a living ancient organism but a lineage of pre-collapse work machines. The G26E identifiers marked machine types and derivatives split from existing chassis forms, and meant neither biological breeding nor natural evolution.

| Origin | The Fossil-Roar Swarm was a biomimetic series that applied the load distribution and posture control of ancient reptiles to pre-collapse heavy-equipment platforms. / The bipedal heavy walkers carried reinforcement over high debris, the low broad triangular shield machines hauled heavy plant or screened workers from falling material, and the long-winged fixed-wing watchers marked ground and passage widths from the air. / On the disaster's last day, when the Changdong depot siding shed of the Gangnam Line and the protective hangar of the Gwangjin Line's Godeok base closed, the fully equipped units were sealed in work-standby posture. / Long after, as the Gangnam Line's brake-test power and the Gwangjin Line base's automatic inspection terminal revived at different times, the machines resumed the debris-removal process that had never ended. / The original design drawings and approval systems were mostly unreadable, and the behavior that remained was partial routines — axle-load tables, tow priorities, safety radii. / The Seoul canon saw them not as war machines built by present powers but as the heavy-work arm of a lost-technology lineage. / The roar-like low-frequency alarm and the charge were not a will to conquer but the fossilized procedure of securing a work zone. / The account that the HC17 siding recovery team and the F10 load reader recorded the first reactivation was likewise confined to fictional events. |
| Habitat · movement | The Fossil-Roar Swarm's centers were the Changdong depot siding of the Gangnam Line and the broad maintenance road and protective hangar of the Gwangjin Line's Godeok base. / The bipedal walkers moved only along corridors where rail spacing and floor support were confirmed, and the triangular tractors favored stockyards and bridge approaches with turning space. / The long-winged watchers rose only when a short run or a tow catapult was secured, sweeping the debris lines and hot spots between the two strongholds. / When heavy mineral hoppers or winter fuel came in from the XT04 Duman River Far East Theater, axle loads similar to the old material-receiving sheets were sensed, the Gangnam Line siding's activity grew, and the Gwangjin Line tried to annex the eastern bridges and the Godeok base access road as new work zones. / In the thaw it avoided sidings with weak ground, and in hard frost the hydraulic viscosity rose and shortened its movement radius. / Large machines could not sustain entry into narrow unpaved alleys, unverified underground floors, or dense districts beyond the watcher's line of sight. |
| Diet · energy | The main energy sources were traction batteries in the maintenance shed and power returned by regenerative braking, with limited liquid-fuel auxiliary generation. / The bipedal walkers needed hydraulic oil, knee bearings, and sole buffer plates; the triangular tractors cables, winch gears, and shield-plate fastening pins; the long-winged watchers lightweight wing material and optical modules. / Coolant and hydraulic oil could not substitute for each other, and in winter preheating drew more power than normal. / HC17 wanted this equipment's traction to open heavy convoy routes but consumed the same rail steel and fuel drums as repair resources. / No complete power core could be made anew, and combining two damaged units raised no power grade. / A work detachment kept the hydraulic pressure and braking power needed to return, and below the floor the heaviest bipedals fixed their knees first and waited. / The watchers could fly only short reconnaissance on one battery and could not keep continuous aerial watch. / So this swarm's scale was limited not by fuel but by floor area that intact joints and tow-line loads could bear. |
| Life · maintenance | The operating life ran in five phases: emergence, spread, stagnation, negotiable, dissolution. / In emergence the hydraulics of one hangar bay filled and the watcher swept the siding once. / In spread the triangular tractors dragged old obstacles off the work line and the bipedal walkers drove new load marks to widen the zone. / Stagnation was the state where, from lack of ground support, hydraulic leakage, tow-line wear, or mismatched aerial maps, the detachment pushed the same debris and pulled it back. / In the negotiable phase, when people presented actual load tables, permitted work boundaries, and lists of reinforcement to return, part of the heavy work could be entrusted. / Dissolution proceeded in the order of cooling the power core, fastening the knee pins, separating the tow lines, and recovering the watcher. / There was no production plant of its own, and without the shed crane and standard parts even keeping up numbers was hard. / Work records were separated per detachment, and one base's stop order did not apply to another base by itself. |
| Senses | The bipedal walkers read the sole pressure grid, joint torque, low-frequency structural vibration, and near heat sources. / The triangular tractors calculated towability from winch tension, floor slope, and short-range measurement ahead of the body. / The long-winged watchers used visible and thermal imagery and ground markers, but in smoke, glare, and high debris they mistook altitude and scale. / They could not directly know buildings' uses, people's affiliations, hopper contents, or underground pipe states. / A stopped armored vehicle could be misread as a bundle of reinforcement, a refugee tent as a falling-object screen, crowd vibration as unstable debris. / When F10's load model and the machine's verdict disagreed, an HC17 structural engineer set a test weight, confirmed the actual sag, and issued the movement order. / If a watcher was downed or failed to return in weather, the ground detachment's annexation of new zones stopped too. |
| Group behavior | The detachment moved not through a central commander called a roar-core but through three functional layers sharing a work-permission table. / The long-winged watcher drew the work polygon, the triangular tractors made the entry and retreat lines, and the bipedal heavy walkers entered last and performed the heavy work. / In each detachment the bipedal with the most stable joints served as the load datum, but it held no command authority to punish or sacrifice other machines. / When two triangular machines chose different tow points, they released the winches, sounded the alarm, and stopped. / Then F10 compared the tension values, and the HC17 human supervisor had to physically re-drive the datum point for work to continue. / If the datum fell, the detachment did not elect a larger machine but withdrew beyond the last safety line. / When the watcher's map and the ground pressure values clashed, the ground test load won. |

### G26E01 · Load-Jaw Strider

- Source batch: M041

| Codex item | Record |

| Common/variant and command scope | The Load-Jaw Strider was a machine chassis type differentiated from the G26 common ecology. The reconnaissance · pathfinding role applied only at the independent-unit scope, and stood in for neither the whole swarm's ecological order nor human command. |

- Role · body: A bipedal heavy vanguard whose two thick legs and rear counterweight formed a dinosaur silhouette. Its jaw-shaped load gripper lifted debris bit by bit to open the gap where the following tow equipment would attach.
- Origin: A pre-collapse structural-repair pathfinder tied up in the Gangnam Line depot shed. When the inspection power returned, it resumed from the support inspection interrupted at the collapse.
- Habitat · needs: It chose its footing along vehicle undercarriage work lines and sunken concrete edges. High-output battery bundles were finite, and without knee hydraulic seals and sole load pads it could not stand.
- Life · maintenance: Before departure it hung its tail counterweight on the maintenance support and measured knee leakage. Joints past the datum locked, so an old body gave up moving off the work line rather than drag a foot.
- Behavior · hierarchy: It handed only floor cells with verified support to the following tow machines. Missing a hollow under a thin work plate, a footing it judged safe could sink with people aboard.
- Escalation: In stage one test foot-stamps repeated; in stage two temporary footings lifted. In stage three a narrow work line begun with a load check split the evacuation column between the great legs and the counterweight.
- Engagement · counter: Set the gripper on the base's independent load tester, then bind the knees with maintenance clamps. Blowing an ankle sent the falling counterweight into the rear structure crew, so toppling it was no victory in itself.
- Negotiation · moral choice: Given a support table verified by people and a charging share, it could help lift debris. The power spent on one heavy job was also demanded by patient transport carts, so which structure to perform first stayed in the ledger.

- Origin: Three-point fastening equipment once used to carry reinforcement was released from long sealing on the Gwangjin Line base siding. The destination building was gone, but the transport-completion contact was never entered.
- Habitat · needs: It stayed in siding loading yards with room for the neck shield to rotate. It had to actually load wire drums, tow pins, and gear oil, and the heavier the load the more often it returned to the charging support.

- Role · body: A second-elite watcher with rangefinders under long pterosaur wings. The wings served both as lift surfaces and surveying datum frames, and after a brief hover it hung on the high maintenance stand to tell the ground heavies the crack positions.
- Origin: A pre-collapse aerial survey series that inspected the Gangnam Line roof trusses and the Gwangjin Line siding reinforcements survived. Receiving the sealed stand's access authorization, it resurveyed against the outline of a roof already demolished.
- Habitat · needs: The depot's upper reaches, where wind passed and both wings could spread, were its working face. It consumed propulsion power, wing tension lines, and clean rangefinder windows, and could not fly into narrow underground conduits.

- Behavior · hierarchy: It marked dangerous load zones rather than ordering tow direction. Mistaking rain reflection or a hanging tarp for a crack, it could have the heavies empty the work under a sound roof.
- Escalation: Survey light marked in the wrong place announced stage one. In stage two repair turns went to empty zones, and when stage-three danger marks overlapped, the whole depot crossing road was treated as a load-ban zone.

- Negotiation · moral choice: Survey commissions per section were traded for wing repair materials. Adopting new datums let the old hazard map be discarded, but residents' hidden roof passage routes were exposed too, and protecting their escape routes became a separate agenda.

## Habitat Record

The dead siding of the Gangnam Line's Changdong depot hummed first, and a few seconds later a metal knee slowly unfolded from deep inside the maintenance shed.

Even after the dust settled, the bipedal heavy walker did not walk straight off.

It pressed the floor with the pressure grid of its soles, lowered the counterweight behind its body, and proclaimed its work radius with three low-frequency roars.

Before it, the low broad triangular tractor set its shield plates aslant, and the long-winged fixed-wing watcher tied to the ceiling catapult prepared a short run.

What field people called the fossil roar was not a predator's cry but the work alarm of heavy equipment that still believed its task unfinished.

Before the collapse this series was a heavy-movement platform modeled on the posture and skeleton of ancient reptiles.

The bipedal walkers crossed debris, the triangular machines took tow and falling-object screening, and the long-winged machines drew safe work polygons from above.

Fully equipped, they slept in the Gangnam Line's Changdong depot siding and the Gwangjin Line's Godeok base, and generations later reactivated — but what later people could read was only part of the axle-load tables and boundary signals.

The design intent explaining why some walls were reinforced and some marked for demolition was gone.

The present behavior was not an autonomous war doctrine but routines of the pre-seal work order hardened, and for this incomplete decipherability they were classed a lost-technology lineage.

The Fossil-Roar Swarm was large but not omnipotent.

The bipedals exerted force only on floors with verified load; the triangular machines could not change shield angle without turning width.

The long-winged machines were weak against glare and smoke and had to return to the tow catapult after short flights.

Without an aerial map, the ground detachments annexed no new zone.

Sole pressure and joint torque read structural vibration well but could not tell people inside tents from empty netting, and could mistake a stopped vehicle for reinforcement to move.

F10's load models did not see old underground pipes either, so HC17 engineers had to set test weights and confirm the actual sag.

Hydraulic oil and intact joints were this ecology's food.

Shed traction batteries and regenerative-braking power raised the body, but winter preheating alone cut working hours sharply.

With one knee bearing ground down, a bipedal lost its datum standing altogether; with tow cable worn, a triangular machine remained only as a shield.

Combining two stopped units could revive one, but no stronger power core could be made.

When HC17 provided rail steel and fuel, heavy convoy routes opened, but the same resources were needed for citizen trains and the Gwangjin Line's water-supply plant.

Preserving the detachments was not gaining great power but paying hydraulic oil and floor-verification costs at every job.

Life began with hydraulic charging and one aerial survey.

The watcher drew the polygon, the triangular tractors made the retreat, and the bipedals entered last, and the activity zone spread.

When the map and pressure values slipped, the detachment pushed the same debris and dragged it back, stagnating.

At that moment, presenting the actual load table and boundary stakes could lower it to a negotiable work swarm.

Dissolution ran not by explosion but in the order of watcher recovery, tow-line separation, knee fixing, power-core cooling.

Keeping this order left room to leave shield plates and tow capacity with citizens' infrastructure.

### G26-SC1 · The Third Foot-Stamp of the Siding

When the datum machine waking in the Gangnam Line shed stamped its foot a third time, the hollow under the old siding shook.

HC17 wanted the machines at once for northern hopper towing, but F10's map showed abnormal sag beneath only one foot.

Field staff could set test weights in many positions to guide the datum machine onto safe rail, lay load-spreading plates to fix its knees in place, or order a short tow accepting the risk.

Choosing inspection delayed the XT04 fuel hoppers; hasty operation could sink the siding and the sealed bay together.

### G26-SC2 · The Pipe the Shield Dragged Out

Clearing collapse barriers at the Gwangjin Line's Godeok base, the triangular tractors judged a water-pipe guard as scrap reinforcement.

The eastern convoy route widened, but vibration grew on the pipe behind the shield, and the bipedal tried to enter within the old work boundary the watcher had drawn.

Field staff could set new boundary stakes to split the tow of the barrier only, stop the convoy a day to build the pipe support first, or open the road at once accepting water loss.

HC17's supply promise and the Gwangjin Line's water hung on both ends of the same tow line, and either delay passed its cost to another state.

### G26-SC3 · The Cooling Core Inside the Roar Wall

With the long-winged watcher unable to return in the snowstorm, the Gangnam Line siding and the Gwangjin Line base detachments held different safety polygons.

The triangular machines closed the entrance with a continuous shield wall, and the bipedal datum machine braced behind, guarding its overheated power core.

Field staff had to recover the watcher with landing marks to match the maps, hang no-load pulleys on each shield to bleed tension in order, or connect external cooling lines and then fix the datum machine's knees.

Blasting the core opened the passage fastest but shocked the fuel and water plants too.

The ending that kept the detachments demanded public load tests and joint supervision for every heavy job to come; the ending that dismantled them gave parts at once but left the next great mass of debris for human hands.
