## Common Forms and Variants

A raccoon family group moving between the conduit joining the Ttukseom coalition's pump station, the dry shelter beneath the workshops, and the Mapo coalition's cable gallery. By day it hid below manholes; by night it circled the alleys about fifty meters from the exits, feeding on aquatic prey, garbage, carcasses, and microbial film. The elder female held priority only in kit movement and narrow passages, and there was no combat command.

From conduit patrol to the conduit remnant frame these were ordinary raccoons adapted to urban streams. Rising to the high ventilation rooms before the pumps was family learning that shared flood memory, not a superpower, and the dry shelter and cable trays were animal-filled hiding places, not machine strongholds. This deployment had no mutant individuals, and the absent E13 through E16 belonged to an excluded deployment.

### Role · body

The Dry-Den Elder Female was the Sewer-Raccoon Pack's named controller, holding priority only in kit movement and narrow passage points, not combat command.

Her whiskers were long, conduit rust was worked into her soles, and she read the pressure change before pump starts with her body, climbing first to the high ventilation room.

### Origin

She was the elder female of the family that settled in the dry shelter beside the Ttukseom coalition pump after the Jungnang and Tan trunk inspections ended.

The rule of knowing the water level before the pumps was fiction, and no real local-government accident was attributed.

### Habitat

The core was the dry den of the pump-station conduit and the workshop shelter beneath.

When flood level rose she moved her kits to the Mapo coalition's transmission gallery, and in the XT04 cold snap she shared the only dry approach with other families.

### Needs

Aquatic prey, organic garbage, and carcass remains were her energy; low flow and dry shelter were the upkeep.

When HC04 skipped dredging the feed film thickened and density rose; over-dredging and she pushed her kits out to the ground market.

### Life · maintenance

In the dry season each family kept its own shelter, and before rainfalls she moved to the ventilation room on the leading pressure.

When shelters flooded in succession she gave up time division and dispersed into the alleys.

Priority was not hereditary bloodline but survived experience.

### Behavior · hierarchy

She gave no combat orders to other families.

Even when H04's robot summed the heat sources into a single colony, the HC04 drainage watch split them by sleeping places and entry times, and the human eye was final.

### Escalation

In stage one the pre-rain move to the ventilation room became an unofficial alarm.

In stage two droppings and microbial film masked the water-level sensor and collided with inspection robots, and in stage three, in cold snap and flood, pump maintenance and refuge competed in a single dry conduit.

### Engagement · counter

Poison feed and total submergence harmed the downstream and workers together through carcass decay.

Partial flow control, an alternate shelter conduit, sensor washing, and night ground-passage limits were the basics, and success meant pump recovery time, sensor reliability, and zero ground casualties.

### Negotiation · moral choice

Adopting raccoon movement as a preliminary alarm bought preparation time but left the responsibility for false alarms; waiting for HP01 the Sluice House's measurement could miss sudden inflow.

Preheating the pumps only when the two signals disagreed saved fuel but missed short localized downpours.

Whether to entrust public safety to an unofficial ecological signal was this controller's moral cost.

### Scenario links

She made the central movement of G04-SC1 'The Leading Indicator Beneath the Manhole,' held one family's priority in the time division overlapping the dredging window in G04-SC2, and in the G04-SC3 cold snap forced one of heating shelter conduit, inspection delay, or external staging.

### Role · body

The Conduit-Patrol Raccoon was the pack's common ecology form, hiding in the conduit beneath manholes by day and circling the alleys within about fifty meters of the exits by night.

It read current and the air flow of gaps with its whiskers, separated pump vibration from boot sounds by hearing, and its body was wet fur and narrow shoulders, not combat claws.

### Origin

An ordinary adult of the family that entered the rain trunk people had vacated, following aquatic prey, garbage, and carcasses.

On the basis of urban stream sighting records, it observed an ecological order affecting conduit operation in 2126.

### Habitat

The Ttukseom coalition's low-flow conduit was its base, and when the level rose it dispersed briefly toward the Mapo coalition's ventilation shafts.

When cold snaps changed feed density it moved to the eastern branch and returned to familiar conduits after the thaw.

### Needs

Microbial film, organic garbage, and carcass remains were its food; without dry shelter its ground stays lengthened.

When dredging stripped the feed film all at once, these individuals were the first pushed out into the market alleys.

### Life · maintenance

In the dry season it circled only its own family's feeding ground, and after floods it shared the same trunk line in divided time slots.

A small build favorable to narrow conduits could persist across generations, but ground crossing and cold snaps grew more dangerous.

A shelter change of a few days was learning, not genetics.

### Behavior · hierarchy

It honored the elder female's priority only at passage points and otherwise followed its own timetable.

Because robot logs confused it with floating debris, people did not count it in colony numbers before confirming.

### Escalation

In stage one it followed the climb to the high ventilation room before rain; in stage two it met inspection robots in the sensor-masked conduit; in stage three, with one dry conduit left, it overlapped the pump approach.

### Engagement · counter

Driving it out of the alleys only shortened the fifty-meter circuit to residential doorsteps.

Dividing night-passage windows, opening an alternate shelter conduit, and washing the sensors were the response; culling worsened the water quality.

### Negotiation · moral choice

Dividing the fifty-meter alley into no-passage windows inconvenienced people and kept the raccoons in the conduit.

Ground expulsion cut immediate complaints but increased residential intrusion.

Poisoning out of disease fear endangered conduit workers and downstream drinking water together, and lost the leading-indicator species.

### Scenario links

In G04-SC1 it was the ordinary individual following the female up to the ventilation room, making the alarm's density; in G04-SC2, when the dredging window broke, it was the first wave out into the ground market; in G04-SC3 it overlapped the dry passage as one of three family lines.

## Habitat Record

The Sewer-Raccoon Pack was a raccoon family group moving between the Ttukseom coalition's pump-station conduit, the dry shelter beneath the workshops, and the Mapo coalition's underground cable gallery.

After the collapse, as the Jungnang, Tan, and internal rain trunk inspections ended, individuals following aquatic prey, garbage, and carcass remains settled in the conduits people had vacated.

By day they hid beneath manholes; by night they circled within about fifty meters of the exits.

The sight of wet fur rising from storm drains and covered channels stirred the anxiety that sewers were flowing back into the living sphere, but seeing them as one underground army was a mistake.

The part touching reality was only that wild animals could appear around urban streams and facilities.

Sensing the water level before the pumps — an order affecting conduit operation — was fiction set in this region and explained no real institution's or company's accident.

The core habitat was the low-flow conduit beside the Ttukseom coalition pump and the dry shelter.

When rain continued and the level rose, the families moved kits to the high ventilation rooms and then dispersed to the Mapo coalition's transmission gallery and vents.

When cold snap and logistics severance from the XT04 Duman River Far East Theater changed feed and waste distribution, they moved briefly to the eastern branches and returned to familiar conduits after the thaw.

Aquatic prey, organic garbage, carcasses, and microbial film became food, and dry shelter and gentle current lowered survival cost.

When HC04 the Ttukseom Heat-Source House put off sediment dredging too long the feed film thickened and density rose; scraped away all at once, the families were pushed out into the ground market.

Droppings and microbial film masked water-quality sensors and slowed even pump judgment, so the matter could not be reduced to plenty of feed.

The Sewer-Raccoon Pack had no fixed commander.

In dry season each family used its own feeding ground and dry shelter, and when several families used the same trunk they divided time slots.

The elder female held priority only in kit movement and narrow passage points and gave no combat orders to other families.

After floods thickened the feed film, different families used one conduit in shifts, and when shelters flooded in succession they gave up the sharing rule and scattered into the ground alleys.

Even when H04 the River-Warden's conduit robot summed many heat sources and movements into a single colony, the HC04 drainage watch had to divide again by sleeping place, kit count, and entry times.

Treating the families as one mass did not make movement routes easier to predict — it missed different refuge directions at once.

They read the current and the air flow of narrow gaps with their whiskers and sensed pre-pump water-level rise through pressure change in feet and body.

Smell divided feed, disinfectant, and decayed carcasses; hearing separated pump vibration from human boots.

There were cases of moving higher before the official sensors, but not always accurately.

It could be the result of a family sharing past flood experience and learning a safe dredging timetable.

Over long generations a small build favorable to narrow conduits and strong nocturnality could remain, but such adaptation lowered ground-crossing ability and cold resistance.

Behavior changing shelter within days had to be distinguished as family learning, not genetic change.

H04's logs confused raccoons, floating debris, and crouched people, so human visual confirmation was needed for final judgment.

Conflict began at the pressure leading signal and grew through sensor mismatch and shelter contention.

In the first stage, before rain, the families moved to the high ventilation rooms and served as an unofficial alarm.

In the second stage droppings and microbial film made the official level gauges read low, and inspection robots met the families in narrow conduits.

In the last stage flood or cold snap left one dry conduit, and pump maintenance crews and several families demanded the same space.

Poison feed and total submergence looked like quick solutions but worsened water quality through carcass decay and endangered the downstream and workers together.

Partial flow control, sensor washing, alternate shelter conduits, and restricted night ground passage came first, and success was measured not by removal count but by pump-operation recovery time, sensor reliability, and zero ground casualties.

G04-SC1 'The Leading Indicator Beneath the Manhole' began as the families moved kits to the high ventilation room from daytime though rain had not come.

The Ttukseom coalition and HC04 could adopt the move at once as a preliminary alarm, wait until HP01 the Sluice House's field measurement ended, or preheat the pumps only when the ecological signal and the measured value disagreed.

The first choice bought preparation time but left responsibility when fuel and manpower were wasted on false alarms.

The second was clear in grounds but could be late for short, sudden inflows.

The third saved ordinary fuel but risked missing the localized downpour where both signals ran late.

Raccoon behavior could be useful auxiliary material but must never become grounds for punishment or culling.

Whether to entrust part of public safety to an unofficial ecological signal was the burden of this choice.

In G04-SC2 'The Dredging Window's Two Families,' the night dredging window HC04 took overlapped the shift-shelter times of two families.

Putting equipment in as planned brought both families out into the Ttukseom and Mapo coalitions' ground markets at once, and complaints rose on both the cable and drainage sides.

Dividing dredging into two sections preserved each family's retreat but delayed completion.

Opening the empty dry conduit as an alternate shelter reduced ground emergence but gave up the Mapo coalition's spare communication-cable space.

Buying out one family's use window with feed and a safe route made concentrated dredging fastest, but it unfairly changed the time-division rule other families had kept.

Judging only by efficiency the last choice could win, but a sharing rule broken once returned as a larger simultaneous emergence at the next rainfall.

G04-SC3 'The Pump Room's Dry Passage' was the stage where, in the XT04 cold snap, three families overlapped in occupying the only unfrozen pump approach.

Providing a heated alternate shelter conduit emptied and dispersed the inspection route, but the power ration shrank and the Mapo coalition's other facilities had to endure the cold.

Postponing pump inspection avoided the immediate collision but grew flood risk at thaw or sudden rain.

Installing temporary external staging allowed maintenance without emptying the conduit, but the work crew bore the cold-snap labor and fall risk.

HC04 coordinated the dredging hours, the ground-passage prohibition hours, and the alternate conduit's heating on one ledger.

At the last sluice inspection before overflow, the Ttukseom coalition slowed the upstream inflow and, instead of scraping the channel sediment, accepted a plan preserving the continuity of the dry passages and feeding grounds the Sewer-Raccoon Pack had used.

The Mapo coalition closed the hidden discharge outlet and opened its water-quality records to the joint ledger, and XT04 linked the alarm net and the detour channel so the refuge routes would not lock even at sudden level rise.

HC04 took charge of treating injured individuals and moving young ones while halting indiscriminate capture, and H04 bound the night patrol and residents' delegates to cut dumping and feed conflict around the passages.

Thus the central collection plant changed from a wall dividing the human and habitat zones into a buffer dividing the times water and waste moved.

Each group did not stop at not intruding on the other's zone; they alternated inspection, cleaning, and rescue duties in set windows, and when damage occurred they investigated the cause jointly first and then shared the burden by inflow contribution and recovery capacity.

After the first rain passed and the channels held and the backflow stopped, the old expulsion order was withdrawn.

The Sewer-Raccoon Pack was recognized as a lawful underground resident, and the five parties accepted one another not as surveillance targets but as neighbors of the same watershed.

So the accord would not remain a one-time truce, the later management hardened not into seasonal co-stewardship but into a joint care system.

The Ttukseom coalition and XT04 measured level, flow, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, and ammoniacal nitrogen weekly; the Mapo coalition recorded monthly illegal-discharge traces and floating-waste weight; HC04 and H04 together published quarterly headcounts, kit survival rates, skin and respiratory anomaly ratios, passage-use counts, resident reports, and response times.

The standards were set at monsoon backflow once a month or less, main-passage operation above ninety-five percent, injured-individual ratio reduced year over year, and initial response to emergency reports within thirty minutes.

Out of the standard two consecutive times, the alarm net automatically rose a stage, and temporary embankment installation, contamination-source blocking, vulnerable-individual movement, and independent re-sampling ran in order before limited closure of the cause section only.

If data were lost or a responsible party left, records were restored through paper ledgers and distributed observers, and part of the next season's management right was entrusted to a joint committee to recover trust.

Unexpected changes also appeared.

As stench and garbage shrank, the night-insect distribution changed; bats and birds chasing them increased, the food web around the drains widened, and late-night resident activity grew on the safer walkways.

Since ecological recovery could thus return as new noise and lighting pressure, the indicators included illumination ceilings, low-noise time bands, and seasonal detour sections, and the range of coexistence kept being revised.
