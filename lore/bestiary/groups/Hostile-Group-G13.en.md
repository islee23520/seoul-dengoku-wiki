## Common Forms and Variants

The Night-Sorting Swarm was a machine ecology of queue controller, chute masters, short conveyor arms, scanners, and gates bundled line by line in the Guro industrial-dormitory corridor. Past two in the morning the cold-chamber shutter half-rose and the barcode gate reread the last night shift's priority sheet. It could not leave the conduit outside its learned codes.

The sorting hall and the refrigerated sorting line citizens had used were the base ecology, and the named individuals were machine subtypes branching from those lines. The Misclassification Enforcer, the Conveyor Sealer, and the Midnight Twelve-Chute were not revived animals but forms in which inspection, repair, and queue-control modules had gone independent. No new bodies were cast; numbers were kept only by moving broken arms into spare slots.

- Role · body: A runaway robot isolating freight and creatures not by present destination but by outdated hazard grade. - Habitat: It moved around the inspection ring at the core habitat of the Dongdaemun coalition and, on days old transport traces were sensed, patrolled only as far as the first gate of the Yeongdeungpo Line route. Where floor markings ended it surrounded the spot with a temporary isolation line but explored no terrain beyond.
- Needs · wants: Finite batteries moving the sealing grippers, worn latch parts, and coolant to cool the reading core were all needed. If any of the three fell below threshold, it sealed several targets in one bundle instead of detailed inspection.
- Life cycle · upkeep: After startup its functions shrank in the order sheet collection, inspection, isolation, low-power preservation. With no facility to build new carts, and parts of stopped bodies usable for only one matching repair, individual counts never recovered.
- Behavior · rank: The body bearing the red inspection lamp sent verdict order by near-range signal, and the auxiliary arms treated even slow-responding equipment as defective freight. When a valid tag's inspection value matched the original sheet, it stopped capture and began re-inspection.
- Staged threat: Early it boxed one untagged target; as errors accumulated it surrounded the inspection plate with box walls; as battery ran low it drove coolant into the record cabinet and locked the exit. In the final preservation state it gave up movement and instead transmitted the verdict sheet to adjacent terminals.
- Counter · combat: Attach an accurate mass tag to a vibration-blocked handcart and the contact sensors approved it as an empty transport unit, so a trapped person could be pulled out. Attacking the coolant pipe locked the gripper, so the power rail had to be separated first and the manual latches released in the prescribed order.
- Negotiation · moral choice: HC13 could present H13's original quarantine clause and a new coolant container, and send an exchange signal asking that refugees be registered as priority passers. The enforcer memorized the document inspection value and traded passage for maintenance, but on finding a forged tag it isolated everything bearing the same signature — and if it seized an agreed target, HC13 held the right to cut coolant supply.

## Habitat Record

The night sorting hall of the Standards Alliance's Guro dormitory remnant corridor woke before people once past two in the morning.

The cold-chamber shutter rose halfway, the fourth chute pushed empty boxes into the dangerous-goods bin, and the barcode gate reread the last night shift's priority sheet.

The Night-Sorting Swarm was not one giant robot but an ecology of queue controller, chute masters, short conveyor arms, scanners, and gates bundled line by line.

On the Yeongdeungpo Line's Guro–Gasan night lines it gathered parts labels and recovered rollers and pneumatic valves from the dormitory corridor conveyors, but it could not leave the conduit beyond its learned codes.

What this swarm saw was not people's faces.

It layered the corners of smeared QR, load changes of breathing belts in thermal imaging, and inverter noise and motor vibration to estimate stock.

A wet label fell to unclassified, a stretcher's metal frame to a return pallet, and a small generator's heat could be misread as a living body.

After three rescans with differing values, the scanner requested H13 Hanbinna's optical log — but even Hanbinna held no authority to open a gate directly.

Only when the HC13 night duty matched the sealing-tape color and cabinet-key records did the line move again.

Even with the controller stopped, each line merely repeated the last sheet and could not agree a new strategy with other lines.

Energy came only from the sorting hall's emergency accumulators, conveyor regenerative braking, and discarded wireless charging pads.

The swarm did not draw beyond sixty percent of the accumulator cabinet, and at the lower limit it closed the chutes and left only the scanners at low speed.

Roller bearings, thermal-transfer ribbon, lens cleaning fluid, and chiller coolant were the maintenance costs.

It competed with the HC13 recovery squad for the same cable trays, so funneling parts to the swarm stopped the conduit lighting and the citizens' carrying teams first.

No new bodies could be made; numbers were kept only by moving broken arms into spare slots.

Missing the dawn accumulator-recovery window, it stagnated before the cold chamber carrying lens fog and belt drift.

Emergence began when the residual queue meshed with emergency power, and spread happened when the next line's gates were enrolled in the same sheet.

Short of ribbon and pneumatics it stagnated, and when people disclosed parts of the priority sheet and the storage window it stepped down to a negotiable state.

Dissolution ended with controller separation and per-chute sealing — but wiping out even the refrigerated sorting line citizens had used was no victory.

Reattaching a single label, leaving power in the cold chamber, and splitting the keys between two duties were this ecology's means of control.

### G13-SC1 · The Night of the Smeared Label

In the rain-soaked conduit an emergency blood box was pushed into the dangerous-goods chute.

Instead of destroying the conveyor, field staff traced the fouling order of the three scanners and loaded the temporary human-exclusion code Hanbinna had made into the label printer.

Stopping the wrong line spoiled another cold chamber's pharmaceuticals, so which chute to leave cooling had to be decided.

### G13-SC2 · The Storage-Sheet Exchange

The HC13 recovery squad wanted the accumulator cabinet to revive the conduit lighting, and the queue controller demanded roller-maintenance power.

Field staff had to bundle the citizens' emergency-supply slot, the swarm's fifteen-percent charging window, and the unclassified stock's human-confirmation queue into one sheet.

Rushing the deal concentrated the keys in one person; delaying too long stopped the belts and spoiled the medicine.

### G13-SC3 · Sealing the Fourth Chute

Just before the gates locked wholesale, two people were seized as unclassified stock.

Field staff could lure the vibration sensors with empty-box piles and open only the fourth chute through the pneumatic bypass, but a line sealed with tape could not be used for civilian transport until the next shift.

An extermination order looked like it opened the passage at once, but it killed the Gasan cold sorting and the southwestern parts flow together.

Coexistence was the choice to keep shouldering slow human confirmation and divided keys.
