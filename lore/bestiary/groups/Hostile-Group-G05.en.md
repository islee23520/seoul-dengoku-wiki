## Common Forms and Variants

An ordinary rat colony formed between the lower level and cable trays of the Gwangjin Line's transfer shopping street and the refuge stairs of the Yeouido Line's Sangha Relief State. Crumb carbohydrates and protein remains with heating-pipe warmth made the density, and it spread no special pathogen. There was no fixed boss; when feed traces changed, even a core that looked central moved away.

Gap-Whiskers, the Warm-Core Seat, and the Counter-Core were route and breeding concentrations of the same species, not a king and guards. Tray-Warmth and Closed-Shop Shade were facility sections occupied by rats, and the Unloading-Jam was a phenomenon of many families gathering on the hub floor after protein unloading. The Freezer-Gap was a local branch adapted to elevator drip, and E01 through E06 remained in an excluded deployment.

### Role · body
A trade-type individual, a route pathfinder pushing its whiskers into tray gaps to find air-leaking holes. Temperature and vibration reaching feet and belly separated trains, ration carts, and human steps, but it could not read distant heat points on screens or decode information displays.

### Origin
In a winter when ultrasound and sealing panels repeated in the same order, survivors split as they remembered only the open side. After testing an unused pipe bracket beneath the Gwangjin Line's Escort Protection State shopping street, that hole became a branch between cores.

### Habitat
By day it clung to cable-tray edges and heating-pipe brackets, and on heavily disinfected ground it put its nose out only at night. It brushed past the backs of closed shutters as temporary storage only.

### Needs · economy
The strength holding the holes was pipe warmth, not crumbs. When the heat cooled the whiskers dulled and pathfinding stopped, and when scraps moved to another core the hole too was abandoned within a day.

### Life · maintenance
When its whiskers wore on rough sheathing it lost the air flow and opened a wrong retreat. Exposed long to bright platforms its energy consumption rose and it skipped that season's breeding.

### Behavior · hierarchy
It gave no orders and only continued branches toward cores with many feed traces. When H05 Yunjae raised one heat point to boss status, steam waste heat mixed in and a wrong blockade began.

### Staged threat
In stage one it made test holes in the opposite tray. In stage two, one core blocked, it moved branches beneath the shop shutters. In stage three the feeding station closed first and it opened a back-flowing hole into the refuge-stair cables.

### Combat · counter
Toxic spraying and flame reached sections where children and pets passed, so they stayed last resorts. After confirming occupied sections, opening a one-way retreat toward the side people did not use and blocking holes from the far side back, success was measured not in captures but in short-circuit counts, food contamination, non-target deaths, and refuge passage time.

### Negotiation · moral choice
Closing one unoccupied section long to test a retreat meant not handing passage records to HP09 the Digital Media City Trust House, but that space could not serve as refuge margin. Filling every hole at once sped hygiene measures while pathfinding went down to deeper cable layers and recovery costs grew.

### Scenario links
G05-SC1's one-way retreat test staged on this hole, G05-SC2's sealing-order error pushed the same hole up the stairs, and in G05-SC3's protein-unloading week the branches reached the elevator throat.

### Combat · counter
Putting flame into the warm-core burned sheathing and hurried the short circuit. Opening a one-way retreat, sealing from the far side, and recovering scraps were the basics, and success was judged by insulation resistance, zero food contamination, and passage time.

### Scenario links

### Origin
As heating pipes and power trays ran side by side through the transfer link, the metal strip keeping warmth even in winter became bedding. Each time lighting failed people recalled contagion, but the cause was sheath damage and humidity, not pathogens.

### Habitat
The main trays beneath the Gwangjin Line's transfer shopping street were the center. When refugee density rose, branches ran to the Yeouido Line's refuge-stair cables, and heavily disinfected sections were crossed only at night.

### Needs · economy
What sustained the stronghold was pipe warmth and fine scraps on the trays. Reducing scraps while leaving trays open kept the warmth, and density returned.

### Life · maintenance
A damaged sheath section held humidity and became the next bedding. Only when insulation resistance returned to the baseline and no back-flow traces appeared for three days was this stronghold judged reduced.

### Behavior · hierarchy
Movement volume over the trays only leaned toward cores after feed traces; there was no command. Unless HC05 compared monthly sheath-damage rates with humidity, heat points alone misjudged the stronghold.

### Staged threat
In stage one night crossing traces lengthened along the tray. In stage two, one section blocked, warmth was rerouted to other trays. In stage three humidity spread as far as the stair cables, cutting the information displays' available hours.

### Combat · counter
Flooding the trays to drive them out short-circuited first. Maintaining low temperature and dryness, closing lids from the far side, and considering predatory birds in the open atrium only with collision markings and droppings washing.

### Negotiation · moral choice
Sealing every tray reduced colony movement but made lighting-inspection labor detour and could keep the displays dark longer. Opening lids for inspection only at night eased work, and the pathfinding holes learned to fit those hours.

### Scenario links
G05-SC2's back-flow occurred as this tray stronghold reached the stair cables; G05-SC1's opposite tray shared the same warmth structure, and G05-SC3's hub blackout was the result of sheath damage spreading into the atrium.

## Habitat Record

The G05 Transfer-Rat Colony was an urban animal cluster formed at the transfer link where the Gwangjin Line's Escort Protection State underground ration corridor met the Yeouido Line's Sangha Relief State refuge stairs.

Beneath the transfer shopping street floor fell food crumbs, and between the cable trays and heating pipes warmth remained even in winter.

People thought first of contagion and contamination whenever tails vanished into platform gaps or information displays suddenly went dark, but this colony was no spreader of special pathogens.

It was closer to an ordinary animal cluster whose density rose where feed, warmth, and shelter overlapped.

The dispersed core nest network, the learning of control orders, and the management procedure using one-way retreats were, however, fictional ecological rules valid only in this world, not real quarantine guidance.

So explanations turning the colony's appearance into a particular group's filthiness or refugees' responsibility fit neither fact nor response.

The core habitat was the Gwangjin Line's transfer shopping street lower level and cable trays, but the colony's borders changed with human movement and ration circumstances.

When the Yeouido Line's refugee density rose, branch nests ran into the temporary feeding-station walls and refuge-stair cables, and in weeks when XT05 Incheon Trust Theater's frozen and oceanic protein remains came in bulk, movement traces ran as far as the unloading elevator corridor.

It climbed into often-disinfected bright ground zones only at night and used the backs of closed shutters as temporary storage.

What sustained the colony's economy were the three resources of crumb carbohydrates, protein remains, and heating-pipe warmth.

However much HC05 the Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House sealed the archive and reagent warehouse, if feeding scraps remained and the trays stayed open the total density did not fall.

When droppings and humidity hurried the damage of wire sheathing, the available hours of lighting and information displays fell, and this in turn grew the congestion and anxiety of the refuge corridors.

Two or three breeding cores usually formed in sections where feed and heat met.

Each core connected to branch nests running along the cable trays, and even when one core vanished the whole colony did not at once collapse.

Individuals crowded onto the remaining cores, and previously little-used shutter gaps or pipe brackets became new movement routes.

Lowering density over the long term required sustaining scrap recovery and dryness in every section; removing one visible nest was only a temporary relocation.

When the order of toxic substances, ultrasound, and sealing work repeated, the population found the open retreat faster.

This was not sudden bodily change but learning by surviving individuals and pathfinders dodging the dangerous order.

A tendency to endure low temperature or bright platforms could remain over generations, but at the price of higher energy use and lower breeding rate.

The Transfer-Rat Colony had no fixed boss.

A core with much movement volume merely looked central for a time, and when feed traces changed the center moved too.

Pathfinding individuals scanned air flow and gaps with whiskers and smell, and separated the approach of trains, ration carts, and people by the temperature and vibration reaching feet and body.

They could not precisely classify distant heat sources or read electronic signals.

H05 Yunjae's infrared map compensated for these limits, but one heat point on the screen must not be fixed as boss or nest.

Steam, people, and pipe waste heat could all appear as similar blobs.

H05 had to mark night droppings traces, kit footprints, and per-core movement volume together, and right after one point vanished, trace first the flow spreading into the remaining nests.

The situation worsened in three stages: core search, route rerouting, sealing-order crisis.

Early on, confirming the separated breeding cores was the heart.

Blocking only one crowded individuals beneath the shop shutters and into other trays, beginning the second stage.

Closing the feeding-station side first in the wrong order put the colony into the emergency stairs, and the existing work was in effect a failure.

One had to reconfirm occupied sections, open a one-way retreat from the cores into a human-unused section, and seal in order from the far side.

Blanket toxic spraying and flame stayed last resorts for the exposure of children, pets, and other wildlife.

Scrap recovery and low-temperature dryness were the basic response, and success was judged not by capture count but by electrical short-circuit counts, zero food contamination, zero non-target deaths, and refuge passage time.

The colony could not be spoken with directly, but there was ample room for negotiation among the surrounding human groups.

HC05 the Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House mediated between the Gwangjin and Yeouido lines over scrap-recovery cycles, tray-sealing work order, and night shop-shutter gaps.

In XT05's protein-unloading weeks it supported the temporary double-sealing line first, and keeping ration speed meant shouldering the cost of dividing unloading hours.

Extermination was not the goal.

Reducing feed access and separating movement routes from the refuge corridors was enough for coexistence.

Conversely, raising contagion fear and blockading only the refugees' zone sent the colony deeper into the cable layers and grew recovery costs.

Postponing scrap recovery for fast ration fixed the rerouted state.

Whose passage and labor to delay for safety was this ecological problem's actual moral cost.

G05-SC1 'The Second Core Nest' began when only one large colony appeared on H05's thermal image but kit traces were found on the opposite tray.

The Gwangjin Line and HC05 could survey every core first and confirm non-target creatures and refuge corridors.

This choice was safest but delayed corridor opening.

Buying the old cable heat map from HP09 the Digital Media City Trust House cut survey time but paid with past passage records.

To avoid privacy exposure, a one-way retreat could be tested in one section, but with failure in mind one human-unused space had to stay closed long.

Whichever choice, conflict arose between those wanting immediate hygiene and those preventing mistaken capture.

Ignoring the invisible second core and removing only the first nest moved the colony's center opposite and grew the next stage's cost.

In G05-SC2 'The Sealing Order's Error,' after the feeding-station shutter closed first, the colony back-flowed into the Yeouido Line refuge-stair cables.

S05, the Yeouido Line, HC05, and H05 could halt work and reseal from the far side.

Contamination fell but refuge-corridor work and ration delay lengthened.

Pausing scrap recovery to leave bait and guide the colony back to its original section emptied the passage quickly but deliberately accepted food contamination.

Sending refugees over the ground detour for one night made cable-layer work safe but required heating and guard manpower to protect those vulnerable to cold.

No path made refuge speed, food safety, and non-target harm all best.

Especially shifting danger onto refugees and declaring only work success wrecked the basis of later negotiation.

G05-SC3 'The Open Atrium's Predator' was raised when, after XT05 protein unloading, the colony blocked the transfer hub.

The Gwangjin and Yeouido lines could guide predatory birds in limited fashion into an open atrium with collision markings, droppings washing, and non-target monitoring.

There was a chance of quickly thinning the colony, but bird collisions and new droppings problems needed standing management manpower.

Keeping to staged retreats and sealing without bringing in predation had few ecological side effects but long delayed ration and refuge.

XT05 dispersing the unloading schedule itself reduced feed inflow without making non-target risk, but cold-chain logistics cost and food loss rose.

HC05 and H05 had to publish short circuits, contamination, passage time, and non-target deaths together whatever the choice.

The moment one ecological problem was transferred into another animal's predation, responsibility was not transferred with it; coexistence with the Transfer-Rat Colony came down to deciding who shouldered the invisible cleaning labor and the slowed ration.

Long-term management had to harden not into one-time capture but into S05, the Yeouido Line, and HC05 accumulating changes of feed, heat, and shelter per transfer section in a joint ledger.

The Gwangjin Line measured daily post-closing scrap weight and recovery delay; the Yeouido Line recorded night crossing traces per hundred meters of refuge stairs and the ninety-fifth-percentile passage time; HC05 compared monthly cable sheath-damage rates, insulation resistance, humidity, and non-target animal sightings.

H05's heat points were judged active cores only when matching fresh droppings counts, kit footprints, and per-tray bait loss, and even without shorts or food contamination, movement traces rising two consecutive weeks triggered precautionary inspection.

When sealing failure spread the colony into the refuge corridors, additional blockade stopped, one previous retreat was reopened to lower pressure, the feeding storage line was doubled temporarily, and human detour lines were secured first.

Recovery was completed not by capture volume but when no back-flow traces appeared for three days, no new-core signs for seven nights, and insulation resistance and refuge passage time returned to baseline.

The same workers took the same sections each season to remember even small changes, while unloading, ration, and facility divisions shared the rotating cost so responsibility did not fixate on cleaning staff alone.

Here, sharply thinning the colony could let the fine scraps so far consumed ferment in the drainage grooves and raise insect density, which meant more chemical use and drain washing and ironically a heavier food-zone burden.

So watching scrap putrefaction and insect catch together even after the fall in numbers was part of long-term stability.
