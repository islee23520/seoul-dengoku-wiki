# Entering the Station Interior

Crossing the threshold of a great station unfolded not a simple corridor but a stronghold built of complex stairways and sectors. The facilities inside the concourse and the platforms opened and ran in sequence, by the rules of the survivors.

All 334 named stations within Seoul, the principal treatment plants, the depots, and the markets worked as such three-dimensional strongholds. How the underground facilities and the surface scenery had changed since the change of age decided each stronghold's character.

## Entry and the Panorama

After the encounters and the combat, seizing the initiative in a station was only the beginning. This stronghold, where fates, goods, and reputation crossed, showed its true face only when the door was opened and the party stepped in.

The strongholds recorded in the Seoul station catalog held multi-story structures and convenience-facility sectors of their own. Whether a station was a transfer station or an ordinary one changed the scale of facilities it could seat. The Yeongdeungpo, Ttukseom, and Guui treatment plants, the Sinjeong and Cheonwang depots, Garak Market, and the Noryangjin Fisheries Market stood beside stations, forming vast infrastructure to meet the survivors.

## The Interior Opening Procedure

When the expedition party entered a station, the upper concourse opened by default. The platforms, transfer corridors, and deep tunnels below showed themselves whole only where they stood intact without collapse or flooding, and blocked sectors could be reached only after the restoration conditions were met.

The interior space was no plane but a complicated solid of entwined floors and sectors. Ordinary stations held small-scale facilities and transfer stations held larger ones. Markets, aid stations, repair workshops, generators, water-supply facilities, warehouses, and signal stations could settle in, and where a station had a unique infrastructure at its side, that character overrode the stronghold's identity.

Immediately after occupation, a stronghold's labor, power, and stored goods were unstable, and a seizure taken by force could hardly win the residents' cooperation. The party had to repair what was broken, redeploy labor, and pass the daily settlement day by day to stabilize the stronghold. Beyond the confused state of dispute, through occupation and stabilization, only when authority and cooperation were held for a set span did full integration arrive.

## The Three-Dimensional Layers and Combat

The station interior was a many-layered battlefield. The battlefield was a three-dimensional space that had to be scanned and commanded freely up, down, left, and right, and the commander could at any moment read the situation and tune the units' positions and formations precisely.

A small station house, a tangled transfer station, a terminal where the tracks ended, a collapsed tunnel, surface ruins, a vast depot, a rail bridge crossing the Han — the shape of the battlefield changed with each stronghold's character.

The end of the day closed with the settlement of resource distribution, combat results, and the shifts in authority and cooperation. Reserved goods were laid out, produced resources were consumed, and shortfalls swelled the stronghold's unease.

Each facility devoured labor, power, and raw materials to keep turning. A facility cut from power stopped in its tracks, and a stronghold grown past its reach met the limit of its management.

The expedition's return to the stronghold meant taking up again the interior life that had stood still. The end of a campaign could be a homecoming, a new settlement, a bloody conquest, an endless wandering, or a bond made by trade.
