# River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse

After the collapse, Seoul's buildings were not built anew. The positions and forms of 2026 on the eve of the collapse were the skeleton, and only their use changed on top of it. A past record calling a building a school did not mean it was used as a school now.

## Powers and Buildings

The dong remained the cells that divided area, the minimum unit a power could take was the building, and planting a flag on a dong's name occupied nothing.

Where a dong held a subway or rail station, rationing, transit tolls, shelter, and refuge looked to that station building first. As with the Yeongdeungpo Station unloading floor, the Seoul Station concourse, and the Jegi-dong transfer, the station became the mouth of life, and schools, community centers, and hospitals remained support buildings.

A dong held one of three states.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `held` | One power held it with dominance of 50 or more. |
| `contested` | Two or more powers shared it, or one power's dominance stood below 50. |
| `vacant` | No power's flag stood. The buildings and the station remained. |

## The Three Layers

| Layer | Unit | Work |
|---|---|---|
| River | The Han's north and south, the tributaries | Flooding, embankments, and berths decided whether the first floor was swallowed. |
| Gu | The 25 autonomous districts | Set which trade took the district's surviving buildings. The district was not turned into a state. |
| Dong | The 427 administrative dong | For each observation anchor, recorded the pre-collapse use and the post-collapse application. |

Place names and functions came from the past, the borders divided, and the present use lay over the top.

## The Han River and the Tributaries

The districts set north of the Han were Jongno, Jung-gu, Yongsan, Seongdong, Gwangjin, Dongdaemun, Jungnang, Seongbuk, Gangbuk, Dobong, Nowon, Eunpyeong, Seodaemun, Mapo, and Gangseo; those set south were Yangcheon, Guro, Geumcheon, Yeongdeungpo, Dongjak, Gwanak, Seocho, Gangnam, Songpa, and Gangdong.

The tributaries split the districts. The Jungnang Stream ran through Jungnang, Dongdaemun, and Nowon; the Anyang Stream divided western Yangcheon, Guro, and Yeongdeungpo; the Tancheon divided Gangnam and Songpa; the Cheonggye divided Jongno, Jung-gu, and Seongdong; and the Hongje divided Seodaemun and Eunpyeong. Where a dong's name held Yeouido, Ichon, Banpo, Jamsil, Mangwon, Hapjeong, Amsa, Guui, or Jayang, it was read as main-channel waterfront.

The waterfront rules were read from the layers first. The first floor of the main channel was monsoon storage or abandoned space, habitation pushed above the third floor, and the ration windows sat inside the embankment. The tributary banks were sewage and flood, wild boar and raccoon dog corridors, and school playgrounds served as detour embankments. Inland highlands like Gwanak and Dobong took slopes and blackouts instead of floods, and the schools became evacuation halls.

## The Trades the Districts Took

The spheres of the sixteen states were laid over the districts, but a district's building trades came out of the district's own facilities. The Yeongdeungpo treatment plant and the Sinjeong depot, the Banghwa depot, the Changdong wheel works, the Noryangjin cold storage, and the Jegi-dong medicinal herbs were the first hands each district kept.

| District | River side | Buildings taken first |
|---|---|---|
| Yeongdeungpo | South, Han and Anyang | Treatment plant and station unloading floor. The market as a reserved-ration window. |
| Yangcheon, Guro, Geumcheon | South, Anyang | Depots and industrial blocks. Schools for drying parts. |
| Gangseo | North, Han | Banghwa depot and research blocks. The airport memory went to storage. |
| Yongsan, Mapo | North, Han | Rail warehouses and berths. Broadcast blocks as record relay. |
| Jongno, Jung-gu | North, Cheonggye | Administrative blocks and universities. Ledgers and sealed stacks. |
| Seongdong, Gwangjin | North, Han | Workshops and bridge approaches. Schools as repair practice. |
| Gangdong | South, Han | Treatment plant and eastern bridges. Hospitals as turbid-water isolation. |
| Songpa, Gangnam, Seocho | South, Tancheon and Han | Ration towers and the Jamsil and Suseo windows. Apartment lower floors sealed. |
| Dongjak, Gwanak | South | Noryangjin cold storage, southern-slope evacuation. |
| Eunpyeong, Gangbuk, Seongbuk | North, Bukhansan | Evacuation halls, highland warehouses. |
| Dobong, Nowon | North, Jungnang | Changdong wheel works, northern rail quarters. |
| Jungnang, Dongdaemun | North, Jungnang | Transfers and Jegi-dong. The market split medicinal herbs and transfer freight. |

## Past Use and Present Application

Pre-collapse use grounded the present application. What follows was the default, and the river and gu layers lay over it.

| Observed | Default use |
|---|---|
| School | Dong militia quarters. The playground for freight marshaling. Special rooms sealed. |
| Community center | The dong's ledgers and the ration window. |
| Hospital · clinic | Field medicine. Near the treatment plants, turbid-water isolation. |
| University | Record-copying workshops. Laboratory blocks sealed or stripped for parts. |
| Market | Licensed market. Splitting reserved portions from sale portions. |
| Station | Hand-off of living freight and the refuge floors. The tracks carried the state's transit toll. |
| Apartment | Habitation only above the third floor. Floors 1–2 flooded storage or abandoned. |
| Park · green space | Thin cultivation, grazing, monsoon drainage. Explicitly stated unless used as burial ground. |
| Shop · office | Workshop or shutter-sealed. |

## Floor by Floor

| `state` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `flooded` | Monsoon and tributary water. Habitation forbidden. |
| `sealed` | Planks and iron gates. No entry without license. |
| `occupied` | In human use. Rations, quarters, hand-offs. |
| `abandoned` | Corners emptied. Low-intensity searching only. |
| `yard` | Open air. Playgrounds, freight marshaling. |

Minimum floors by use:

| Observed | Floors |
|---|---|
| Station | B2 tracks, B1 platform, first floor concourse, rooftop watchtower |
| School | First floor entrance and playground, second floor classroom quarters, third floor special rooms sealed, rooftop |
| Apartment | Floors 1–2 flooded or abandoned, floor 3 habitation, rooftop sealed |
| Community center | First floor window, second floor stacks sealed |
| Hospital · clinic | First floor waiting, second floor beds |
| University | First floor copying workshop, laboratory blocks sealed |
| Market | Basement warehouses, first floor licensed market |
| Park · green space | One floor at grade |
| Everything else | First floor, rooftop when needed |

The waterfront's first floor was `flooded` or storage. Habitation was above the third floor.
