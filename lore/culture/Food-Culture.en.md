# Food Culture

The table of that age survived as the ladle at the station window. Seoul in 2026 had been a city that got through its days on Garak Market auctions, the dawn unloading at the Noryangjin Fisheries Market, convenience stores, and school meals; on the night of the [motive-power revocation](../overview/World-Unbinding.md), when refrigeration folded and remote ordering stopped, the leftover rice, the ice, and the water in the tanks gathered into concourse rations.

A century later, in 2126, the line still stood before that window. The habit survived without reviving any trade name — water came first, rice followed — and the arithmetic that no facility or market moved until the residents' share was reserved had already been written by [Economy and Production](../economy/Economy-and-Production.md). Only the seats where the numbers showed up as soup and fish were carried on here.

## The Station Ration Meal

Where a dong had a subway, the ration looked to the station first. School playgrounds and community-center windows were support, and the core facility was the station concourse; only when the Yeongdeungpo treatment watch opened the tap did water fill the rice pots of the Sinjeong depot, and once the Amsa escort locked the canisters, the queues at Suseo and Garak lengthened in the same afternoon.

The Cathedral Accreditation State accredited marriages and memorial rites at the Myeongdong altar and owned no fields. Garak Market's price ledger remained an empty ledger of the unaffiliated corridors of unclaimed territory, the Won Buddhists of Dongjak read the weight of fish against the ice credit of the Noryangjin cold-storage rooms, the skeleton of the spheres belonged to [the Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md), and the use of buildings belonged to [River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse](../places/Building-Reuse-Geography.md).

The base of the window was porridge. The pots left by the school kitchens and shelter cookrooms of 2026 clung to one side of the concourse, the licensed markets split reserved portions from sale portions, and when the lower-floor ration stores of Jamsil and Garak failed to manage the heat of the grain sacks they became one room with the damp walls, running on into the coughs of [Ailments and Symptoms](../ailments/Ailments.md).

The refugee shelters on the Bukhansan Bogukmun side leaned on outside rice. They offered medicinal herbs and mountain paths, but heating fuel ran short, and when the autumn columns came south, the windows of the Neutral Convoy City and the Fabrication Alliance came under pressure first. The titles of events belonged to the [Scenario Timeline](../chronology/Scenario-Timeline.md) as canon; only the condition remained here that the rice line could be bound to water rights and service re-registration.

A transfer station cut from power stopped its freight too. Even with the tunnel sound, the traction current died, and Noryangjin's frozen stock was the first to melt on that outage; in summers when counterfeit medicine circulated, ice credit and water tests wavered in one line. When food waste was sealed off ahead of the citizen windows to protect the rations, the leftovers in the alleys called dogs and rats, and the way people and swarms contested the same remains was written by [People and Machines](../people-and-machines/People-and-Machines.md).

## Carp, Crucian Carp, and Catfish of the Tanks

The tanks survived the revocation. Remote safety control was dead, and the collector wells under the concourses, the depot drainage ponds, and the sedimentation basins of the treatment plants remained in human hands; the trade of holding on the station tank water the carp, crucian carp, and catfish that had been caught in the Han River and the reservoirs even in 2026 went by the name of tank aquaculture.

The wetlands outside the Yeongdeungpo and Yeouido sluices were that water surface. The sedimentation basin beside the Ttukseom pumps, the lower cells under the Amsa treatment plant, and the Changdong depot drainage ponds stood in the same line; the tank fish covered the shallow water, and the place where raptors looked down from the wires overlapped the edible cells. The watch wrote the section's water level to be divided among rice, birds, and trains, and the wetland-side colonies were the field of [G06](../bestiary/groups/Hostile-Group-G06.md).

Lifted onto one ledger, the broth turned cloudy. The hands that mixed excrement, feed, and drinking water opened the next line of waterborne disease, and connecting the water of a crowded carp cell to the ration taps made that day's supper one line with a failed inspection. The catfish ate the organic matter off the floor and veiled the cloudiness, and the crucian carp multiplied fast in the shallow water and soothed the numbers on the scale for a while, but they could not erase the reason the medical officer opened the tank and the medicine chest together.

The first floor of the Han's main channel stood close to abandoned space. Habitation pushed above the third floor, cooking pushed inside the embankment, the sewage and floods along the tributaries turned playgrounds into detour embankments, and bringing fish caught beyond them to a window passed the inspection bench of the licensed market. Noryangjin's frozen remains and unloaded viscera became the dogs' night road, and the live fish of the station tanks read as protein that got through one more day without power.

## The Water Compacts and Water Rationing

The creation oral record set no year count. The First Water Compact was struck where the surviving treatment plants and the pump parts lay scattered through different spheres; the Yeouido treatment officer and the Ttukseom pump overseer traded water for parts, and the hands that read it as public rights and the hands that read it as a protection contract divided at that berth.

That exchange was the seed of the great powers. The weight of the oral record in which the Water Compact, the Sermon Registry State, and the Escort Protection State grew into water great powers remained on the scales of the Noryangjin berth, and [the Stories](Oral-Stories.md) arranged that testimony into a list. Only the consequence — that rice followed that weight — was written here.

Without water the pot could not stand. Even with ration rice stacked, without the treatment watch's signature the porridge was boiled in cloudy water, and that cloudiness ran on into the summers of waterborne disease and counterfeit medicine.

The Second Water Compact was written again after the pumps ran out their lives. The officers of Yeouido, Ttukseom, Amsa, and Jegi-dong at that time left the minimum line, the standards, the emergency convoys, and the termination conditions, the Ttukseom overseer stood surety for central water and parts, the minimum water right remained with the support of the lesser states, and the protection clause remained as the grounds for the Amsa elders' intervention. The boxes of the Yongsan transshipment shed and the wheel exchanges at the Changdong scales were the seats of that wording's testimony.

When the third water contract of opening day expired, the price of water rose. Medical convoys ran late and the lesser states' overlapping contracts began; the Cathedral Accreditation State's auction was only the public-merit rite of the Myeongdong altar, not a site of production, Garak's empty price ledger had no owner and shook with that tremor as it stood, and the Won Buddhists' ice melted together with the power.

Water without the Arisu mark was guest water. The habit survived of using it neither for rites nor for the ration pots unless a director-general whose name stood at the sluice gate was present; the lock on the faith side was held by [the Faith-and-Culture Schism](Faith-Culture-Schism.md), and the skeleton of the gates and plants was carried by [the Structures Compendium](../structures/Structures.md).

Garak was a great market with no treatment of its own. The Gwangam plant sat in Hanam, a contested asset outside the opening slots, and the Gangbuk plant sat in Namyangju, weakening the rice lines of Bukhansan Bogukmun, Changdong, Seoul Station, and Sangam; the public water-supply zones were not carried over into post-collapse territory as they stood. A watched sluice made a state, and that state licensed the ladle at the window.
