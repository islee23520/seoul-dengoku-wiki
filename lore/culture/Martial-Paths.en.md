# Martial Paths

These were not skills chosen from a catalog; they read best as the hands of long practice. In the Seoul of 2026 people had lived at the Yeongdeungpo treatment plant's sluices, at the Sinjeong, Cheonwang, and Godeok depots, in the churches where pulpits remained, at guard posts, and in the deed record rooms, and after the call mesh died the hands remained. The Changdong depot lived on in those hands as memory alone, and in the story's present it stood empty without a state, read as the empty depot of the oral record. A century passed and the habits of the hand became transmission, and masters and taboos kept those habits. Technique read as the habit by which a body had survived in the land, not as a slot attached to a species.

Training lay close to continuing the work. Being struck hardened the body; mending grew the hands. When a limb went, the trade changed, and a prosthesis went on only after the cut. After it went on, the numbers spoke. The place and timing before it went on were decided by unit commands.

Names were given by the oral record and the martial masters. On the battlefield they showed themselves only within the current orders given to the unit where those hands gathered. The shield wall, encouragement, focus, and mobile regrouping of the 2026-09-07 formation cards were a POC record and did not go onto the technique list. Detailed numbers this chapter did not lock; where locked, [combat](../../GDD/rules/Realtime-Formation-Card-Battle.md) was canon.

## Hand-Forms

The hand read in six forms. Not a theory of circulating ki along meridians, but the forms a body learned at the plants, the depots, the pulpits, the posts, and the stacks. The world had not forgotten physics, so no single blow that stopped a person by pressing a vital point existed. Nor were there roof-flying steps. Sovereignty over healing sat with [Ailments](../ailments/Ailments.md), the medical officers, and the Medical Neutrality League; no school replaced the medicine that brought down a fever or stopped a dysentery.

| Hand-form | Work in Seoul | Unit command | What was not left as numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inner Breath | Cuts the lungs against brine, turbid water, asbestos, and flooded tunnels. The breath that holds out to the end over registers and seals is also here | Shows first at a halt. Where water stood, the Sluice Hand's breath ran long | Length of breath and numbers of recovery |
| Outer Hand | Being struck hardens the body. The forms of shoulder, knee, hammer, and shield | Attack and formation | Damage numbers, invulnerability |
| Light Step | Moves catwalks, storage tracks, yellow lines, and stack alleys in file | Movement and formation change | Jump height, movement cells |
| Tools | Buys only what sat in the order of battle among the four layers of [this era's arms](../goods/Era-Arms-and-Tech-Level.md) — trade tools, police-issue gear, military remnants, recovered lostech | Attack and halt. Stronghold gear deployed only after a halt | Stock, model issue range, fabrication recipes |
| Restraint | Cuts the number of attackers landing at once with interval, roll-call, accreditation severance, and wheel locks. | Hand-Mending | Mends equipment, registers, the turbid-water confession, and morale. It did not raise fallen soldiers | After a halt | Healing amounts, compounding medicine |

Putting Inner Breath and Hand-Mending in one cell made waterborne disease and hammer repair the same cure. Sickness and repair were divided.

## The Nine Schools

The first five schools came out of facility watches and trades. As the gauges began to be walked again after the weakening of control, four more lines grew from school physical education, military drill, and recovered physical-AI equipment. The kendo, iaido, koryu, taekwondo, and bayonet training of 2026 were named in full only when explaining origins. The people of 2126 had not inherited those organizations and manuals intact, so the successor schools took new names from the things and training places they had kept.

The ledgers and master address used Sino-Korean formal names; the field used the old everyday aliases. The formal names showed what the training handled, and the field aliases were never removed or treated as lesser words.

The hands of medicine, of the altar, of the charter, and the dispatch of the rail cavalry were trades or organizations keeping facilities. They were not given names as if they were schools.

## Schools and States

A school was not the state-name cell. The heads of [the sixteen states](../factions/Sixteen-States.md) did not represent their state's hands; title, trade, and martial path remained three layers. A president could be a maintenance hand; a chairman could be a Record Blade. A trade alias like the Water Director-General was never raised to a school-elder title. Only the five offices of [Offices and Ranks](../offices/Offices-and-Ranks.md) went into the ledger cells.

The rivers and lakes were read by appearance, not as an owned cell.

At the Water Compact the Sluice Hand ran thick, and Wheel Hammers lived together on the Sinjeong depot side. Han Jaemok's hand was the Sluice Hand. Hiding turbid water cut that transmission, and in a state with wide water duty that cut shook the rations. At the Standards Alliance the Wheel Hammer overlapped the bolt rites. Gang Minseo's hand was the Wheel Hammer, and off-standard modification was cast out. At the Line Succession State the Wheel Hammer sat thick on the transshipment watches. Park Taegyeom's hand was that, and escorting was borrowed from the Yongsan garrison memory and convoy contracts. At the Escort Protection State the Escort Shield and the Amsa Sluice Hand stood in tension under one emergency superintendence. Bae Ujin's hand was the Escort Shield. The commander did not double as the Amsa treatment watch's Sluice Hand. The Yangjae Machineworks Company's depot was Wheel Hammer trade, but President Jeong Hojun's hand was the Record Blade. The house that mended car bodies and the chairman who severed ledgers were not merged into one school.

Among the lesser and theocratic states too, the hand did not follow the state. The hand of Yun Seorin of the Republic of Korea Government was the Record Blade; the Gwanghwamun seal chest was that line. On the Yeouido transmission watch the Pulpit Voice grew. The taboo against raising a living pastor's name onto the school remained at the Yeouido microphone stand as well. On the General Assembly register line of the Sermon Registry State there were hands of completed Wheel Hammer transmission. The Pulpit Voice and pulpit-lineage faith were not read as one line; the Pulpit Voice was a school and the pulpit lineage a line of [Faith](Faith-Culture-Schism.md). The Presbyterians did not monopolize the Pulpit Voice.

The hand of Baek On of the Sangha Relief State had no martial path. The rivers-and-lakes branch was the Open Hand inside the order. The hand of Jang Sehwa of the Neutral Convoy City was the Wheel Hammer; she borrowed the Escort Shield only when convoying. Borrowing was order-of-battle, not dual practice. The hand of Go Seojun of the Gate Military Government was the Escort Shield. The Guui Sluice Hand could live inside the same state, but the commander did not double as it. The hand of I Hongwon of the Seocho Computing Group was the Record Blade; the cooling keys and barrier-gate ledgers were that line, and the rail cavalry was facility military, not a school. The heads of the Medical Neutrality League, the Cathedral Accreditation State, and Won Buddhism had no martial path. Medicine, the altar, and the charter remained trades.

The Sluice Hand lived together at the lock-gate watches of Yeongdeungpo, Amsa, and Guui. The Water Compact did not monopolize the transmission. Even if the Wheel Hammer's oral record remembered the Changdong depot, that place was unclaimed-territory training, not a state seal. Suseo, Banghwa, Ttukseom, and Garak were not capitals either, and so were not written as school head temples.

The Escort Shield grew out of those remnants, but the four organizations were not read as schools. When the storage-track run and a rail-cavalry dispatch fought over the same rail, that was an organizational conflict, not a school matchup.

## Masters, Dual Practice, and the Punishment Tribunal

Transmission passed only from person to person. Books alone did not get one into a school; when a martial master died the line became oral record, and mixing schools with schools injured the body. No new plague was invented. When Inner Breath and Outer Hand fought in one chest, the cough and the joints went together. The strata of symptoms followed waterborne disease, asbestos, and the cooling-water ledger.

A master was one kind of relation in the ledger. Not the same title as the priests of a faith chapel. Where this chapter spoke of transmission, it wrote martial master.

The five tiers of title and the ranks of the martial path did not cross. A president could be an Apprentice Hand; a jusa clerk could be an Elder Hand.

| Martial rank | Work | Relation to title |
|---|---|---|
| Apprentice Hand | Did the work but was not yet in the school | Any tier possible. Persons with a trade only stayed here |
| Watch Hand | The hand whose transmission opened before a martial master | Independent of tier |
| Master Hand | Could pass on one line | Independent of tier |
| Elder Hand | Kept the taboo. Declared expulsion and casting-out | Being a head of state did not make one an elder |

Dual practice happened only at the lowest rank. Tier 5 of [Offices and Ranks](../offices/Offices-and-Ranks.md) — jusa, byeongjang, gyosa, gyowoo, sindo, gyodo, jikwon, daeri, johapwon, hosongwon, hoewon — was that lowest rung. When a lowest-rank hand briefly borrowed two forms, the body's price went to two knots. It was not read as a matchup advantage.

When tier 4 or above carried two schools in one pair of hands, that state's accreditation and prosecution opened. The oral records of the martial paths called this the Punishment Tribunal. The Joseon Ministry of Punishments was not revived. The hand that sealed for each state was tier 2. Minister, bureau head, chief of staff, elder, priest, head of the General Directorate, senior dharma teacher, vice-chairman, company president, vice-chairwoman, standing director, corps head, director. When a head of state carried two transmissions, the Punishment Tribunal opened.

Borrowing was not dual practice. Attaching an Escort Shield squad under a convoy contract was order of battle. Jang Sehwa's hand remained the Wheel Hammer, and the borrowed shields were that squad's hands. Commanding a force that included two schools' units and carrying two transmissions in one body were not written as one line.

## The Open Hand

A rivers-and-lakes branch learned by those who wandered between stations and remembered roads and people when needed. It went onto no cell of the nine schools. Baek On's hand had no martial path, and the Sangha Relief State did not own the whole branch. No proper sect name was borrowed, and no staff-trick secrets or sect rankings were made.

Sitting before the tablets emptied the Pulpit Voice's name-fixing with bare hands. A seat with no name raised could not be called. When the refugee-line dividing occupied a catwalk, the Sluice Hand lost its road. That clash was not locked as an Open-Hand matchup advantage.

## The Transfer Circle

The Transfer Circle was not a school. Nor an organization gathered only from the addressless or the homeless. A mobile mutual-aid net in which station veterans, porters, repair hands, interpreters, care workers, traders, and lost travelers, each with a home and an affiliation, exchanged needed work.

Its members tied safe roads between stations, resting places, job leads, water and medicine, storage chests, and the last sightings of missing persons through ledgers and oral record. A record of kept promises became credit, and debts were repaid not only in goods but in labor, information, and care. No state or religion owned the Transfer Circle whole. Local circles could take different decisions on neutrality, taxes, and protection contracts.

Pickpocketing and smuggling were livelihoods that became disputes even inside the Transfer Circle. Even if some members touched them, not all members were called thieves. A member who monopolized a road or sold the weak's lodging lost their surety and was turned away at other stations. The player, rather than trusting rumor and stigma at face value, checked witnesses, hand-off ledgers, and the flow of goods.

## The Wuxia Effect of Lens Breath

Lens Breath was not a school teaching only realistic firearms doctrine. When rare physical-AI lenses and sensors and drive assists attached, the master's movement looked faster than a person could follow. Threats in view were read in layers; recoil and body sway were assisted; and for short spans, engagements in several directions were held together. This effect was an exaggerated martial-arts feel made by equipment, and was not recorded as supernatural magic.

The price was plain. When power fell or the lenses clouded, the body returned to that of an ordinary marksman. Without ammunition or maintenance half the school vanished, and a wrong sensor classification could read a comrade as a threat. Left long to let the module judge in the user's place, it repeated the memory of loss of control. The elders of Lens Breath set first not the strong but those who knew when to switch off the equipment and withdraw.

## Limbs and Prostheses

A severed arm folded that school's transmission in half. With a prosthesis the trade remained, and the martial path became a different line. Fitting an artificial hand did not bring back the Sluice Hand's transmission. The same held after the Record Blade's hand died of forgery. Even if the prosthesis spared the wrist, the transmission did not return. As an efficacy-less stockpiled medicine does not fool a fever, medicine did not give the dead hand back.

| Injury | Trade | Martial path | After the prosthesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Severed arm | That work's hand folds | That school's transmission folds in half | The trade may remain. The martial path is a different line |
| Severed leg | The watch place changes | Light Step folds first | The artificial leg leaves walking. The catwalk step and the storage-track run do not return |
| Hearing | The axle echo and the roll-call die | The Wheel Hammer's inner breath, the Pulpit Voice's roll-call fold | The hearing aid leaves trade communication |
| Lungs and turbid water | Sluice watch becomes dangerous | The Sluice Hand's inner breath folds | Respiratory protection is a trade layer. It does not replace the lock-gate breath |

A prosthesis went on only after the cut. The numbers spoke after it went on, and this chapter did not write them.

## Relation to Combat

A school read as the axis on which a long-practiced hand grew in combat. What was directly commanded was not individual heroes but the chosen unit. Command was limited to setting formation and front, choosing units, and confirming one of movement, attack, facing change, formation change, or halt. The confirmed order replaced that unit's current order. Orders were not stacked, and neither direct hero actions nor card resources existed.

Named heroes and soldier squads were separate battlefield actors. Heroes were not counted into the soldier headcount. A soldier squad's cap was twenty, and that number was not read as school transmission headcount. A hero with a school showed the hand only when that hero received the same five commands. Individual hero action sequences were not directly input.

With an Escort Shield attached, fewer blades landed at once; the Sluice Hand's breath ran long where water stood. The Wheel Hammer lived only on mended equipment. Detailed numbers were not locked; where locked, the combat document was canon.

A technique was the way the hand showed when that order matched the battlefield archetype, the order of battle, and the taboo. Whether water stood, whether equipment had been mended, whether the register was a true copy, whether the unit had halted — those were the premises. The same battle and the same order did not end in a different hand.

The aptitude of a battlefield archetype was quality. It was not counted by adding numbers. The Han crossing was the Sluice Hand's; the depot approach was the Wheel Hammer's; the short fronts of transfer-station floors and small station houses were the Pulpit Voice's and the Record Blade's; the single file of a tunnel was the Escort Shield's; the surface ruins were where Escort Shield mobility met the Pulpit Voice's roll-call. At a terminal's blocked end, Restraint was strong.

End states — ordinary victory and defeat, ordered withdrawal, morale rout, accepted surrender — were not overwritten by techniques. With no lawful exit, only ordered withdrawal was blocked; the choice of surrender never disappeared. Capture was not an end state but a body status attached per person after battle. Surrendered, routed, and wounded were not automatically turned into prisoners.

## Sluice Hand

The hand of treatment-plant and lock-gate watches. Transmission passed person to person at the lock gates of Yeongdeungpo, Amsa, and Guui. The Gangbuk and Gwangam plants lay outside the city and were not head temples of the transmission. The treatment-watch line of the creation oral record remained in the martial masters' memory, and hiding turbid water cut the transmission.

Water was the wreckage of the system that had purified raw Han water and sent it on. After pumps, chlorine injection, and turbidity testing came down to human watches, this hand grew. When the tank-farming cells and the drinking-water cells used one surface, that day's cloudy water became the evening soup. Cooling water did not go onto the drinking ledger. When turbidity reading read cooling-water admixture as clear, the next waterborne disease opened on that watch's signature. Cutting the raw water was a watch bound to taboo, not a technique of the table. The Medical Neutrality League saw the dysentery and fever; the Sluice Hand cut the cause above the water's surface.

## Wheel Hammer

Changdong, Suseo, and Banghwa were unclaimed, so transmission there was dangerous and the standards elders came rarely.
