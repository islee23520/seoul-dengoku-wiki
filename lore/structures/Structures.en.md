# The Structures Compendium

The Seoul of the time was not a flat administrative map but a graph of stations, tunnels, and sluices. Even in 2026 people crossed gu by subway, and the treatment centers and depots sat as the heavy nodes of that graph. On the night of the [motive-power revocation](../overview/World-Unbinding.md), when remote control died, stations became fortresses and the centers of states, tunnels became licensed passage, and treatment plants and sluices became lock gates that opened only under signature. A century on, the sixteen states of 2126 read those points and lines as living spheres and never changed gu names into state names.

This page did not re-describe the stations and gu the geography documents had already locked. The catalog's 334 stations belonged to [the Seoul Station Catalog](../places/Seoul-Station-Catalog.md), the 25 gu and the assembly order to [the world-map structure](../places/World-Map-Construction.md), the six layers to [Seoul and the Subway Layers](../places/World-and-Subway-Layers.md), and the use of buildings to [River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse](../places/Building-Reuse-Geography.md). Here only the kinds of structure that held the states up on that skeleton were gathered.

## Stations

A station was not a flag completed at occupation but a stronghold whose people, facilities, and connections had to be relit. Where a dong had a subway, that station building became the core facility, and rationing, transit tolls, shelter, and refuge looked to the station first. Schools, community centers, and hospitals were support buildings. When Yeongdeungpo Station held the treatment watch and the unloading floor, the Water Compact's window opened in its concourse; when Seoul Station kept the accreditation seals, the Republic of Korea Government's legitimacy stood and remained at its threshold; when Amsa Station overlapped treatment and escort, the eastern perimeter's protection sphere was read on its platforms. Sindorim was the junction where the door became the state's bottleneck between Yeongdeungpo and Guro, and Suseo the place where the southern depot and contract administration sat under one roof.

The interior layers were six: surface ruins, the station concourse, platforms and tracks, transfer corridors, deep tunnels, and depot adjacency. The concourse carried markets, habitation, and administration; the platforms movement, defense, and freight; the transfers the politics between lines and the seat of transit tolls. The entry procedure and facility-slot numbers were written as design in [Entering the Station Interior](../places/Station-Interior-Construction.md), but it did not say the current running map already held the interiors of all 334. A stronghold's authority, cooperation, and integration conditions were computed by [Strongholds and Territory](../economy/Strongholds-and-Territory.md).

Which station name belonged to which state was left unconfirmed. Only the sphere arms were fixed by [the Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md), and occupation went by building-unit dominance. Seoul Station in Jongno and Jung-gu, the rail warehouses of Yongsan, Amsa in Gangdong, the Garak windows in Songpa, and Magok and Banghwa in Gangseo were often called the representative nodes of their spheres. The ban on turning a gu into a state held in this compendium too.

## Tunnels

Between station and station lay lines that were never erased. Only the states changed — normal, hazardous, controlled, blockaded, flooded, collapsed, provisional repair — and cause and repair conditions followed. Yeongdeungpo–Sindorim and Sindorim–Guro were adjacencies the current map already knew, and no line skipped Yeongdeungpo–Guro in one cell. The full catalog's adjacencies went up as design data as 435 drawn from map points.

A transfer station cut from power stopped the traction freight flow even with both tunnels sound. Provisional repair passed only foot and light freight; heavy freight and armed passage took separate approval. The deeps were the seat of covert movement and maintenance, but flooding and communications loss came first. The formulas of throughput, isolation, and overreach were locked in [Logistics and Infrastructure](../economy/Logistics-and-Infrastructure.md).

The looping chassis took these lines as the stage of their last search and patrol routines, and the rat colonies nested in the cable trays under the transfer shopping streets. The bodies beside people were written by [People and Machines](../people-and-machines/People-and-Machines.md). The creation oral record of a tunnel newly dug was not canon. The 2026 lines remained with only their states changed.

## Depots

Depot area was the normal-time memory of rolling stock and maintenance; post-collapse operating volume was creative. The measured names within Seoul were eight: Sinjeong, Cheonwang, Banghwa, Gunja, Godeok, Changdong, Sinnae, and Suseo. The Water Compact corresponded to Sinjeong, the Standards Alliance to Cheonwang, the Escort Protection State to Godeok, and the Neutral Convoy City to Sinnae. Banghwa, Gunja, Changdong, and Suseo were no state's capital depot but remained unclaimed or edge corridors. They were the seats emptied of the Magok research, Ttukseom workshops, Changdong wheels, and Suseo protection capitals. Jichuk, Dobong, Moran, and Gimpo remained pressure from outside the Seoul slots.

A depot was the seat of repair, bulk storage, and siege. Sinjeong's unloading and Godeok's train maintenance became a state's arms of legitimacy because the hands that moved a stopped train again or sent a convoy were there; Changdong's wheels and brakes lost their owner and remained only as leases in neighboring heads' ledgers. The drainage ponds became the water surface where tank aquaculture and migratory birds overlapped, and in the deep bays of maintenance sheds the sealing of looping chassis attached as a creative layer. The charging terminals and hydraulic hoses were the repair layer of [Lost Technology](../technology/Lost-Technology-Lineage.md).

The industrial blocks of Guro and Geumcheon clung to the depots and used schools for drying parts, and the residential shells of Dobong and Nowon could not hold water without Changdong. Depot names were not raised to state names. The wheels and dispatch that people watched over became the state names.

## Treatment Plants and Sluices

For water to flow, a person with a name stood before the lock gate. The measured sites within Seoul were the Yeongdeungpo, Ttukseom, Guui, and Amsa Arisu treatment centers; Gangbuk lay in Namyangju and Gwangam in Hanam. The public water-supply districts were not carried over into post-collapse territory as they stood. That the Water Compact held the Yeongdeungpo site and sluices, the Sermon Registry State the Ttukseom pumps and workshops, the Gate Military Government the Guui limited supply, and the Escort Protection State the Amsa treatment and Godeok depot under military superintendence was the creative disposition of the time. The table's source states were canon in [the Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md).

The sluice was the seat where rite and contract shared one handle. The Arisu mark rose as an old-world symbol, and the habit remained that water without the mark was guest water, used in neither the ration pots nor the rites. The oral record that the First and Second Water Compacts bound the watches of Yeouido, Ttukseom, Amsa, and Jegi-dong in one wording was arranged as a list by [The Stories](../culture/Oral-Stories.md). The symptoms of turbid water becoming waterborne disease and of cooling loops mixing into the drinking ledger were [Ailments and Symptoms](../ailments/Ailments.md); the order in which the pot waited for the sluice was [Food Culture](../culture/Food-Culture.md).

The first floors north and south of the Han, and the tributaries Jungnang, Anyang, Tan, Cheonggye, and Hongje, decided which dongs got wet. Yeouido, Ichon, Banpo, Jamsil, Mangwon, Hapjeong, Amsa, Guui, and Jayang were read as main-channel waterfront. Habitation pushed above the third floor and the ration windows sat inside the embankment. The details of the waterfront rules were already written by [River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse](../places/Building-Reuse-Geography.md).

## Passages

Passages were the narrow lines where transfer, maintenance, and refuge overlapped. The transfer corridors were the hubs of politics and logistics between lines, and blockades, transit tolls, and congestion came first. The junctions where the Gangdong and Amsa ration corridors met the Eunpyeong and Seongbuk evacuation stairs became the warm nests of the rat colonies, and the fictional Sindorim junction split the main freight door from the maintenance passage that took only people and packloads, using siege as its stage. The limit that the Sindorim incident's place was one fictional junction unrelated to the catalog's 334 stood in [the Scenario Timeline](../chronology/Scenario-Timeline.md).

The vertical connections were stairs, elevators, and ventilation shafts. Flooded floors showed cause and repair conditions and kept people out — that was the design. The Bukhansan Bogukmun mountain refuge roads, the Jegi-dong herb unloading, and the lower passages under the Noryangjin elevated joined the same graph from outside the stations. Connections were never deleted. Their states changed, and those states changed the times of rice, medicine, and convoys.

The market's four names — Garak Market, Gangseo Market, Yanggok Market, and the Noryangjin Fisheries Market — were surrounding facilities of the stronghold slots and could not replace stations. Garak was the hub of price discovery and not a production site; Noryangjin leaned on frozen and ice credit and was fragile to power cuts. The thousands of convenience stores and clinics were the noise of trades, and went into no cell of this compendium.
