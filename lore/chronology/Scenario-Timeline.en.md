# Scenario Timeline

## Principles of the Chronicle

This timeline was not to be read as a script replaying fixed history. Events created conditions, and conclusions shifted with the ambitions, fears, relations, memories, and offices of living persons. When a key person died, was captured, or was persuaded, the same event led to different outcomes under a different actor.

The common-era anchors were locked at the motive-power revocation of 2026 and the story opening of 2126. The events, goods, and institutional changes of the 101 years between were canon in [the Hundred-Year Annals of Seoul Dengoku](Century-Annals.md). This document owned the conditional events after the opening, the seasonal chronicle, and the prototype cards. The name of the cause lived in [motive-power revocation](../overview/World-Unbinding.md).

The generation of 2126 had not lived the revocation. The First Water Compact, the military-household register, the Sixteen Banners, the Merit Subjects' Winter, and the Second Water Compact were the creation oral record. A founder entered in the ledgers and a person seated at the opening were not read as one body.

The four seasons of the first year and the Hegemony War of the second were story time after the opening. No common-era year went into the titles. The starting point of technology and law was 2026. [The arms and technology of this era](../goods/Era-Arms-and-Tech-Level.md). The conglomerate families, the old government, the external theaters, and the reform houses belonged to [the Houses](../factions/Chaebol-Houses-and-Century-Factions.md).

## The Connected City, Just Before 2026

Seoul was still the capital of one country. The conglomerate families controlled the affiliates with small stakes; the Military Manpower Administration took volunteer intake selection; the pulpits and altars held real estate and registers. Factory density was first in the world, and humanoids were under field test at Hyundai Motor. Water and rail, markets and medicine, communications and cooling and heat ran on one call mesh of institutional and family ledgers. The connections that later became states grew from that year's watch rosters. Whose mistake had been covered up became, a century later, the secret of the license chest.

## The Night of the Revocation, 2026

The oral record called it the Great Blackout. The canon name was motive-power revocation. The fleet calls, GNSS, the mobile networks, and the licensing servers stopped waiting for one another, and the grid SCADA, belonging to the same mesh, folded the lights. The emergency officers of the Yeouido plant, the Ttukseom workshops, the Amsa guard, and the records wire exercised emergency powers to save their own jurisdictions. The joint command mesh broke that night. Living-sphere emergency committees arose in every sphere, and when those powers were returned stayed the seed of a century of legitimacy disputes.

## The Creation Oral Record — No Year Counts

Han Jaemok, Im Hajun, Bae Ujin, Yun Seorin, Gang Minseo, and Baek On were the generation of 2126. The acts of creation were done by the offices and the prior organizations. The names the house ledgers entered as founders and the people alive at the opening were not read as one body. The titles remained; no common-era year attached.

In the First Water Compact, the surviving plants and pump parts lay scattered through different spheres. The Yeouido treatment officer and the Ttukseom pump overseer struck a provisional compact trading water for parts. The hands that read it as public rights and the hands that read it as a protection contract divided on the spot, and the seed was planted from which the Water Compact, the Sermon Registry State, and the Escort Protection State grew into water great powers.

The military-household register dispute opened before the outer raids and the shortage of restoration hands. The Amsa military faction demanded household-unit service registration; the Bukhansan Bogukmun refugee delegates refused. Where voluntary service and citizenship bound together, the league of the Jegi-dong and Bogukmun elders remained; where forced registration remained, the Amsa officer network grew. The register citizens and escort protection of that time were copies of this dispute.

The Sixteen Banners rose when the central emergency committee dissolved for the last time. The sixteen spheres declared their own charters and offices — five great powers, eleven lesser states. The states that received accreditation gained legitimacy but owed the Records Bureau. The sixteen-state disposition of that time was read as the descendant of those banners, never as a photograph of that year.

In the Merit Subjects' Winter the founding merit subjects demanded office, ration rights, and hereditary rights all at once. Shaving the rewards grew coups; lavishing them scattered the titles. Some states became condominiums and some became court-guard states. The empty cells and hereditary cells the 2126 generation inherited were that winter's transcription.

The Second Water Compact reopened when the provisional pumps and train parts ran out their lives. The officers of Yeouido, Ttukseom, Amsa, and Jegi-dong of the time wrote the minimum line, the standards, the emergency convoys, and the termination conditions again. The Ttukseom overseer stood surety for central water and parts, and that wording remained in the ledgers up to the third contract of the time. The minimum water right was the lesser states' support, and the protection clause was the grounds of the Amsa elders' intervention.

## The Empty Century

Between the revocation and the opening lay one century. The event titles of this period were not canon. No missing year was written anew. The living-sphere banners and the water ledgers ran on, but the faces of the founding merit subjects and the everyday life of 2026 became oral record, copies, and myth.

The corporate heirs who inherited the conglomerate ledgers, the record guilds transcribing seals, the external watch officers of the gates, granaries, and strait, and the trade houses that raised the sluices, dispatch, and medicine lived through that century. Trade names remained as signboards or hardened into charter names. The generation of 2126 received the sixteen states, the water contracts, the HC·HP houses, and the XT theaters as a world taken for granted. The powers that held the original ledgers dunned the debts of creation; the powers with only copies made their present charter the sole law. The layer-by-layer mapping was in [the Houses](../factions/Chaebol-Houses-and-Century-Factions.md).

## The Story Opening

### Opening Day: The Third Water Contract

Im Hajun, Im Chowon, Han Jaemok, Jeong Hojun, Bae Ujin, Yun Seorin, Gang Minseo, Baek On, and Nam Yungyeong stood in that place. They were the generation of 2126 and had not lived the motive-power revocation. Im Hajun, steward of the Sermon Registry State, vanished, and the central water-and-parts contract handed down from the creation oral record expired at the same moment. As the great powers intervened over the Ttukseom succession and the new contract, the price of water rose, medical convoys ran late, and the lesser states' overlapping contracts and the officers' movements began. It could fork into confirmation of Im Hajun's survival, Im Chowon's enthronement, joint rule of the workshops, an Amsa protection government, or a Ttukseom split, and the three wills of spring received what followed.

## The Four Seasons of the First Year

### Spring: Three Wills

Yun Seorin and Choi Jiwoo, Im Chowon and the workshop delegate Han Somi read the wills. When three differing wills of Im Hajun and an edited voice record were found, the Records Bureau opened an accreditation review and the transmission company searched for original evidence. Support coalitions formed around each successor candidate. If Yun Seorin fixed the true copy, the Records Bureau's accreditation power strengthened; if Choi Jiwoo broadcast the fabrication first, riots and an open re-election broke out. If the player hid evidence, secret deals with the great powers became possible, but the risk of exposure remained. The turbid waters of summer followed.

### Summer: Turbid Water and Counterfeit Medicine

Jeong Hojun, Ryu Eunbi, Han Jaemok, and O Haerin took charge of the turbid water and the counterfeit medicine. When some water tests failed after the storms and counterfeit drugs circulated, the research council demanded open testing and the Water Director-General demanded delay to prevent panic. Trust in water and medicine shook, and the Noryangjin frozen stock fell into crisis. Immediate disclosure brought short riots and long-term recovery of trust; concealment kept supply but opened Choi Jiwoo's exposure event. A successful joint investigation formed the non-military league of Jegi-dong, Magok, and Sangam. Next came the autumn columns of Bukhansan Bogukmun.

### Autumn: The Bukhansan Bogukmun Columns

Baek On and Gang Minseo, Bae Ujin and Jang Sehwa opened the columns. When the north ran short of food and the service contracts expired, refugee families moved south, and Amsa bound water rights to service re-registration. The Neutral Convoy City and the Fabrication Alliance came under pressure over the refugee question. A citizenship-and-voluntary-service accord strengthened the lesser-state league; forced registration swelled Amsa's ranks but opened desertion, mutiny, and family-rescue events. Closing the borders brought mountain fighting at Bukhansan Bogukmun and a winter famine. The black dispatch board of winter followed.

### Winter: The Black Dispatch Board

Park Taegyeom, I Hongwon, Jang Sehwa, and Bae Ujin traced the dispatch board. When Amsa-made weapons were found on a medical train and the northern successor-examination parts were tampered with, the lesser states investigated whether the official command net or the secret officer network had moved the train. Suspicions of Bae Ujin's coup preparations and Park Taegyeom's adoptive-succession plan surfaced together. If Bae Ujin punished the officers, a limited joint defense was possible; if he approved after the fact, an eastern protection war began. When evidence of tampered weapons emerged, it revealed that a third person had steered toward war. The lesser-states council of the second year followed.

## The Hegemony War of the Second Year

### Spring: The Council of Eleven Seats

Jeong Yura called Jang Sehwa, Ryu Eunbi, Baek On, and the delegates of the eleven lesser states to one table. Where the water and protection contracts of three great powers crossed, they discussed joint negotiation rights for the lesser states and a mutual convoy treaty. The possibility arose that the structure of five great powers versus eleven lesser states would turn into five great powers versus one league of lesser states. It could fork into a joint charter, partitioned submission by great power, internal bribery and assassination, or a collapse of the council, and the next cell was the Sluice War or the Seoul Compact.

### After Summer: Five Unification Plans

The game forced no single conquest ending. According to the living persons and the treaties concluded, the following orders competed.

Yun Seorin's Records Federation united around legitimacy and the open ledgers; Han Jaemok's Sluice Alliance bound with the minimum water right and ration contracts. Gang Minseo's Fabrication Alliance bound through repair standards and labor citizenship; Jeong Hojun's Joint Technology Institute through a league of knowledge, medicine, and verification organs; Bae Ujin's Waterworks Protection Sphere through military command and a record of development; Jeong Yura's Multistate Compact through the joint negotiation of the lesser states and limited central power.

Each unification plan could deform into a peaceful federation, a loose alliance, a hegemonic protectorate, or a military conquest.

## Regional Events: Same Place, Different Pasts

Where the seasonal events wrote the chronicle of whole spheres, regional events changed the plan when the past left in one place returned. They could open at any story time, independent of the chronicle, and what the place remembered was only the state the player had actually left behind. The direction followed [the same Seoul, different adventures](../../GDD/proposals/Narrative-Direction.md).

### The Sindorim Junction: The Siege That Does Not Close the Door

A card registered as a track record. No human playtest had yet been run; the experiment procedure and record sheets were owned by [issue #86](https://github.com/islee23520/seoul-kenshi/issues/86), and the casting, space, and settlement design by [the design proposal](../../GDD/proposals/Scenario-Hold-the-Gate.md).

The expedition (the player) and companions, the site officer and the work crew, the transport officer and two porters, and the armed retrieval squad stood in that place. The cast was a set of role contracts, not canon cast assignments.

On the return leg of a personal transport commission received at Yeongdeungpo and closed at Guro, an additional transport commission was proposed. Two people meant to move one load of filter media and one load of copper parts; the site crew waited for the filter media; and the armed retrieval squad, claiming ownership of the copper, had sent notice of their arrival time. The truth of that claim was not yet verified. Even without taking this job, the first fee received remained yours. The space was a fictional junction of one station — an outer waiting point, the main freight door, an inner rallying point, and a maintenance passage that took only people and packloads; it did not reproduce the actual Sindorim facilities. The maintenance passage took no carts; one load of filter media could be split onto backs, but the copper load could not be moved without equipment. The retrieval squad already existed in all three pasts, and did not change the intent, times, and route it had published at departure to fit the ending.

The plan was one of: fulfill today, defer to the next transport window, partial delivery, an authorized agreement, or refusal. Repairing the door created no third party's passage right, and the copper could stay sealed in storage until ownership was confirmed. Here, unless the authority and authenticity of a written condition — that the retrieval squad would not object to the movement of people and filter media — were verified, the negotiation was not judged a success. The additional fees were promised separately for the filter-media delivery and the copper delivery; without delivering the copper, that share was not received. On deferral, the freight stayed in the verified outer warehouse. The transport officer arranged licenses and escort anew with the next work crew, and this card created no new disaster or grudge through deferral.

The remaining door state and licenses, the people and freight delivered, and the promises fulfilled forked by the three pasts and the plan. The three pasts were comparison conditions of different departure histories, not a sequential chain. How each past changed the next plan was the body of this event. The follow-up was a revisit.

The default conditions of B and C carry no designated crew license and no valid warrant standing in for it, and a new license from the ordinary window takes until past today's transport window. Settling the breach damage remains a separate promise and does not vanish because passage was licensed.

Choosing combat asked a separate question. In A, why keep the door open; in B, what would control the approach that cannot be closed instead; in C, whether there was reason to fight in the main passage at all. When only killing the same enemy in the same order remained as the answer, the goals lacked difference. The engagement plan was recorded, but this track card fixed no victory, casualties, or freight movement. That verification belonged to a separate combat prototype.

#### Companion Variants

The base comparison held companion conditions identical. A separate variant changed "unlicensed" to "permit documents unconfirmed" in B or C alone, and placed a valid warrant — covering the designated crew, the passage, and the D0/D1 times — in the transport officer's file from the start. The door state was unchanged. The companion changed information, and the information changed the plan.

| Condition | Verifiable help | Plan that could change |
|---|---|---|
| Courier along | Confirms the retrieval squad's arrival window and responsible window through a notice delivered by hand. | Reviews today's movement within the verified time slack. It cannot issue a passage license in their place. |
| Records keeper along | Spends 10 minutes checking whether the existing warrant covers this crew, passage, and time. | After the D0 13:40 check, today's window can be used. It cannot restore the broken door or adjudicate copper ownership. B's damage dispute and C's freight constraint remain. |
| Neither | Uses the published timetable and the ordinary window. | Defers to the next transport window to secure a license, or refuses the additional commission. The event as a whole is not blocked. |

Using the courier's information as the records keeper's authority, or conjuring a nonexistent warrant merely because a records keeper was present, was failure. The cast comparison of companion relations and motives was a separate task.

#### Six Outcome Cells

Outcomes remained in six cells instead of one sentence.

| Record cell | Values to distinguish |
|---|---|
| People | Delivered / waiting in warehouse. In engagement, capture, injury, and death remain undetermined until separate verification. |
| Freight | Filter media delivered or not / copper delivered, sealed, or held / storage site and receipt authority |
| Original fee | Already paid. Not recovered as an outcome of the additional commission. |
| Additional promises | Filter-media share / copper share / the promised today or next transport window / the actual time of fulfillment |
| Facilities · rights | The door's operable or damaged state / license holders, passages, times / unresolved damage disputes |
| Direct relations | Who saw what, and with whom what promise was kept. No automatic state alliance or campaign-long grudge |

The revisit followed the outcome. In A, with everything delivered, the work crew used the new filter media and paid the promised price. In B, with the copper on hold, the transport officer thanked the party for the people's return and asked to meet again at the unsealing. In C, even though the maintenance passage was actually used, the big freight door's repair was still the next job. If the additional fee was already received at first delivery, the revisit settlement turned into receipt confirmation and paid nothing twice. On refusal, no line was played praising a structure not built today.

#### Counterexamples

| Change to inject | Expected handling |
|---|---|
| A's license changed to cover the party only | No designated transport today. Return via separate authority check or deferral |
| Choosing "close the door" in B | Tell them it is physically impossible, and steer to other access control, negotiation, or withdrawal |
| Auto-inserting a retrieval-squad battle into C | Refuse. If the transport does not move or takes another road, no siege of the main passage is forced |
| The site officer dies or is absent | No joint-defense lines and no personal help. Judged separately from the validity of already-valid licenses. If change authority is needed with no lawful deputy, defer. No improvised new cast |
| Recovering the original fee / re-delivering the same outcome | Not recovered. The same outcome is only checked against the existing receipt; no new payment and no relation event |
| Conflicting outcomes of the same battle arriving late | Not merged. Recorded as a conflict with no state change |
| Changing license or dispute conditions from outside after combat begins | The battle inputs fixed at start are not silently edited. Handled in the next story judgment |

Heir generation was left to the separate rules of [the world ledger](../characters/Heirs-Names-and-World-Ledger.md). This event called no heir-generation system and moved no dead officer's memory into a new person.

#### Implementation Boundaries

The filter media and the copper were comparison-purpose fictional freight, not existing item IDs or economy balance. The two porters were also markers unrelated to canon cast or squad survival counts.

The tokens and processing order of the paper card were not the combat turns, squads, and card counts of the [RTFC contract](../../GDD/rules/Realtime-Formation-Card-Battle.md), and the courier and records keeper created no new combat cards. The settlement kept the one ResultId of the [round-trip contract](../../GDD/rules/Strategy-Battle-Roundtrip.md) and did not disguise a non-combat outcome as a combat one.

The trigger conditions connected to the location, time, noise, control, recent events, and party input of [Travel and Encounters](../../GDD/rules/Travel-and-Encounters.md), and the persistent states and ownership of the door, licenses, schedule, and dispute were preserved by this event.

Independently of the 334 stations of [the world-map structure](../places/World-Map-Construction.md), this event's place was the one fictional Sindorim junction. [The station-interior structure](../places/Station-Interior-Construction.md) stated that interior entry and facility slots did not yet exist.

Relation changes remained only with the few persons within direct sight and reporting range; documents alone did not automatically apply global reputation or alliance changes.
