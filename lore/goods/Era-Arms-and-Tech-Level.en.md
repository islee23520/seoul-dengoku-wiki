# The Arms and Technology of This Era

Post-collapse Seoul's equipment was examined in four layers: trade tools, police-issue gear, military remnants, and lost-tech salvage. What follows was a design proposal, not completed per-item research or completed game implementation. Livelihoods, military organization, and equipment proficiency were set separately.

The `guards`, `assault`, and `archers` of the core combat were not world design. They were a simulation scaffold.

## Hands After a Century

Only the revocation of 2026 and the opening of 2126 were canon as common-era years. No year between them was dated as an equipment chronology. The generation of 2126 had not lived the revocation; the tools they held were wreckage of 2026 statutes and factories, not that year's issue items.

The Firearms and Swords Act had not let guns stand as everyday tools. Even crossbows had required permits. Those clauses remained in the workshops of the time. There were no permits — only the habit of taboo. The K-series firearms were brought in only as the fact that they existed. Who held how many was not written. A recovered barrel was an event, not supply. When ammunition ran out, the gun became again the weight of a trade tool.

Factory robots had stood at world-first density, and humanoids had just begun field trials. When the call mesh died, the arms repeated the last transfer. The maintenance hands of the time dismantled those arms into pumps, traps, and watch stoves. The neural-net weights they could not mend; hoses, terminals, and lenses they could. That was lost tech. Physics was not forgotten.

Discarded: the 2036/2146 proposal, the 2042 (+6) opening, and the creation oral record's calendar that had dated the collapse's first 1–5 years as 2027–2031.

## How to Divide the Sources

Proper names, lines, chronology sentences, maps, uniforms, and arms of outside works were not carried into the current canon. Factual citations — statutes, the existence of issue equipment, manufacturers' public pages — and creative settings were not mixed into one layer.

| Layer | Where it is used | Where it is not |
|---|---|---|
| World canon | Livelihoods, relative chronology, the rule that ammunition is supply, the three lost-tech lines | Writing the discarded 2036/2146 proposal back as canon |
| 2026 fact | Gun control, the existence of the K-series, crossbows being permit items | Claiming guns were common after the collapse, or fixing 2026 issue counts and upgrade deployments |
| Reference logic | The kinds of scarcity; the economy where salvage becomes equipment, traps, conditional weapons | Reproducing original proper names; fixing bows as the basic arm |

- PlayStation's Horizon Forbidden West official introduction (viewed 2026-09-12): it writes that weapons, equipment, and traps are crafted from salvaged parts. What was taken was that economy. Proper names and tribal-bow defaults were not planted into the public copy. [PlayStation](https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/horizon-forbidden-west/)
- SNT Motiv defense page (viewed 2026-09-12): it supports the existence of the K-series firearm line. This page did not prove the numbers of 2026 issue counts or upgrade deployments. [SNT Motiv Defense](https://www.sntmotiv.com/eng/business/defense.html)

## What Actually Existed in 2026

Viewing date 2026-09-12. Not post-collapse fiction but the starting point of the near-future remnant. Stock counts and warehouse addresses were not written.

- Firearms and Swords Act, Article 1: it governed the manufacture, sale, lease, transport, possession, and use of guns, swords, explosives, spray devices, electroshock weapons, and crossbows. This was not a country where civilians carried guns as daily routine. Effective dates and clause detail followed the statute's original text. ([Korea Law Service Center](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSc.do?menuId=1&query=%EC%B4%9D%ED%8F%AC%C2%B7%EB%8F%84%EA%B2%80%C2%B7%ED%99%94%EC%95%BD%EB%A5%98), viewed 2026-09-12; earlier capture `.omo/ulw-research/20260911-200129/browse/01-law-go-kr-firearms-swords.png`)
- Public material introduces the K2 as the ROK Armed Forces' service rifle. Post-collapse possession through military remnants or market inflow was a separate creative assignment. Who holds how many in 2026 was not what this source said. ([English Wikipedia, Daewoo K2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo_Precision_Industries_K2), viewed 2026-09-12)
- A 2015 policy briefing stated that general reserve training issued M16s in place of carbines. The training issue fact of the time was confirmed, but 2026 counts, per-unit issue tables, and warehouse locations were not fixed by this material. ([Policy Briefing](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=148791733), viewed 2026-09-20)
- SNT Motiv's public defense page shows the existence of the K-series production line. The manufacturer's product descriptions did not stand in for actual issue counts to the armed forces or reserves. ([SNT Motiv](https://www.sntmotiv.com/eng/business/defense.html), viewed 2026-09-20)
- The canon preset already wrote the police-issue staff and patrol shield as "regulation staff · patrol shield." The original 2026 police equipment regulations and the 2026 issue range of service pistol models needed further research. The regulation numbers were not locked.

Improvised firearm recipes, ammunition manufacture, modification recipes, real armory addresses, and tactical manuals were not written in this document.

## The Four Layers

The unit that fought was the seven livelihoods of [persons, powers, and professions](../characters/Characters-Factions-and-Professions.md): maintenance hand, logistics dealer, medical officer, patrol, courier, explorer, records keeper. Tactical contribution was device disabling, formation holding, trap detection. Not arms-of-service.

| Layer | What the hand holds | Where it comes from | Ammunition · consumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade tools | Staff, crowbar, lantern, hand tools, rescue cutters | Station worksites, depots, substations, fire and facility remnants | None, or fuel and batteries |
| Police issue | Regulation staff, patrol shield, emergency kit, stab-resistant clothing | Autonomous patrols, station guards | Basic staff and shield take no ammunition. Medical items and clothing need replenishing |
| Military remnants | Service firearms including the K-series candidates, protective gear | Warlords, deserters, sealed armories — counts not locked | Goods-point stock. When short, sortie counts fall |
| Lost salvage | Capacitor, actuator, sensor modules; protocol cards. Conditionally equipment, traps, weapons | Disaster-robot dismantling | Not bullets. Charging, aging, supply-source limits. [Lost Technology](../technology/Lost-Technology-Lineage.md) |

Art silhouettes were already locked: explorer crowbar-lantern-antenna, medical officer red cloth, patrol shield-bearer. ([Character art](../../GDD/art/Character-Art-Direction.md)) Oddland and the left-right side-scroll were the current POC. The target battlefield was the 3D free-command camera of the 2026-09-19 adopted design default (not an owner's direct answer). The battlefield moved, rotated, and zoomed; the viewing angle was not locked. Pause allowed orders only in this party's closed battles; the story world and other parties did not stop.

Ammunition was a supply item tied to food. It cut only tactical equipment and sortie counts, not residents' labor. ([Economy and Production](../economy/Economy-and-Production.md); [Warfare and Sieges](../../GDD/rules/Warfare-and-Sieges.md))

Sweeping wild swarms away with firearms was a last resort. ([Hostile ecology index](../bestiary/Hostile-Ecology-Index.md))

Lost salvage was not a personal universal-weapon catalog. The default stayed modules, tools, and stopping; what Horizon lent was the economy of stopping or dismantling machines and then using the parts. Those parts could conditionally become equipment, traps, weapons. Hunting dinosaurs with bows was not the default picture. The remnant line's equipment and statutory starting point was Seoul 2026, and the generation of 2126 went on using it through tools, ledgers, and transmission without having lived that city.

The merchant preset's "merchant stock never fights" was a candidate long-term rule of that preset. It did not become a world rule "merchants cannot fight." Hiring escorts, improvised trade tools, and joining stronghold guards were not blocked.

The stationmaster preset's equipment assessment was "average," and no concrete items were written. No specific rifle was issued at start merely for holding stronghold authority.

The preset's "Sindorim autonomous council" and "Guro warlord" were not yet meshed with the state names of [the Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md). The Guro sphere was the Standards Alliance, and its military was the engineers and the barrier-gate combat teams. The deserter squad's issue weapons were the military-remnant layer. There was no need to hide model candidates; the issue counts simply stayed unlocked.

The "military" of the sixteen states was an organization name guarding facilities — the sluice guards, the engineers, the barrier-gate combat teams, the rail cavalry. ([The Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md)) Not guards, assault, or archers, and not a claim that organized armies were impossible. The remnants overlapped without adding to the sixteen slots. [Conscription Remnants and the Military Registers](../factions/Conscription-Remnants.md)

## What to Take from the References / What to Drop

The references were design comparisons, not the remnant line's factual record. The current canon was a setting independent of outside works' chronologies, states, persons, and maps.

| Axis | Take | Drop |
|---|---|---|

The 2026-09-07 formation-card combat record remained POC; cards were not made world law. ([Combat](../../GDD/rules/Realtime-Formation-Card-Battle.md))

## What Not to Put In

- Raising `guards` / `assault` / `archers` into world arms-of-service
- Fixing bows and crossbows as the default ranged arm (conditional candidates are in the table)
- Adding pistols to the existing drifter preset without grounds
- Asserting the calendar proposal as approved canon years
- Carrying original works' proper persons, weapons, or institutions into the narrative as if remnant-line creations. Work titles may be used in source attribution
- Manufacture and modification recipes, ammunition making, real armory addresses, tactical manuals
- Writing stock counts as canon

Below were universal bans no longer used, left over from an earlier version.

- "Bows never exist under any circumstances" — a conditional candidate
- "Merchants cannot fight" — only a candidate preset rule
- "Firearm model names cannot be written in ledgers" — the AU was an original-fiction reproduction issue; citing the K2 was the fact layer
- "Organized armies are impossible" — the sixteen states simply are not increased
- "Foreign army names cannot be written even as fact" — reproducing original-fiction uniforms and arms differs from geographic facts like the Yongsan garrison

## Not Yet Locked

### Game Estimates for Reserve and Military-Remnant Stock

The values below were not actual ROK warehouse counts or locations. What public material confirmed went only as far as the existence of the K-series, the separate legal framework of the reserve system, and the past M16 training issue. The game laid an `original-fiction estimate` over those facts.

- Military firearms usable in combat within Seoul were not common personal equipment; they opened only through military and conscription-remnant powers with surviving ledgers and through recovery events.
- The K2, K2C1, and M16 families were handled as candidate groups with differing storage states and parts problems. A shared model name guaranteed neither serviceability nor ammunition.
- Numbers were managed in three bands rather than single answers — `sealed stock`, `repairable`, `immediately usable` — and story seeds assigned counts deterministically within each band.
- Real addresses of stockpile sites were not written into canon. Warehouse-opening events were created from the ledgers of nameless mobilization stores, outposts, and depots, and did not mean the current state of real military facilities.
- Estimates were managed in story-balance material; this document fixed no exact firearm counts, ammunition box counts, or warehouse coordinates.
