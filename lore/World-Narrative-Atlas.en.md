## The Korean Prose Contract

The narrative body is now written in Korean plain declarative style from a third-person limited point of view. Guidance and schema notes use a short polite style. One paragraph moves one cause and one effect. A scene puts the place, the action, and the price first, and political interpretation after.

Names, affiliations, offices, relations, and agents of action are preserved unchanged. A change in a form of address is allowed only when a relation inside the scene causes it. Translationese, noun-listing administrative prose, same-rhythm triples, and interchangeable trauma summaries are avoided.

Multicultural and cross-border figures are drawn through migration, language, family, livelihood, and civic record. Ethnicity or nationality never predicts loyalty, violence, class, ability, or monstrosity.

Synthetic personae are seen only inside sensor uncertainty, energy and parts, custody responsibility, and broken memory. They are never written as omniscient narrators or endless power.

Fact, inference, and fiction are separated by `source_kind`. Institutions that exist today are not recorded as the subjects of fictional crime.
