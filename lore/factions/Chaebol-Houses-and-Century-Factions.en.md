# Chaebol Houses and the Century Factions

The sixteen states of Seoul in 2126 had begun in the duty rooms of the conglomerates and public offices of 2026. On the night of the blackout, the duty officers and executives who held the infrastructure control panels, the drawing files, and the sluice valves carried that authority across a hundred years, handing it down to their children and their disciples. The companies became states, and the duty officers became houses.

## Houses and the Division of the Keys

The head of each of the sixteen states stood as the foremost figure of his borough, yet the control of the infrastructure that actually moved the territory — the "key" — lay in the hands of the houses. The state did not lead the houses on a leash; only where a house granted it did the state's water flow and its electricity turn. Rarely did a single house hold a whole key alone. Opening the sluices required the stationmaster of the Water Compact and the Yeongdeungpo Sluice House to sign together, and controlling the standards needed the Standards Alliance chairman together with the opening of the Guro Alloy House's drawing files.

The governance structures of the former companies and the emergency powers of the former institutions hardened over the years into house bloodlines and hereditary succession. The trade names of the great conglomerates lived on as the names of states, and the ledgers of the old companies and government offices turned into the licenses and duty chests that proved a house's power.

## The Remnants of the Old Government

The "Republic of Korea Government" that kept its thread alive around Gwanghwamun Station was not the intact state order of 2026. On the night of the motive-power revocation, with the lines cut and the networks dead, what remained were accreditation rights and seals transcribed on paper, and the conscription registers. Water, food, and large-scale repairs all had to be borrowed from the other great powers and the houses, and the government fell to the rank of a lesser state.

The president of this place was not chosen by election; the one who had kept the original box of the Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House unbroken succeeded to the office. The Seoul Station Record-Law House was only the guild that managed this old government's records, not the state itself. The memory of the old army likewise splintered into living-sphere militias and scattered among the conscription remnants.

## External Powers and the Theaters

In the seams between the sixteen states and beyond them stood five external theaters that pressed on Seoul's control. They took no formal state slot, but they squeezed the life of Seoul with quarantine, grain, the strait, the far east, and the ocean trust.

The Imjin River Gate Theater held the North's quarantine and the return registers, and the West Sea Granary Theater gripped the grain queues at the river-mouth berths. The Korea Strait Theater, the Duman River Far East Theater, and the Incheon Trust Theater likewise throttled the breathing of the sixteen states each through logistics, smuggling, and the communications trust, knotting the power map of 2126 tighter still.
