# Conscription Remnants and the Military Registers

Korea's conscription system was taken as the starting point of the remnant military organizations and of service experience. Conscription duty, active service, reserve formation, and equipment holdings were not read as one overlapping register; they read as separate strata. A person's form and period of service remained in verified records or in written biography, and were never decided by gender or name alone. There were also those who enlisted anew after the collapse.

These four organizations were creative proposals operating in overlap with the existing [Sixteen States of Seoul](../factions/Sixteen-States.md). They were not a plan adding four independent states. Units that kept their command systems, units tied into local self-rule, and groups that refused orders could coexist in the same sphere. Troop and ammunition counts remained undecided.

## The Relation to the Calendar

The common era from the revocation of 2026 to the opening of 2126 belonged to [the Hundred-Year Annals of Seoul Dengoku](../chronology/Century-Annals.md). The reserve-formation regulations of the present were not carried over as the institutions of 2126. The military registers of the 2126 generation were not read as units from just after the revocation; they remained as transcribed copies of remnant organizations that had passed through the creation oral record and the empty century.

## How the Collapse Cut the Units

Even where a unit's name survived, when the communications, the feeding, the maintenance, and the ammunition supply that carried orders broke, it could no longer move in the same way. The fault lines of a remnant unit did not seem to be settled by who knew the service numbers alone. Commanders held the call-up authority, maintenance hands held the judgment on whether equipment ran, and residents held the ration and passage conditions, and they negotiated with one another.

| Surviving organization · record | Function maintained | Object of conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Mobilization registers and command systems | Affiliation and contact nets, order transmission | Whether to keep following old orders or accept local self-rule's control |
| Weapons, supply, and maintenance handlers | Equipment lending, stock management, readiness judgment | To whom to issue, and which mission to support first |
| Deserters and call-up refusers | Survival with family and comrades outside the registers | Whether to treat voluntary departure and lost communications as the same desertion |
| Emergency committees, markets, water-supply organizations | Rations, housing, passage, civilian delegates' approval | What burden to demand of residents in exchange for military support |

The initial owning organ of military equipment and its post-collapse owner could differ. Recovery, trade, and new issue were also paths. The [arms-candidate table](../goods/Era-Arms-and-Tech-Level.md) carried public model names such as the K2 with their grounds, but no estimate of actual stock or of any specific depot's location went in.

This table was not to be read as material describing the 2026 stationing or movement of real units. Yongsan's past garrisoning and its return status, and the fictional fences, depots, and watch organizations, had to be recorded as distinct layers. Factual citation of real military names and the post-opening fictional organizations were not to be mixed into one stratum.

The deserter-squad preset's "good but conspicuous regulation issue" connected to the setting that the old affiliation was identifiable from issued gear. No rule was kept under which stealing a register alone opened every armory.

## Who Carried a Gun

| Opening-moment person | Record to verify | Initial plan and later approach |
|---|---|---|
| The drifter | Service experience, possession of recovered goods, trading partners | The existing preset is a staff and worn clothes. Not a condition permanently limiting armament afterward |
| The former patrol officer | Police career and military service verified separately | The existing preset is a staff and shield. Firearms assigned when issue, purchase, or recovery paths and proficiency are verified |
| The deserter squad | Form of service, moment of departure, issued gear brought along | Candidates for issue weapons and protective gear. Remaining ammunition and maintenance support set separately |
| The stationmaster | Personal career distinguished from the stronghold's authority | Commanding a stronghold's guard did not automatically confer ownership of or proficiency with any particular rifle |
| The merchant | Presence or absence of combat, escort, or service experience | Cart and scales are the default, but a personal combat history may exist |

The initial equipment in the table above connected to the proposal in [Starting Presets](../characters/Starting-Presets.md). The rule of a specific companion who avoided combat was not applied to all merchants. A person could own equipment without knowing its use, and even a skilled handler could hardly operate it without ammunition or power.

## What Not to Put In

- Fixing that a military force of a particular size controlled all of Seoul without order-of-battle or supply grounds
- Inferring a real person's service from name or gender, or marking fictional troop counts as measured figures
- Bringing Core test roles, or the uniforms and insignia of the source fiction, in as fixed worldview settings
- Rules blanket-banning organized armies, firearm model names, bows, or merchants' combat

## Not Yet Locked

Each organization's troop counts, officer-to-maintenance ratios, held equipment, and ordnance supply lines were to be settled together with item-by-item research. The base material on conscription and reserve institutions, the records of Yongsan's return and relocation, and the individual biographies were to be checked against one another then. That this document existed did not mean the organization and equipment selection of the military powers was finished.
