## Large-billed Crows in the Winter

The large-billed crow is the commonest crow in Seoul. It has a heavy bill, a domed forehead, and a sharp mind. In a city thinned out since the Great Blackout, rooftops, pylons, and the steel frames of Han River bridges have become good roosts for crows. In winter, with food short, hundreds gather at a single roost, and by day they fly to station exits, grain stores, and food-waste yards where people put out food. There is no special leader and no individual that commands signals.

Crows imitate and remember sounds well. They learn repeated sounds such as horns, inspection bells, and the start-up hum of transformers as signals of food or danger, and spread them through the flock. This is the same as real crow ecology. They cannot read human radio or communication content.

The danger comes in three forms: tearing open grain sacks and food-waste yards to steal food, pecking at wire insulation and ceramic insulators and soiling them with droppings, causing short circuits and communication outages, and avian influenza and droppings-borne pathogens carried by crows that contaminate water and grain. In the breeding season of spring they dive at people passing near nests, but in winter attacks on people are rare.

## Ecology

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Winter movement | At the end of autumn, scattered families gather at large communal roosts. The factory pylons of Daerim and Garibong, the roofs along the Yeongdeungpo line, the overhead-wire crossings at Wangsimni, and the steel frames of Han River bridges serve as roosts. In the morning they scatter to feeding grounds, and at dusk they return. |
| Food | They eat insects, earthworms, grain, food scraps, and the carcasses of small animals. In winter, food people have thrown away, gaps in grain stores, and market leftovers make up a large share. When nesting they take thin wire stripped of insulation and fabric scraps. |
| Breeding and life cycle | In spring a pair holds a single pylon and builds a nest. In winter they do not breed, and several families stay together at a communal roost. If one of a pair vanishes, a neighboring pair tests the empty spot, and if a nest is torn down by force, young birds set up a new roost nearby. |
| Senses | By sight they read people’s helmets, poles, and flashlight reflections, and by hearing they sort the order of horns and machine sounds. At night their eyes are poor and they hardly fly, but they gather around bright exits and floodlights. They cannot read the content of radio signals. |
| Flock behavior | A communal roost has no commander. When one bird raises an alarm, those around it fly up. Alarm calls and dives are done together, and when one finds food, dozens soon gather. |
| Disease and danger | Crows carry avian influenza, and their droppings contaminate water and grain. When droppings build up on insulators, damp causes short circuits and cuts communication and lighting. Pecked insulation and torn sacks become a way in for rats. |
| Response principles | Close the gaps in grain stores and collection yards, replace wire insulation on time, and clear droppings under roosts. If a sound is changed, false alarms rise for a few days and the birds soon learn the new one, so keep using the same alarm. Chasing them with gunfire only moves the roost to the next tower. |

### G02E01 · Breeding pair

- Size and place: They nest on pylons and bridge steel frames.
- Winter behavior: In spring they hold one tower and share carrying food. In winter they return to the communal roost.
- Danger: In the breeding season they dive at people passing near the nest. This is rare in winter.
- Response: In spring, go around the passage under the nest. If it is torn down, a new roost appears beside it.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0034"></a>
<a id="g02e02"></a>

### G02E02 · Communal roost flock

- Size and place: Daerim and Garibong pylons, Wangsimni overhead wires, Han River bridge steel frames.
- Winter behavior: By day they scatter to feeding grounds and return at dusk. They learn sounds from one another.
- Danger: They tear grain sacks and peck wire insulation, and droppings build up under the roost.
- Response: Close the store gaps and clean under the roost. If driven off, they move to the next tower.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0264"></a>
<a id="g02e12"></a>

### G02E12 · Fledged young

- Size and place: They leave their parents and try out new roosts on nearby school paths and rooftops.
- Winter behavior: At the edge of the flock they copy the sounds of the adults.
- Danger: Inexperienced, many die in collisions with people and vehicles.
- Response: Clear food off the roads to reduce accidents.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0287"></a>
<a id="g02e13"></a>

### G02E13 · Old bird

- Size and place: A low branch at the edge of the communal roost.
- Winter behavior: It responds only to familiar sounds and is slow to react to unfamiliar alarms.
- Danger: Having followed long-learned sounds, it takes off late for a new alarm.
- Response: When changing an alarm, first play the same sound alongside it for a few days.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0218"></a>
<a id="g02e10"></a>

### G02E10 · Daerim and Garibong pylon flock

- Size and place: Factory pylons in Daerim and Garibong in Yeongdeungpo-gu, and in Guro. This is Free City land.
- Winter behavior: They sleep on the Yeongdeungpo-line roofs and pylons, and by day circle the factory sites and market alleys.
- Danger: Insulators covered with droppings short out and cut the workshops’ electricity in the Free City.
- Response: Regularly clear droppings under the pylons and replace insulation so it does not become nesting material.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0241"></a>
<a id="g02e11"></a>

### G02E11 · Han River bridge roost flock

- Size and place: Steel frames of Han River bridges. Whose land it is depends on the districts the bridge connects.
- Winter behavior: They sleep in inspection openings and steel gaps. Each morning they pick up food dropped by vehicles on the bridge.
- Danger: Droppings on the bridge are slippery, and people and carts fall. Dives sometimes stop traffic.
- Response: Close bridge inspection openings and clear food scraps to make the roost less usable.

## Habitat and Winter Response

A large-billed crow’s territory is set by its roosts. In the west the factory pylons of Daerim, Garibong, and Guro in Yeongdeungpo-gu (Free City land), and in the east the overhead-wire crossings at Wangsimni in Seongdong-gu (Government land), are the large roosts. The steel frames of Han River bridges belong to whichever faction holds the districts the bridge reaches. As winter deepens they move for a time along the river in search of insects and fish carcasses on the western Han River bank.

The Free City has many technicians who repair workshops and electricity by hand and can replace insulation themselves, but there are too few people to tend every pylon. The Government spends labor on inspecting the overhead wires near Wangsimni, but with no doctors, someone who falls from a pylon gets first aid only. The people working at height carry the greatest risk.

Crows do not slip through shutter gaps, but they gather in front of open exits and vents and target food-waste yards and grain stores. People who live deep underground can seal themselves off with shutters. People who live on the surface must guard the grain sacks under their roofs and eaves themselves.

Good response means blocking the food and changing the roost. Keep grain and food scraps in lidded containers and replace wire insulation on time to remove nesting material. Do not change the sounds; keep using the same alarm. Results are judged not by how many were driven off but by short-circuit counts, grain lost, and time communication was down.

## Linked scenarios

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0446"></a>

### G02-SC1 · Three Horns

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: On a winter morning at the overhead-wire crossing in Wangsimni, when a maintenance vehicle sounds its horn three times, the crows at the communal roost make the same sound.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Seongdong, overhead-wire maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Crows learn a repeated sound as a signal that food is coming and spread it. When the sound is heard they gather in front of the grain store.
- Choices: Change the horn sound / Use flags instead of sound / Move the grain store
- Outcomes: Changing the sound raises false alarms for a few days, and the birds soon learn the new one. Flags spread less among the birds. Moving the store takes labor.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to reduce first the safety of the workers or the cost of driving the birds off.
- Source dossier: G02

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0448"></a>

### G02-SC2 · The False Blackout Alarm

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: After crows peck at insulators and droppings pile up, the electricity in the Daerim workshops cuts out and a blackout alarm sounds.
- Actors: Free City stationmaster in Daerim, workshop technicians, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Damp and droppings cause a short circuit and the alarm sounds, and the crows learn that sound as a signal of food.
- Choices: Replace and clean the insulators first / Switch off the alarm sound for a while / Put an off-limits cord around the pylon
- Outcomes: Replacing the insulators restores electricity but takes labor. Switching the alarm off risks missing a real blackout. The off-limits cord does not stop the birds.
- Moral cost: Keeping the workshops powered collides with the safety of the people given the dangerous pylon work.
- Source dossier: G02

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0450"></a>

### G02-SC3 · A New Signal on the Bridge

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: After a large roost forms in the inspection openings on a Han River bridge, traffic on the bridge stops every dawn.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the districts the bridge reaches, porters and cart-pullers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Gaps in the bridge steel become a sleeping place out of snow and wind, so crows gather, and food dropped by vehicles draws the birds each morning.
- Choices: Close the inspection openings / Shift the passage hours / Clear the food off the bridge
- Outcomes: Closing the openings shrinks the roost but adds inspection labor. Shifting the hours leaves porters standing longer in the cold. Clearing the food moves the birds to the next bridge.
- Moral cost: Decide who bears the bridge inspection labor and the burden on the next bridge the birds move to.
- Source dossier: G02
