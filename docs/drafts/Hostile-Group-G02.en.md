## Common species and variants

At the factory pylons of Daerim and Garibong, the roofs of the Yeongdeungpo Line Gauge, and the overhead-wire junctions of the Wangsimni Union, breeding pairs of large-billed crows and non-breeding communal roosts overlapped. They fed on night insects and leftover market protein, and stripped fine wire and fibers went into their nests. They learned horns, inspection bells, and transformer start-up sounds as food or danger, but they did not read the content of the radio waves.

The base form of the Large-billed Crow is a wild bird that moves by the sound shapes it has learned. The Residual-Tower Cross-Iron Tone and the bridge inspection-hatch cylinder are not birds but place mechanisms made by repeated wind and lamps, and the Simultaneous Circle is a temporary phenomenon in which several families gather in one circle. The Shore-Feather Perch is a regional branch of the same species tuned to the sea wind and the breakwater bell, not a different being that deciphers transmissions.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0014"></a>

### Role and Body

The breeding pair at the top of the Sangam tower gathered sparks in their metal feathers and held a horn imitation in their whole bodies. At dusk, when that imitation spread over the factory roofs, the dark bodies of the communal roost leaned in one direction and the flock swarmed over the work basket.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0016"></a>

### Origin

Even before G02-SC1, factory waste heat and catenary sparks drew insects, and a pair claimed the tower. After they memorized three horn blasts as the promise of food, the whole roost began to divide the evening sky over Seoul Station by that pattern.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0018"></a>

### Habitat

They clung by day to the roofs of the Gauge on the Yeongdeungpo Line and the tops of disused railway towers, and flew far only at night. Outside the breeding season the roost shared only call patterns without a leader, so this pair's pattern became the temporary clock of the communal roost.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0020"></a>

### Needs and Economy

Insects, small carcasses, and the heat left by sparks were the pair's fare. When the pattern weakened, the flock could not cover the basket and daytime roof work became easier, but insects stayed in the inspection openings instead and drew the next sparks.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0022"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

They changed their metal feathers every season and filled the gaps with fine wire their subordinates carried in. When the filling was clumsy, the imitation split and the roost divided into two beats.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0024"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

In the breeding season the pair divided nest defense and food hauling between them, and young helpers joined the dives. The nonbreeding roost had no leader, so this pair's horn imitation alone became the center of repeated learning.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0026"></a>

### Escalating Threat

When the horn imitation mixed with the inspection bell, the workers' helmets were the first to lose direction. That same evening the flock came down on the basket and scattered the tools, and after the third horn blast the sky over the bridge was bound into a single call pattern, tangling the basket and the schoolchildren's umbrellas in the same wind.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0028"></a>

### Combat and Response

Fitting a reverse-phase noise generator to the base of the tower threw the horn imitation gathered by the ruling pair one beat off. On that missed beat the flock tore into two branches in midair. If the generator came later than the third horn blast, the work basket was covered with claws all the same, and the day shift put off the roof work by a day.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0030"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Whether to publish the three-blast pattern in the work log or keep it as a call known only to the roof watch was borne by the day-shift foreman. A published pattern was memorized by the communal roost overnight and could not be taken back, and if it was hidden, a new shift worker called the expectation of food down onto the same basket. G02-SC1 also recorded the price of the human safety call going empty for several days while that pattern was retired.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G02-heading-0032"></a>

### Scenario Links

The three horn blasts of G02-SC1 became, through this pair's bodies, the clock of the whole roost. The misclassification of G02-SC2 and the convergence at the bridge in G02-SC3 left behind the nights when that clock overlapped with human calls.

Management results were read separately as transmission uptime, dives per thousand work hours, the share of alarms cancelled after visual confirmation, the amount of insulator fouling, the number of fledged young per nest, and the reuse rate of alternative roosts.

When a short circuit or large-scale nest removal caused a failure, the old sound patterns were halted for three days and the work zone closed with visual markers alone, and then the insect lamps and food exposure resumed first at the alternative roosts.

After that, the new inspection bell was not spread all at once but played pylon by pylon at staggered times, and main-line work was restored once the mimicry at the communal roost had settled.

Recovery was judged not by whether the crows had gone but by whether they spread out to the designated roosts instead of swarming at alarm sounds, and whether residents could tell a real breakdown from a bird reaction.

When fixed silent hours and maintenance timetables held for a long time, market haulers and unauthorized scavengers too came to watch the crows' movements and guess the moments when the equipment stood empty.

So to stop the side effect of a predictable signal system lowering bird clashes while becoming a clock for human intrusion, the published safety signals had to be kept while the actual inspection order and material routes were run irregularly.

### G02-SC1 · Three Horns

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: The new horn order of the Yeongdeungpo Line work crew overlaps the food-drop signal, and the crows gather on the work basket.
- Actors: free-city, HC02, H02
- Ecological mechanism: A sound shape learned at the communal roost triggers the expectation of food before it triggers a warning.
- Choices: Abolish the horn order / Train a separate food signal / Move the food drop to a hornless night
- Outcomes: Abolishing it slows the work-safety procedure, and retraining accepts several days of false alarms. The night move protects the daytime school route but shifts the burden onto the night workers.
- Moral cost: One of the two has to change: the safety signal familiar to people, or the survival signal the birds have learned.

### G02-SC2 · The False Blackout Alarm

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: H02 classified the repeated dive calls as a precursor of a blackout, but the real cause is the night insect lamp.

- Moral cost: Discarding an inaccurate ecological alarm reduces malfunctions, but the cheap disaster watch disappears too.

### G02-SC3 · A New Signal on the Bridge

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: The XT02 sea wind overlaps the breeding season, and the air above the bridge and the transmission inspection hatch becomes a communal roost.
- Actors: free-city, government, XT02, HC02
