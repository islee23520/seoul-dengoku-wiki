<a id="gate"></a>

Sindorim is the bottleneck between Yeongdeungpo and Guro. Loads bound from one to the other pass through a junction there.

<a id="junction"></a>

## The junction

The junction runs from an outer waiting point through the main cargo gate to an inner assembly point. A separate maintenance passage beside it admits only people and back loads.

A load of filter media can be split across several backs, so it can go through the maintenance passage.

A load of copper parts cannot be broken down or carried without equipment. It moves only through the main cargo gate.

<a id="request"></a>

## The day's request

The party finished a transport job taken in Yeongdeungpo at Guro and received the agreed pay. On the way back through Sindorim, the transport chief offers a separate haul.

Two porters are to move one load of filter media and one load of copper parts. The work crew at the site is waiting for the filter media.

An armed salvage squad claims the copper and has announced that it will arrive at 15:00. Whether the claim is valid has not yet been confirmed.

The extra pay is split into a share for delivering the filter media and a share for delivering the copper. If the copper is not delivered, its share is not paid.

The pay for the finished job stays with the party. Turning down the extra haul does not take it back.

<a id="passage"></a>

## Conditions of passage

- Passage rights are issued separately from any repair to the gate. The permits of the party and of the designated convoy are checked one by one.
- The copper can be kept under seal until its ownership is confirmed. A sealed-storage agreement carries a written condition that the salvage squad will not object to the movement of the people and the filter media.
- A sealed-storage agreement takes effect once the signer's authority and the document's authenticity are confirmed.
- If the haul is postponed, the loads stay in the confirmed outer warehouse. The transport chief arranges the permit and escort again with the next shift.
- The salvage squad comes at the same hour with the same intent whatever state the junction gate is in.

## Schedule

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| Day one 13:30 | The offer and the public timetable are read. |
| Day one 14:00 | Today's haul departs. Permit, route and participation are settled before then. |
| Day one 14:20 | One run through the main passage is complete. |
| Day one 14:40 | People and filter media sent through the maintenance passage arrive. |
| Day one 15:00 | The salvage squad arrives. From then on, delivery of the copper is settled only with a valid sealed-storage agreement or a separate settlement. |
| Day two 09:00 | The general counter issues new permits. Any damage settlement for a broken gate remains a separate matter. |
| Day two 10:00 | The next haul departs. Delivery is at 10:20 through the main passage and at 10:40 through the maintenance passage. |

<a id="crossings"></a>

## Three earlier crossings

The way the party first got through the junction decides the state of the gate and the permits on the day it returns.

<a id="repair"></a>

### Repair and agreement

The party paid for the repair when it set out and fixed the control mechanism. The gate can therefore still be opened and closed.

The main-passage permits of both the party and the designated convoy are valid. The transport chief can agree with the party on when to open and close the gate.

<a id="forced"></a>

### Forced breach

The party broke the gate to get through. The broken gate stays open and cannot be closed.

The party got through, but the designated convoy has no permit. The transport chief will not send the loads until a permit is confirmed.

The damage dispute with the site remains open even after a new permit is issued.

<a id="detour"></a>

### Maintenance-passage detour

The party found the maintenance passage and went through it. The main cargo gate is still broken and shut.

Light passage through the maintenance passage is permitted to the party alone. The convoy needs a new permit to use it.

After a forced breach or a detour, the designated convoy has to obtain a new permit at the general counter. The counter issues it at 09:00 the next day, after today's departure.

<a id="plans"></a>

## Plans and their costs

| Earlier crossing | Plan | Cost shown first | Outcome without a fight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repair and agreement | Use the permit and the salvage squad's arrival time to move the haul before the squad arrives. The order of opening and closing the gate goes into the crossing order and the retreat plan. | To make today's departure, the party gives up its other plans for the day. | With time and permit confirmed, both people and both loads are delivered and every extra promise is kept. If a condition fails, the party chooses again among postponing, negotiating and fighting. |
| Forced breach | Confirm the haul permit first and keep the copper under seal to set the dispute aside. Closing the gate is not available. | Through the general counter the haul moves the next day. The copper share of the extra pay is not yet earned, and the damage settlement remains. | Once an authorised agreement stands, both people and the filter media are delivered and the copper is held in the warehouse. Without a permit the convoy does not move. |
| Maintenance-passage detour | Extend the maintenance-passage permit to the convoy and split the filter media across backs. | Through the general counter the haul moves the next day. The party helps carry, and the copper load stays in the warehouse. | After the permit is confirmed, both people and the filter media are delivered. The big gate and bulk transport remain unsolved, and no fight takes place in the main passage. |
| Any of the three | Agree with an authorised party on storing the copper and on a later review. | The copper share of the extra pay is held back, and a later recipient must be named. | The agreement leaves gate, permit and carrying capacity as they were. People and filter media move once each case's passage conditions are met. |
| Any of the three | Postpone today's haul or turn down the extra job. | There is no delivery record and no extra pay this time. | The earlier job stays complete. Both people and both loads wait in the warehouse, and the transport chief rearranges the schedule. |

If the party chooses to fight, each earlier crossing poses a different question. After a repair it must decide why the gate stays open, after a breach what controls an entrance that cannot close, and after a detour whether there is any reason to fight in the main passage.

Victory, casualties and the movement of cargo in a fight are settled by the fight itself.

<a id="companions"></a>

## Messenger and archivist

After a breach or a detour, the transport chief's file may already hold a mandate. The mandate names the designated convoy, the passage to use and the hours of both days.

| Companion | What can be confirmed | How the plan changes |
|---|---|---|
| Messenger | Confirms the salvage squad's arrival window and responsible counter through a notice delivered by hand. | The party weighs moving today within the confirmed time. Issuing permits remains the counter's work. |
| Archivist | Spends ten minutes checking whether the mandate in the file covers this convoy, passage and time. | With the check done at 13:40, today's departure can be met. The broken gate, the copper dispute and the passage's cargo limit stay as they are. |
| Neither | Uses the public timetable and the general counter. | The party postpones to the next day to obtain a permit, or turns down the extra job. |

Without a mandate in the file, the permit conditions are the same whoever travels with the party.

<a id="record"></a>

## What the outcome records

| Field | What it records |
|---|---|
| People | Delivered, or waiting in the warehouse. If there is a fight, capture, injury and death follow from its result. |
| Cargo | Whether the filter media was delivered, whether the copper was delivered or held under seal, the storage place and who may receive it. |
| Pay for the earlier job | Already paid. The outcome of the extra job does not take it back. |
| Extra promises | The filter-media share, the copper share, the promised haul time and when it was actually kept. |
| Facilities and rights | Whether the gate works or is broken, who a permit covers for which passage and time, and any damage dispute left open. |
| Direct relations | Who saw what and whose promise was kept. The outcome stays with those people and does not become an alliance between states or a grudge across the city. |

<a id="revisit"></a>

## Returning later

If everything was delivered after a repair, the work crew is using the new filter media and pays the promised fee.

If the copper was held back after a breach, the transport chief thanks the party for bringing the people back and asks to meet again when the seal is lifted.

After a detour, the maintenance passage has actually been used, and repairing the big cargo gate is still the next job.

If the extra pay was handed over at the first delivery, the return visit only confirms that it was received.

If the party turned the extra job down, the transport chief only tells it when the loads left in the warehouse will move next.

<a id="absence"></a>

## When the site chief is gone

If the site chief dies or is away, the promise to defend the gate together lapses with them. Permits already issued stay in force.

If a permit has to be changed and there is no lawful deputy, the haul is postponed.
