## Boars in the Winter

Wild boars are ordinary wild animals that live on the slopes of Bukhansan to the north, on the foothills of Gwanaksan and Umyeonsan to the south, and in the reed beds of Nanji on the Han River. Since the Great Blackout people have withdrawn from the surface, and the line between mountain and riverbank has faded. As the winter drags on there is nothing to eat in the hills, so boars come down toward the stations where people live. There are no mutated individuals and no leader that commands the herd.

The center of a herd is an older female (sow) who walks with her young. The sow decides when to move, and the younger animals go ahead to check the feeding ground and the way out. Adult males travel alone, use the same feeding grounds, and do not follow the herd. This is the same as real wild boar ecology.

The danger comes in three forms: digging into food stores and food-waste collection yards, meeting people on empty night roads and outdoor stairs outside the stations, and diseases and ticks carried by boars passing to people and livestock.

## Ecology

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Winter movement | When snow piles up, the acorns and roots in the hills are buried. Herds from the slopes of Bukhansan come down along Jeongneung, Pyeongchang-dong, and the Bulgwangcheon stream, herds from Gwanaksan come down along the road toward Namtaeryeong, and the herd in the Nanji reed beds enters the surface settlements of Mapo along the riverbank. |
| Food | They eat acorns, roots, earthworms, insects, and the carcasses of small animals. In winter they also seek out food scraps and grain crumbs that people have put out, and the salt in heaps of calcium chloride stored for de-icing. |
| Breeding and life cycle | Females give birth in spring, but in winters of scarce food breeding falls and the herds range over long distances. More than half of the young do not survive their first winter. A family group splits only when its young survive two seasons in a row. |
| Senses | Smell is sharpest. They tell food, people, and disinfectant apart from far away. Eyesight is weak, and they are sensitive to footsteps and vehicle vibration carried through the ground. They cannot read signals from the machines people use. |
| Herd behavior | The sow leads her young and the younger animals, and the half-grown animals scout ahead. If the sow dies or disappears, the family group circles the same place for a few days and then scatters. Males move alone. |
| Disease and danger | Boars spread African swine fever. It does not pass to people, but a place that raises pigs can lose all of them at once. Eating the raw meat brings parasites and brucellosis, and ticks pass fevers to people. Wounds from tusks and charges run deep, and on the side with no doctors they become serious injuries. |
| Response principles | Shooting scatters the herd into every alley and makes injuries to people more likely. Seal the food firmly, steer the food trail to one place, and split the hours when people move at night. Results are judged not by how many were killed but by how many times they reached a store and how many people were hurt. |

### G01E01 · Sow herd

- Size and place: They come down from the slopes of Bukhansan, the foothills of Gwanaksan, and the Nanji reed beds.
- Winter behavior: The sow decides when to move, and the young follow. When snow falls they circle food-waste yards and grain stores.
- Danger: They dig under store walls and tear open grain sacks. If the young are touched, the sow charges at once.
- Response: Seal the food and never stand between the sow and her young. If only the sow is taken, the young keep returning to the same place for days.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0034"></a>
<a id="g01e02"></a>

### G01E02 · Subadult scouts

- Size and place: They check the feeding grounds and escape routes ahead of the family group.
- Winter behavior: At night they sniff around station exits and outdoor stairs, then return and bring the family group.
- Danger: If they meet a person in the dark they panic and ram.
- Response: Keep exit lights on and leave no food smell under the stairs. If the scouts are driven off, the main herd comes by another route.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0264"></a>
<a id="g01e12"></a>

### G01E12 · Young

- Size and place: They stay beside the sow and sleep in brush and hollows.
- Winter behavior: Many die in the first month of winter. Those that survive quickly grow used to the food people put out.
- Danger: Animals that have grown used to coming near people become bolder as they grow.
- Response: Do not feed the young. If they are fed, the same animals come closer the next winter.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0287"></a>
<a id="g01e13"></a>

### G01E13 · Old adult

- Size and place: It lives apart at the edge of the herd.
- Winter behavior: Its teeth are worn and it cannot eat hard roots, so it comes close to the stations looking for food people have thrown away.
- Danger: A starving, wounded animal sometimes stands its ground in front of people instead of running.
- Response: Do not approach, and leave a path open. Watch from a distance, and shut the gate after it passes.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0333"></a>
<a id="g01e15"></a>

### G01E15 · Lone male

- Size and place: It lives alone, apart from the family group.
- Winter behavior: It uses the same feeding grounds but does not follow the sow’s movements. If it meets a person or a cart on the road it does not give way.
- Danger: Its tusks are long and it is strong, so ramming a cart or a person causes severe injury.
- Response: Do not walk alone at night. Warn it off at passages with light and noise.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0356"></a>
<a id="g01e16"></a>

### G01E16 · Bukhansan slope population

- Size and place: It lives on the slopes of Bukhansan and in the surface villages below the ridge. The land is Government land in Seongbuk, Gangbuk, and Eunpyeong.
- Winter behavior: When snow falls it comes down from the ridge and passes in front of the surface houses of Jeongneung and Pyeongchang-dong. When the snow melts it climbs back up.
- Danger: At night it digs up surface houses and vegetable plots and blocks people on the forest paths. Surface villagers meet it first.
- Response: Open the ridge path only by day, and fence the vegetable plots to change the food trail.

## Habitat and Winter Response

A boar’s territory is set not by lines on a map but by the depth of snow and the smell of food. In the north they come down from the slopes of Bukhansan along Jeongneung, Pyeongchang-dong, and the Bulgwangcheon stream. In the west, from Nanji on the Han River, they follow reed beds and the riverbank levee toward the surface villages of Mapo. In the south they come down from the foothills of Gwanaksan and Umyeonsan along the Namtaeryeong road toward Gwanak and Seocho.

On Government land in the north (Seongbuk, Gangbuk, Eunpyeong, Mapo), surface villages and vegetable plots suffer first. The Government has no doctors, so the White Cross can give only first aid, and anyone badly torn by tusks does not come back. On the Free City side in Gwanak there are doctors who can treat wounds, but people carry the cost of feeding. On Arc land in Seocho the Umyeonsan herd comes around station entrances and grain stores.

Boars enter the stations by three routes: open entrances, gaps where a shutter does not fully close, and vents. The deep underground levels can be sealed with shutters and are safe, but people who live on the ground floor and above must hold out behind a single wall or fence. This is why the higher people live, the greater the danger.

The best response is to change the food trail. Close the food-waste yards and grain stores tightly, and open a detour with food placed deliberately where nobody is, to lead the herd that way. Shooting scatters the family group into the alleys and hurts more people. Young that have lost their sow search the same place over and over for days, so night patrols should rest in the meantime.

Results are not measured by the number killed. Look at how many times they reached a store, how long a road was blocked, how many people were hurt, and how much vegetable plot was dug up. If intrusions fall but traces of the young vanish too, that is not stability but a sign that hunger and capture pressure have grown.

## Linked scenarios

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0443"></a>

### G01-SC1 · The First Hoof Outside the Embankment

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: The day after the first snow settles, the prints of a sow and her young appear in front of a grain store outside the embankment on the Nanji riverbank of the Han River.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Mapo, White Cross, surface villagers
- Ecological mechanism: When food is buried under the snow, the sow leads the family group to the places where people put out food waste.
- Choices: Delay the collection yard’s night operation / Open a reed-bed detour with a scent lure / Capture only the sow
- Outcomes: Delaying operations delays the ration too. A detour creates a long-term trail. Taking the sow leaves the young circling the same place for days.
- Moral cost: The people’s ration and the survival of a family group with young collide on the same timetable.
- Source dossier: G01

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0445"></a>

### G01-SC2 · The Price of the Salt Pool

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: When heaps of calcium chloride for de-icing are piled in front of a station, the herd moves its feeding ground to the semi-basement alleys and surface villages in search of salt.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free Cities, ordinary citizens, road-clearing workers
- Ecological mechanism: The sow that knows where the salt is leads the family group back to the same spot. Even if the heap is removed the herd does not vanish; it is pushed into a more dangerous village.
- Choices: Leave a deliberately placed salt pool in one spot / Prioritize recovering alley scraps and salt / Lower the density with a hunting permit
- Outcomes: The pool fixes the cost of coexistence. Prioritizing recovery takes labor away from other rations. Hunting makes the remaining animals better at avoiding people.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to keep the danger in a safe place and manage it, or to pass it on to a vulnerable alley.
- Source dossier: G01

<a id="Hostile-Group-G01-heading-0447"></a>

### G01-SC3 · Right of Passage on the Flood Line

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: A column of porters from outside Seoul passes along the flood line on the Han River levee, and the Bukhansan herd comes down the same road.
- Actors: Government stationmaster, porters and refugees from outside Seoul, White Cross
- Ecological mechanism: In the snow the only dry ground is the road on the levee, so people and boars use the same road. Food crumbs dropped by the porters draw the herd in.
- Choices: Divide the passage hours / Turn the food convoy onto a bait trail toward the reed beds / Close the levee road for a while
- Outcomes: Dividing the hours reduces collisions, but people at the back endure the cold longer. The bait trail uses convoy crews and containers. Closing the road drives the porters to other stations.
- Moral cost: The fastest passage and the safest passage are hard to achieve together.
- Source dossier: G01
