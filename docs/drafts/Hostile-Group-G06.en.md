## Grey Herons and Cormorants in the Winter

Grey herons and cormorants are birds that catch fish at the waterside. The heron stands in shallow water, waits, and spears; the cormorant dives and chases its prey. The wetlands of the Han River, Nanji, and Amsa and the waterways of Seoul are winter resting places for migratory and resident birds. In the artificial winter, when rivers and ponds freeze, food shrinks, and they crowd into drainage ponds that do not freeze, puddles at depots, and channels warmed by geothermal heat. There are no mutated birds and no individual that commands the flock.

Several birds appear together in this document: waterfowl that swim on the surface eating water plants and small fish, waders that walk the shallow edges, and raptors that watch from high wires and pylons for rats and tired small birds. They do not command one another; when one group moves, the others merely change where they are.

The danger comes in three forms: raiding fish farms and tanks at the waterside and causing a loss of food; colliding with people or trains on pylons, wires, and railway tracks; and avian influenza and droppings-borne pathogens contaminating water and grain. Direct attacks on people are rare.

## Ecology

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Winter movement | Migratory birds coming down from the north gather at the Han River, Nanji, and Amsa from November. When the river freezes they move to channels and drainage ponds that stay unfrozen and to puddles at depots where heat rises. When wetlands dry up they also fly to fountains in inland parks and rain gutters on roofs. |
| Food | Herons eat fish, frogs, and rats, and cormorants eat fish. In winter, fish in farms and tanks and food scraps washed out of drains make up a large share. Raptors catch rats and tired small birds. |
| Breeding and life cycle | Breeding takes place in spring in separate protected areas. The birds resting in Seoul in winter are mostly migrants that have finished breeding or resident birds wintering in Seoul. If the water level suddenly drops, waterfowl leave first, and the feeding relations of waders and raptors unravel in turn. |
| Senses | Sight is the main sense. They take off at reflections on the water, the color of vests, and the shadow of drones. Engine sounds, gunshots, and loudspeakers tell them only distance. They do not understand human signals or water-level numbers. Drones, balloons, and birds are hard to tell apart on radar, so a person must check again by eye. |
| Flock behavior | There is no command structure. Waterfowl use open water in flocks, waders sweep the shallow edges with spacing between them, and raptors follow the density of other birds from high wires. When one group moves, the places of the others change because of food and alertness. |
| Disease and danger | Avian influenza, salmonella, and parasites in droppings soil water and grain. When a flock of migrants settles all at once, the water is polluted and affects drinking water and tanks. On the Government side with no doctors, an outbreak is hard to stop. A collision between a bird and a train on the tracks or on a pylon causes an accident. |
| Response principles | Cover farms and tanks, and keep birds from perching around tracks and pylons. Driving them with sound and light only moves the birds to the next wetland. Lowering the water level all at once scatters food and pushes birds toward people. Results are judged not by how many were driven off but by damage to farms, the number of train collisions, and the amount of contaminated water. |

### G06E01 · Waterfowl flock

- Size and place: Open water, unfrozen drainage ponds, and the Han River riverside.
- Winter behavior: They use the water surface in flocks and eat floating matter and fish. When the water freezes they crowd onto unfrozen ponds.
- Danger: They raid farms and tanks, and when they settle all at once the water gets dirty.
- Response: Cover farms and tanks and keep the shallow edges clear.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0057"></a>
<a id="g06e03"></a>

### G06E03 · Wader flock

- Size and place: Shallow muddy edges and around drains.
- Winter behavior: They sweep the edges with spacing between them, eating worms and small fish.
- Danger: They eat up the small fish at the edges of tanks.
- Response: Block the edges low and fix the hours people pass.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0149"></a>
<a id="g06e07"></a>

### G06E07 · Raptor

- Size and place: On high wires and pylons.
- Winter behavior: From a height it watches the density of birds and rats below and moves accordingly.
- Danger: On tracks and wires it may collide with a train or be electrocuted.
- Response: Keep it from perching on tracks and wires.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0218"></a>
<a id="g06e10"></a>

### G06E10 · Unfrozen drainage pond

- Size and place: The drainage ponds of the depots in Gangnam-gu and Seocho-gu. This is Arc land.
- Winter behavior: Heat rises so they do not freeze and birds gather. They are close to the depot’s inner lines.
- Danger: Birds and trains collide, and when migrants settle all at once the water is polluted.
- Response: Keep birds from settling around the inner lines and slow trains.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0287"></a>
<a id="g06e13"></a>

### G06E13 · Old wader

- Size and place: It stands for long periods at the waterside.
- Winter behavior: Old, it flies slowly and leaves late.
- Danger: Even when people approach it leaves late and meets with an accident on tracks or roads.
- Response: Do not go near, and let people go around first.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0356"></a>
<a id="g06e16"></a>

### G06E16 · Inland population

- Size and place: Waterside wetlands toward Seoul Station, fountains in inland parks, and rain gutters on roofs. This is Government land.
- Winter behavior: When wetlands dry up they temporarily move to fountains in inland parks and rain gutters on roofs.
- Danger: Droppings pile up in rooftop gutters and the water is polluted.
- Response: Cover the gutters and clear them regularly.

## Habitat and Winter Response

A heron’s or cormorant’s territory is set by unfrozen water. North of the river, the wetlands along the Han River toward Seoul Station and the Han riverbank at Nanji (Government land) are large resting places. South of the river, the Han riverbank at Amsa and the drainage ponds at the depots in Gangnam and Seocho (Arc land) give unfrozen shelter, and the drainage ponds become unfrozen pools where migrants gather. As winter deepens, the unfrozen water draws together into one place, and both birds and people target the same spot.

When birds gather close to a depot’s inner lines, they collide with trains and cause accidents. Tidying the area around the inner lines so birds cannot settle, and slowing trains, is Arc’s job, but Arc has few people and cannot do it at once. On Government land, people working on pylons and wires carry the risk of collision and electrocution. Free City fish farms can make and fit covers themselves, so damage there is comparatively small.

Birds do not slip through shutter gaps, but they enter surface buildings with open roofs, vents, and tanks. People who live deep underground seldom meet birds, while people on the surface guard their tanks, grain, and washing places themselves, since they are all in the same place.

Good response means blocking food and places to perch. Cover tanks and farms, and remove perches around tracks and pylons. Lowering the water level all at once scatters food and pushes birds toward people, so lower it slowly. Results are judged not by how many were driven off but by damage to farms, the number of train collisions, and the amount of contaminated water.

## Linked scenarios

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0446"></a>

### G06-SC1 · Three Functional Groups in the Shallow Water

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: As the river begins to freeze, waterfowl, waders, and raptors all gather at a single unfrozen drainage pond.
- Actors: Arc stationmaster in Gangnam, facility maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: When unfrozen water concentrates in one place, waterfowl use the surface, waders the edges, and raptors the high pylons, so three groups overlap at one pond.
- Choices: Fix the pond’s water level / Deliberately clear one edge to lead the birds there / Fit a cover around the pond
- Outcomes: Fixing the level increases birds and the risk of train collisions. Clearing one side gathers birds there. Covers take labor.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to protect train safety or the unfrozen water where birds rest.
- Source dossier: G06

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0450"></a>

### G06-SC2 · The Birds That Learned the Timetable

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: Every time a train passes on the depot’s inner line, the birds take off and the train stops and inspection drags on.
- Actors: Arc stationmaster in Seocho, train maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Birds learn the repeated train times and quiet inspection hours, and gather beside the tracks or leave according to the schedule.
- Choices: Change train times / Remove perches around the tracks / Slow the trains
- Outcomes: Changing times leaves the birds wandering for a few days before they learn again. Removing perches takes labor. Slowing trains slows operations.
- Moral cost: Decide where to spend time: on train operations or on the burden of inspection labor.
- Source dossier: G06

<a id="Hostile-Group-G06-heading-0452"></a>

### G06-SC3 · The Tracks of Migratory Week

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: In the week migrants arrive, birds settle on the tracks and pylons and train service stops.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government, the Free Cities, and Arc; train workers; ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Migrants settle all at once, raptors take the high pylons, and waterfowl and waders gather in the shallow water around the tracks, so three heights overlap on one line.
- Choices: Stop trains during migratory week / Put up temporary netting around the tracks / Make a pool for the birds far from the tracks
- Outcomes: Stopping halts supplies. Netting takes labor. A distant pool draws birds away from the tracks, but people who lose the water complain.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to reduce freight by train or the unfrozen water where birds rest.
- Source dossier: G06
