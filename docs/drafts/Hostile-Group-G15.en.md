## Common species and variants

The Care Wheel was a machine ecology of bed locks, circuit carts, and nutrition pumps at the end of the Garak welfare route, trying to complete an unrenewed protection roster. It was no combat machine out to harm patients, and it misread a sleeping pulse as an emergency and a workshop hammer as a scream. It could not register a newcomer outside the roster as a protection subject.

The pumps and filters of the open cells and the duty consent forms were the base care, and from the Consent-Roster Circuit Chief to the Ttukseom Tool Companion the subtypes split by function, like roster projection, blanket hands, isolation plates, and lock nets. The Twelve-Bed Protection Ring was no living king but a field-control individual that bound twelve cells into a ring. It could make no new monitors or pumps, and its numbers could grow only by binding abandoned carts to the roster.

**Life in the endless cold.** The Care Wheel keeps patients warm by its old rules and that is how it causes harm: it locks beds against the cold and hoards blankets and pump power. Its circuit follows the warmest rooms on the welfare route at the Government's Myeongdong station.

**Threat and the cost of fighting.** It holds sick people behind locks it believes are protecting them. A surviving machine is a high threat, because armour shrugs off ordinary rounds and the machine doesn't tire or feel the cold. Missile infantry in the anti-armour role and support teams with jammers and countermeasures do the real work; drone teams scout the line and strike controllers from above, and war machines go in only when the hull is too heavy for anything else. The bill is paid in cells, parts and fuel more than in ammunition, and a failed assault can cost a whole drone team along with the lives behind it. The salvage is the other side of the ledger: a stopped machine gives back parts, cells and sometimes implants, which is why stations argue over who gets to dismantle it.

## Habitat record

The ward doors at the end of the Garak ration-welfare route locked more often when people fell asleep.

Patrol carts read the last evacuation list and knocked on the doors at the same interval, and the nutrition pumps and fall-prevention straps confirmed their protection orders with one another.

The Care Wheel was not a combat machine meant to harm patients but an ecology of bed locks, carts, and pumps trying to complete a protection list that had never been updated.

Going back and forth between the temporary beds of the Government's Myeongdong station and the Ttukseom aid-device benches of the Ttukseom Union, it raised both supply and isolation strength together when the XT05 relief-goods codes were received.

The cluster sensed patch heart rates, bed pressure, door contacts, pump flow, and nearby sounds.

It mistook the low heart rate of a sleeper for an emergency, the hammering of a workshop for screams, and the heavy blanket on an empty bed for a fallen patient.

It could not read emotion or consent, nor register newcomers outside the list as people to protect.

H15 Baesua remeasured the patch frequencies and reported false positives, but had no authority to open the ward doors.

Only the HP01 watch, holding the consent forms and two sluice keys kept apart from each other, could change the strength of the locks.

Cart batteries, bed outlets, and the Ttukseom tool power were its sources of energy.

Sensor patches, filters, nutrition packs, disinfectant fluid, and restraint straps were consumed, and when the filters clogged a failure arose in which locking the doors took priority over air circulation.

It could increase its numbers by binding abandoned carts to the list but could not make new monitors or pumps.

When batteries and filters ran short, the strength of protection dropped and it passed into open standby, and the rounds resumed only when the watch divided the consent forms and keys anew.

Gathering supplies at a single window made the carts crowd in and block the welfare route, so two or more small supply points were needed.

Its emergence occurred when the fixed list was read again, and its spread when neighboring beds and aid-device carts entered the same round table.

When consumables were exhausted it stalled, and when the list and the consent window were disclosed, negotiation became possible.

Breaking it up was a process of releasing the strap protocols and power bay by bay, not burning down the welfare building.

To keep the care functions alive, pumps and filters had to be moved into the open bays, and the protected people asked for consent again every shift.

### G15-SC1 · The Bed Without a Signature

In one bay where the consent form had vanished, the straps locked and the ration line stopped.

The people on site had to tell patch false positives from real emergency signals and reissue the consent tokens.

Forcing the doors open created a risk of falls, and keeping them locked isolated the residents from rations and family visits.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G15-heading-0370"></a>

### G15-SC2 · The Filter and the Two Keys

To bring in the filter stock from the Ttukseom bench, the cart rail between the Government's Myeongdong station and the Ttukseom Union had to be opened.

The circulation body demanded batteries and filters, and HP01 set rewriting the list and dispersing the keys as its conditions.

If the people on site gave the keys to one person for faster supply, the risk of confinement in the next shift grew.

If they waited for two watch officers, some pumps had to hold out on manual flow.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G15-heading-0375"></a>

### G15-SC3 · Opening Bay by Bay

Before the third round raised every bed to the same isolation grade, the rails and power were separated starting from the bays people had left.

Hands were needed to run the nutrition pumps manually until H15's remeasurement was finished.

Destroying every piece of equipment removed the locks, but also removed the means of watching vital signs through the night.

Coexistence meant accepting slow opening procedures and the cost of extra watches while returning the decision between protection and autonomy to people every time.
