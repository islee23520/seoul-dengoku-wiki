## Common species and variants

They were raccoon family groups going back and forth between the pipelines connected to the pumping station of the Arc in the Ttukseom Union, the shelters beneath the workshops, and the cable galleries of the Yeouido Investment Federation in the Mapo Union. By day they stayed beneath manholes and by night they circled the alleys within about 50 meters of the exits, eating aquatic food, trash, carcasses, and microbial mats. The elder female held priority only in moving the young and at narrow passages, and there was no battle command.

From conduit patrol to conduit bones, these were ordinary raccoons adapted to the urban streams. Climbing to a high ventilation room before the pumps did was family learning shared from flood memory, not a supernatural power, and the dry refuge cavities and cable trays were hiding places animals had filled, not machine strongholds. This batch held no mutant individuals, and the missing E13 through E16 belonged to an excluded batch.

## Individuals and battlefield formation

They read the current and the air moving through gaps with their whiskers, and felt pre-start pressure changes through their feet and bodies. When a pipe filled or an air layer broke, the same senses went dead, and a move that ran ahead of the official sensors was not always right. Even when H04 Gangbyeol's robot summed the heat sources into one colony, the drainage watch split them again by sleeping place and entry time.

### Role and Body

The Dry-Cavern Elder Female was a named controller of the Raccoon Dog, but she held no combat command, only priority over the movement of the young and over narrow passing points.

Her whiskers were long and the rust of the pipes had soaked into her paw pads; she read the change in pressure before the pumps started with her body and climbed first to the high ventilation room.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0017"></a>

### Origin

She was the elder female of a family group that settled in the dry refuge chamber beside the Ttukseom Union pumps after the inspection of the Jungnang and Tancheon trunk lines broke off.

The rule that she knew the water level before the pumps did was observed only in this region, and no accident was attributed to any real local government.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0020"></a>

### Habitat

Her core was the dry burrows in the pipes connected to the Ttukseom Union pumping station and the refuge chamber under the workshop.

When the flood level rose she moved her young to the underground transmission gallery of the Mapo Union, and in the XT04 cold wave she shared the only dry approach with other families.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0023"></a>

### Needs

Aquatic food, organic garbage, and carcass leftovers were her energy, and low current speeds and dry shelters were her upkeep costs.

When HC04 skipped dredging, the food film thickened and the density rose, and when it overdredged, this female pushed her young into the surface markets.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0026"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

In the dry season each family used its own shelter, and at the leading pressure before rain they moved to the ventilation room.

When the shelters were flooded again and again, they gave up the division of time and scattered into the alleys.

Her priority was not a hereditary line but the experience of having survived.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0030"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

She gave no combat orders to the other families.

Even when the H04 robot summed the heat sources into a single colony, the HC04 drainage duty split them by sleeping places and times of coming and going, and the human eye had the final word.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0033"></a>

### Escalation

In the first stage, her move to the ventilation room before rain became an unofficial alarm.

In the second stage, droppings and microbial film covered the water-level sensors and clashed with the inspection robots, and in the third stage, in a cold wave or flood, pump maintenance and evacuation competed in the one dry pipe.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0036"></a>

### Engagement and Response

Poison bait and full flooding harmed both the downstream and the workers through rotting carcasses.

Partial damming, substitute shelter pipes, washing the sensors, and restricting night passage on the surface were the basics, and success was the pump recovery time, the reliability of the sensors, and zero bites on the surface.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0039"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Adopting the raccoons' movement as a preliminary alarm bought preparation time but left the responsibility for false positives, and waiting for the measurements of the HP01 Sluice House could be too late for a sudden inflow.

Preheating the pumps only when the two signals differed saved fuel but missed short local downpours.

Whether to entrust public safety to an unofficial ecological signal was this controller's moral cost.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0043"></a>

### Scenario Links

She made the central movement of G04-SC1 "The Leading Indicator Beneath the Manhole," in G04-SC2 she held one family's priority in the division of time that overlapped with the dredging window, and in the dry passage of the G04-SC3 cold wave she forced one of three choices: heated shelter pipes, delayed inspection, or outside footholds.

It was an ordinary adult of a family group that came into the stormwater trunk lines emptied by people, following aquatic food, garbage, and carcasses.

The low-current pipes of the Ttukseom Union were its base, and when the water level rose it dispersed briefly toward the Mapo Union vents.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0060"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

In the dry season it circled only its own family's feeding grounds, and after a flood the families divided the hours and used the same trunk line.

A small build suited to narrow pipes could survive across generations, but it grew weaker at surface crossings and in cold waves.

Changing refuges within a few days was learning, not heredity.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0064"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

It recognized the elder female's priority only at passing points, and otherwise followed its own timetable.

Because the robot logs confused floating debris with this individual, it was not counted in the colony numbers until people had confirmed it.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0067"></a>

### Escalation

In the first stage it followed up to the high ventilation room before rain, in the second stage it met the inspection robots in pipes where the sensors were covered, and in the third stage, when only one dry pipe was left, it overlapped with the pump approach.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0069"></a>

### Engagement and Response

Driving it out of the alleys only shortened its 50-meter rounds to the doorsteps of homes.

Dividing the night passage windows, opening substitute shelter pipes, and washing the sensors were the response, and culling ruined the water quality further.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G04-heading-0072"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Dividing the 50-meter alley into no-passage windows inconvenienced people and kept the raccoons in the pipes.

Driving them out on the surface reduced complaints at once but increased intrusions into homes.

Poisoning out of fear of disease endangered both the pipe workers and the downstream drinking water, and lost a leading-indicator species.

### Scenario Links

## Habitat record

The Raccoon Dog were raccoon family groups going back and forth between the pipelines connected to the pumping station of the Arc in the Ttukseom Union, the dry shelters beneath the workshops, and the underground cable galleries of the Yeouido Investment Federation in the Mapo Union.

After the collapse, when the inspection cycles of the Jungnang, Tancheon, and inner storm-water trunks were cut off, animals that had moved following aquatic food, trash, and carcass remains settled in the pipelines people had abandoned.

By day they hid beneath manholes and by night they patrolled the alleys within about 50 meters of the exits.

The sight of wet fur rising from storm drains and covered channels stirred the fear that sewage was flowing back into living spaces, but seeing them as a single underground army was wrong.

The part that touched reality went no further than the fact that wildlife could appear around urban streams and facilities.

The order of sensing the water level before the pumps and influencing pipeline operations was observed only in this region after the collapse, and did not explain accidents at any real institution or business.

The core habitat was the low-velocity pipelines and dry shelters beside the Ttukseom Union pumps.

When rain continued and the water rose, the families moved their young to the high ventilation rooms and then dispersed into the transmission galleries and ventilation shafts of the Mapo Union.

When cold snaps and logistics breakdowns struck from the direction of the XT04 Duman River Far East Theater and changed the distribution of feed and waste, they moved briefly to the eastern branch lines and returned to their familiar pipes after the thaw.

Aquatic food, organic trash, carcasses, and microbial mats became their food, and dry shelters and gentle currents lowered the cost of survival.

When HC04 Ttukseom Heat-Source House put off sediment dredging too long, the food film thickened and density rose, and when it scraped everything out at once, the family groups were pushed out into the surface markets.

Droppings and microbial mats covered the water-quality sensors and slowed even the pumps' judgments, so it could not be reduced to a problem of too much food.

The Raccoon Dog had no fixed leader.

In the dry season each family used its own feeding ground and dry shelter, and when several families used the same trunk they divided the hours.

The elder female only held priority in moving the young and at narrow passing points, and gave no battle orders to other families.

When the food film thickened after a flood, different families took turns using one pipeline, and when their shelters flooded repeatedly, they gave up the sharing rules and scattered into the surface alleys.

Even when the pipeline robots of H04 Gangbyeol added up several heat sources and movements into a single colony, the HC04 drainage watch had to divide them again by sleeping places, numbers of young, and times of coming and going.

Treating the family groups as a single swarm did not make their movement routes easier to predict; it meant missing their different escape directions all at once.

With their whiskers they read currents and the airflow in narrow gaps, and through pressure changes felt in their feet and bodies they sensed the water rising before the pumps started.

By smell they told food from disinfectant and rotting carcasses, and by hearing they separated pump vibration from the sound of human boots.

There were times they moved to high ground before the official sensors did, but they were not always right.

For it could be the result of families sharing past flood experience and learning the safe dredging timetable.

Over many generations a small build suited to narrow pipes and strong nocturnality might remain, but such adaptations lowered their ability to cross the surface and their resistance to cold.

Changing shelters within a few days had to be distinguished as family learning, not genetic change.

H04's logs confused raccoons with floating debris and crouching people, so the final judgment required human visual confirmation.

Conflict began with pressure precursor signals and grew through sensor disagreement and competition for shelter.

In the first stage, before rain, the family groups moved to the high ventilation rooms and served as an unofficial alarm.

In the second stage, droppings and microbial mats made the official water gauges read low, and the inspection robots met the family groups face to face in narrow pipes.

In the last stage, floods or cold snaps left only one dry pipe, and the pump maintenance crews and several families demanded the same space.

Poison bait and total flooding looked like quick solutions, but the decay of carcasses worsened water quality and endangered both the downstream and the workers.

Partial water-blocking, sensor cleaning, alternative shelter pipes, and limited night surface passage came first, and results were measured not by the number removed but by pump recovery time, sensor reliability, and 0 bites on the surface.

G04-SC1 ‘The Leading Indicator Beneath the Manhole’ began when, though rain had not yet come, the family groups moved their young to the high ventilation rooms from daytime on.

The Ttukseom Union and HC04 could adopt this movement at once as a preliminary alarm, could wait until the field measurements of HP01 Sluice House were finished, or could preheat the pumps only when the ecological signal and the measured values disagreed.

The first choice bought preparation time, but left the question of responsibility when a false alarm wasted fuel and labor.

The second was clear in its grounds but could be late for a short, sudden inflow.

The third saved fuel in ordinary times but risked missing a local downpour in which both signals came late together.

The raccoons' behavior could be useful supplementary data, but must never become grounds for punishment or culling.

Whether to entrust part of public safety to an unofficial ecological signal was the burden of this choice.

In G04-SC2 ‘Two Families at the Dredging Window,’ the night dredging time HC04 had set overlapped with the alternating shelter times of two families.

Putting in the equipment as planned brought both families out into the surface markets of the Ttukseom Union and the Mapo Union at once, and complaints arose on both the cable passage and the drainage side.

Dividing the dredging into two sections kept each family's escape route but delayed the completion of the work.

Opening an empty dry pipe as an alternative shelter could reduce surface appearances but meant giving up the Mapo Union's spare space for communication cables.

Buying out one family's hours of use with food and a safe passage and dredging intensively was fastest, but unfairly changed the time-sharing rules the other family had kept.

Judged by efficiency alone the last choice could be advantageous, but sharing rules once broken came back as larger simultaneous appearances in later rains.

G04-SC3 ‘The Dry Passage of the Pump Room’ was the stage at which, in the XT04 cold snap, three families occupied the only unfrozen pump approach together.

Providing heated alternative shelter pipes could clear the inspection route and disperse the families, but power rations fell and the Mapo Union's other facilities had to endure the cold.

Putting off pump inspection avoided the immediate clash but increased the risk of flooding at thaw or in a sudden rain.

Installing temporary external inspection platforms allowed maintenance without clearing the pipes, but the work crew had to bear the labor of installation in the cold and the risk of falls.

HC04 adjusted the dredging times, surface curfew times, and heating of the alternative pipes in one ledger.

At the last sluice inspection just before the flood, the Ttukseom Union slowed the upstream inflow and, instead of clearing the sediment from the side ditches, accepted a plan to preserve the continuity of the dry passages and feeding grounds the Raccoon Dog had used.

The Mapo Union closed the temporary outfalls that had hidden sources of pollution and disclosed its water-quality records in the joint ledger, and XT04 linked the alarm network with bypass channels so that the evacuation routes would not be submerged even when the water rose sharply.

HC04 took on the treatment of injured animals and the moving of the young while stopping indiscriminate capture, and H04 brought night patrols and residents' representatives together to reduce dumping and food conflicts around the passages.

With this, the central sump became not a barrier dividing human zones from habitat zones but a buffer zone sharing the times for water, waste, and movement.

Each group agreed not merely to stay out of the other's territory but to take turns with inspection, cleaning, and rescue at set hours, and when damage occurred, to investigate its cause jointly first and then divide the burden according to contribution to inflow and capacity for recovery.

When the passages held and the backflow stopped even after the first rain, the old expulsion order was withdrawn.

The Raccoon Dog was recognized as lawful residents of the underground, and the five parties accepted one another as neighbors in the same watershed rather than as objects of surveillance.

So that this agreement would not remain a one-time truce, later management hardened not into a seasonal joint stewardship but into a system of joint care.

The Ttukseom Union and XT04 measured water level, flow velocity, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, and ammoniacal nitrogen every week, the Mapo Union recorded monthly traces of illegal discharge and the weight of floating waste, and HC04 and H04 disclosed together every quarter the number of animals, survival of the young, rates of skin and respiratory abnormalities, passage use, the number of resident reports, and response times.

The standards were set at no more than 1 rainy-season backflow a month, at least 95 percent operation of the main passages, a decrease in the share of injured animals from the previous year, and initial response to emergency reports within 30 minutes.

When the standards were missed twice in a row, the alarm network automatically moved up a stage, and after temporary embankments, cutting off pollution sources, moving vulnerable animals, and independent sample retests were carried out in order, only the section at fault was closed on a limited basis.

If data were lost or a responsible party withdrew, the records were restored through paper ledgers and dispersed observers, and part of the next season's management authority was entrusted to the joint committee to restore trust.

Unexpected changes appeared as well.

As the stench and trash lessened, the distribution of night insects changed, the bats and birds that pursued them increased and widened the food web around the drains, and on the now safer walking paths late-hour activity by residents increased.

As a result ecological recovery could come back as new pressure from noise and light, so they included light-level limits, low-noise hours, and seasonal detour sections among the indicators and kept revising the scope of coexistence.
