## Common species and variants

The One-Eye Tower was an ecology of heads on the Yeouido waterfront masts and the Records Bureau rooftop of Seoul Station, short transfer rails, and transmission swarms turning an old threshold table. It was no omnipotent surveillance net tracking people, and it could not merge signals from inside buildings or beyond the mast rows. It mistook cooling-tower steam for fire, metal beds for armed movement, and high-tide railings for intrusion.

The mast rows onto which the threshold sheet was reloaded were the base, and from the Amber-Red Threshold Warden to the West-Sea Salt-Haze Watch Chief the individuals were machine and facility subtypes split by function like thermal domes, tide staffs, flag masts, and rooftop rail nets. The Total-Red Orbit Ring was no biological king but a battlefield strongpoint where sluices and rooftop orbits were bound by a circular relay. No new satellite could be launched, and numbers were kept only by putting abandoned heads onto nearby rails.

## Habitat record

The Yeouido waterfront mast caught the metal rails of a medical bed in the fog as an intrusion target.

When the red flag went up, the transfer route beside the sluice of the Sluice closed, and the head on the roof of the Seoul Station records office received the same threshold table and locked everything as far as the central Seoul Station corridor.

The One-Eye Tower was not an all-powerful surveillance network tracking people but an ecology of mast heads running old threshold tables, short movement rails, and transmission swarms.

When the XT02 sea noise grew strong, its southwestern radius widened, but it could not combine signals from inside buildings or from outside the mast rows.

Low-resolution thermal imaging, metal reflection, short-range optics, radio bursts, and pole vibration were the whole of its senses.

It mistook cooling-tower steam for fire, a metal bed for an armed movement, and railings swaying at high tide for intrusion.

It could not identify faces or affiliations, and it erased its footage when the alarm shift ended.

F01 Cooling-Tower Keeper recorded steam times and temperatures to clear false positives but had no authority to lower the flags.

The red came down to yellow only when the HP03 waterfront watch checked the medical transfer register in front of the head.

It drew energy from mast solar trickle charge, rooftop batteries, and a small share of the sluice distribution panel.

When the gimbal bearings, optical domes, band filters, and flag actuators wore out, a head stared in the same direction for a long time.

A mast that hit the battery lower limit or dome fouling froze at yellow and dropped out of the transmission row.

It could not launch new satellites or link heads across the whole city, and kept its numbers only by mounting abandoned heads on nearby rails.

Medical transfer markers and sluice gauging used the same distribution panel, so a total cutoff removed even the warnings of the next flood and fire.

Its emergence occurred when the threshold table was loaded onto a mast again, and its spread when a neighboring head received the same alarm table.

Fouling and battery exhaustion made it stall, and when the whitelist was disclosed it entered a state of negotiation.

Breaking it up meant fixing the red-flag rails and sending the false-positive heads to yellow standby.

Coexistence held only if the alarm channel alone was separated while the water-level gauging channel was kept, and it was tested again whether a real alarm sounded at the next high tide.
