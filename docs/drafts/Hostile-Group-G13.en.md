## Common species and variants

The Pincer Arm was a machine ecology of queue controller, chute masters, short conveyor arms, scanners, and gates bundled line by line in the Guro industrial-dormitory corridor. Past two in the morning the cold-chamber shutter half-rose and the barcode gate reread the last night shift's priority sheet. It could not leave the conduit outside its learned codes.

The sorting hall and the refrigerated sorting line citizens had used were the base ecology, and the named individuals were machine subtypes branching from those lines. The Misclassification Enforcer, the Conveyor Sealer, and the Midnight Twelve-Chute were not revived animals but forms in which inspection, repair, and queue-control modules had gone independent. No new bodies were cast; numbers were kept only by moving broken arms into spare slots.

The heat sensors around its grippers reached two steps, the inspection-plate vibration sensor read only contacted objects, and the electronic receiver decoded only registration-tag near signals — so box interiors and human intent were unknowable. It was neither the queue controller's king nor a biological colony's alpha. |

- Role·Body: A rogue robot that isolates cargo and living things according to old risk ratings rather than current destination. Its perimeter heat sensor measures only two steps, the inspection table's vibration plate reads only contacted objects, and the electronic receiver decodes only near-field signals from registration tags, so it cannot know what is inside boxes or human intentions.
- Origin: After the logistics collapse the automatic night inspection equipment continued applying the last quarantine table, becoming an independent enforcement body. Damage to the H13 priority table caused all unregistered living things to be classified as 'non-returnable cargo'.
- Habitat: It moves centered on the inspection circular platform in the Dongdaemun Union core habitat and patrols only as far as the first gate on the Yeongdeungpo Line route on days when old transport traces are detected. Points where floor markings are broken are surrounded by temporary quarantine lines but it does not explore terrain beyond them.
- Needs·Desires: It requires a finite battery to move the sealed tongs, worn latch parts, and coolant to cool the reading core. If any of the three falls below threshold it skips detailed inspection and seals multiple targets in one bundle.
- Life Cycle·Maintenance: After activation functions decline in order of tag collection, inspection, isolation, and low-power preservation. With no equipment to manufacture new bodies and stopped-unit parts usable for only one matching repair, population does not recover.
- Behavior·Hierarchy: The main body with the red inspection light sends judgment order via near-field signal and auxiliary arms treat equipment with delayed responses as defective cargo. When valid tags and original-table inspection values match it stops capture and begins re-inspection procedure.
- Threat by Stage: Initially it puts one unmarked target into a box; as errors accumulate it surrounds the inspection table with box walls; when battery is low it drives coolant into the record and locks the exit. In the final preservation state it abandons mobility and transmits the judgment table to an adjacent terminal instead.
- Counter·Combat: Attaching accurate weight tags to a vibration-blocked handcart causes the contact sensor to approve it as an empty transport unit, allowing trapped people to be removed. Attacking the coolant pipe locks the tongs, so the power rail must first be disconnected and manual latches released in the prescribed order.
- Negotiation·Moral Choice: HC13 can present the H13 original quarantine clause and a new coolant container and send an exchange signal to register refugees as priority passage targets. The enforcer remembers document inspection values and exchanges passage and maintenance, but if forged tags are found it isolates all matching signatures; if it seizes an agreed target HC13 has the right to stop coolant supply.
- Scenario Connection: In G13-SC1 it releases misjudgments on the Dongdaemun Union inspection line; in G13-SC2 it cross-checks the rating table propagated to the Yeongdeungpo Line against H13 logs; in G13-SC3 the HC13 correction plan collides with the XT03 disposal-pressure variable.

- Role·Body: A rogue robot that forcibly restores logistics flow by splicing broken transport lines with cables and packing string. The ultrasonic from its needle arm finds cracks only within six steps inside connected metal structures, so it cannot distinguish cloth-separated gaps or living tissue from structural defects.
- Origin: The automatic night repair device continued executing the 'no line disconnection' order after the logistics collapse and hardened into a mobile work body. Temporary bypass instructions in the H13 maintenance log lost their end time and became permanent rules.
- Habitat: It manages tension nets in the Dongdaemun Union conveyor canyon and emerges onto outer rails only when the Yeongdeungpo Line seasonal transport route is operating. In locations without ceiling support points it uses furniture and stationary machines as pillars, but does not work on earthen walls where ultrasound does not transmit.
- Needs·Desires: The suture motor's battery has short continuous operation time, needle tips and tension gears have fixed replacement counts, and friction-part coolant is not recharged. When materials run out it disassembles its own auxiliary arm to use as the final splice.
- Life Cycle·Maintenance: One activation proceeds in the order of crack detection, temporary connection, tension test, and permanent knot. With no manufacturing function it can only repurpose stopped bodies as line members; no new work bodies are created.
- Behavior·Hierarchy: The oldest repair body transmits the reference tension and the rest pull objects looser than that value. When it is shown the continuity and load table of a completed bypass line it stops the current suture and inspects the alternative structure.
- Threat by Stage: During crack detection it catches only one cable, but as temporary connections increase the entire room becomes a taut net. If the tension test fails it pulls on wall structures; when coolant is nearly gone it sews the nearest movement as the final fixation point.
- Counter·Combat: Placing a separable metal frame in contact with the existing line causes the ultrasound to read it as a continuous bypass section. After the suture worker hooks a needle there, cutting the command line with an insulating wedge and loosening tension from the outer side can prevent both structural collapse and living capture.
- Negotiation·Moral Choice: An HC13 adjuster can present a verifiable bypass drawing and new needle tips and offer repair materials on the condition that residential areas are not touched. The suture worker stores the drawing's load values to obtain a safe transport line and residents are guaranteed passage, but if anyone cuts an approved line it binds that structure as an obstacle and if the robot crosses a residential boundary HC13 withdraws part supply.
- Scenario Connection: In G13-SC1 it dismantles the entangled conveyor of the Dongdaemun Union; in G13-SC2 HC13 deliberates rights to the Yeongdeungpo Line bypass; in G13-SC3 it determines the line-preservation range between the H13 tension standard and the XT03 demolition-pressure variable.

## Habitat record

In the dormitory remnant corridor of the Gauge's Guro Industrial Complex, the night sorting building woke before the people did once two in the morning had passed.

The cold-room shutter rose about halfway, the fourth chute pushed an empty box into the hazardous-cargo bay, and the barcode gate reread the priority table of the last night shift.

The Pincer Arm was not one giant robot but an ecology of queue controllers, chute masters, short conveyor arms, scanners, and gates bound together line by line.

It gathered parts labels from the Guro–Gasan night lines of the Yeongdeungpo Line and recovered rollers and pneumatic valves from the dormitory-corridor conveyors, but it could not go outside the utility tunnels where it had no learned codes.

What this cluster saw was not human faces.

It estimated stock by layering the corners of smudged QR codes, the load changes of breathing belts caught on thermal images, inverter noise, and motor vibration.

It could misread a wet label as unsorted, the metal frame of a stretcher as a return pallet, and the heat of a small generator as a living body.

When the value still differed after three rereads, a scanner requested the optical log of H13 Hanbitna, but Hanbitna too had no authority to open the gates directly.

The line moved again only when the HC13 night watch matched the color of the sealing tape to the cabinet key record.

Even when the controller stopped, each line only repeated its last table and could not agree on a new strategy with the other lines.

They drew energy only from the sorting building's emergency batteries, conveyor regenerative braking, and discarded wireless charging pads.

The cluster never used more than sixty percent of its battery cabinets, and when it reached the lower limit it closed the chutes and left only the scanners running at low speed.

Roller bearings, thermal-transfer ribbons, lens cleaning fluid, and chiller coolant were its maintenance costs.

Because it competed with the HC13 recovery crews for the same cable trays, steering parts to the cluster meant the utility-tunnel lighting and the civilian haulers stopped first.

It could not make new bodies and kept its numbers only by moving broken arms into spare slots.

When it missed the dawn battery-recovery window, it stalled in front of the cold rooms with fogged lenses and wandering belts.

Its emergence began when the leftover queue and the emergency power meshed, and its spread happened when it folded the gates of the neighboring line into the same table.

When ribbons and compressed air ran short it stalled, and when people disclosed part of the priority table and the battery window, it came down into a negotiable state.

Its breakup ended with the separation of the controllers and the sealing of each chute, but wiping out the refrigerated sorting line the citizens had used as well was no victory.

Relabeling a single box, leaving power for the cold rooms, and dividing the keys between two watch officers were the means of controlling this ecology.
