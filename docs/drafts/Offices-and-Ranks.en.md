## Origins of Titles

The titles of Seoul in 2126 arose from the hereditary succession that began in the shelters after the Great Blackout and from the differing ways in which the three factions govern. The Great Blackout took place roughly a century earlier, around 2026. The old five-tier rank system is not used.

**A. Shelter Leaders**

After the Great Blackout, people gathered to live in subway stations, and each shelter chose a leader. Leaders were at first elected by vote, but the position soon became hereditary. With no infrastructure, the population did not grow, so the shelters had children take over the work their parents had done. Families who supplied water kept supplying water, and families who repaired power kept repairing power. As work and position were handed down together, a vertical hierarchy hardened. Titles are the names attached to these inherited positions.

**B. The Branching of the Three Factions**

The three factions started from the same hereditary succession but organized positions differently.

| Faction | Territory | Mode of Rule | Principal Titles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Government | 14 districts north of the Han River | Separation of powers and a multiparty system on paper, modern feudalism in practice | President (for life), Senator |
| Free City | 7 districts in the southwest | Independent city-states, no king | Duke (e.g., I Ilseop, Duke of Gwanak) |
| Arc | 4 Gangnam districts | Rule by the Hierophant's clan | Messiah, Hierophant |

In the Government, the National Assembly member elected in each district holds that district's duchy and bears the title Senator. The Senators gather candidates from among themselves to produce a President, who serves for life. A multiparty system is maintained on paper, but in reality there are only two parties. The Government recognizes as citizens only those recorded in its own register, and these citizens are effectively serfs.

The Free City is not a single power. Each city is its own power and holds its own duchy. Because most residents are engineers who trade goods and skills, there was no way to determine whose skills were greater, and so no leader for the cities as a whole could be chosen. Today these cities are collectively called the Union. Subordinate titles within the cities have not been defined.

The Arc split off from the Free City. Go Ye-jin is both Messiah and Hierophant, and rule passes through her clan. Believers replace parts of their bodies with machines, which is called baptism. In the Arc, faith is counted by the number of implanted body parts. Titles below the Hierophant have not yet been defined.

## The Three Factions and the Twenty-Five Duchies

The political units of Seoul in 2126 form two tiers: factions and duchies. There are only three factions: the Government, the Free City, and the Arc. Since each district is one duchy, Seoul has twenty-five duchies, divided as 14 for the Government, 7 for the Free City, and 4 for the Arc. District boundaries, place names, and stations remain as they are; only political ownership is assigned on top of them.

**A. Government**

The Government rules the 14 districts north of the Han River. On paper, it maintains ministries representing the three branches of the Republic of Korea and a multiparty system. In reality there are only two parties, the OMO Party and the Claw Party, and the actual order is modern feudalism. The National Assembly member elected in each district holds that district's duchy and bears the title Senator. The Senators gather candidates from among themselves to produce a President, who serves for life. The Government recognizes as citizens only those recorded in its own register and regards the land south of the Han River as a place where barbarians live.

**B. Free City**

The Free City consists of the 7 southwestern districts and is now collectively called the Union. It is not a single power but a collection of independent city-states. Each city is its own power and holds its own duchy, and there is no king. Most residents are technicians who trade goods and skills, and because there was no way to determine whose skills were greater, they could not choose a leader. The Duke of Gwanak is I Ilseop.

**C. Arc**

The Arc consists of the 4 Gangnam districts and split off from the Free City. Go Ye-jin is both Messiah and Hierophant, and rule is by clan. Believers receive baptism, in which parts of the body are replaced with machines.

## Government: The Three Branches on Paper

The Government is the faction that occupies the 14 districts north of the Han River. On paper, it keeps the ministries representing the executive, legislative, and judicial branches of the Republic of Korea and maintains a multiparty system. In reality there are only two parties, the OMO Party and the Claw Party. Outwardly it is a republic, but in practice it is modern feudalism.

| Aspect | On Paper | In Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Power structure | Executive, legislative, and judicial branches | A feudal order in which a Senator holds the duchy of each district |
| Parties | Multiparty system | Two parties, the OMO Party and the Claw Party |
| Head of state | An elected President | A President for Life chosen by Senators from among Senators |
| Citizens | Citizens of the Republic of Korea | Only those recorded in the Government register are recognized, and they are effectively serfs |

**A. Elections and Senators**

Each district elects a National Assembly member, and the elected member holds that district's duchy. The title is Senator. Since each district is one duchy, the Government has 14 duchies. According to Chapter 1, the northern Government still holds elections, the same answer comes out every time, and it calls this democracy. Elections survive as procedure, but the results never change.

**B. The President**

The Senators gather candidates from among themselves to produce a President. The President serves for life. This system has been compared to the prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire. Only the Senators hold votes in choosing the President; residents on the register take no direct part in the presidential selection.

**C. Ministries**

The ministries representing the three branches remain on paper. What each ministry actually handles, and where ministerial titles fall in the ranks, has not been defined.

**D. The Register and Hierarchy**

Since the collapse, the Government has recognized as citizens only those recorded in its own register. Those on the register receive the name of citizen, but their condition is effectively that of serfs. Scarcity led children to inherit their parents' work, and as a result a rigid vertical hierarchy formed. The positions occupied by Senators and the President, and those occupied by residents beneath them on the register, are fixed across generations.

The Government treats the entire area south of the Han River as the land of outlanders beyond the wall. Chapter 1 records that people north of the river regard the far bank as a place where barbarians live. The three branches and multiparty system on paper cover this feudal order with the form of a republic.

## Government: The President for Life

The Government rules the 14 districts north of the Han River. On paper, it maintains ministries representing the three branches of the Republic of Korea and a multiparty system. In reality only two parties remain, and the actual order is modern feudalism. The Government's highest title is President, and the power to choose the President rests with the Senators who hold the duchy of each district.

**A. Senators and Duchies**

The National Assembly member elected in each district holds that district's duchy. Their title is Senator. Since each district is one duchy, the Government has 14 duchies. A Senator is both the lord who rules his or her own duchy and an elector who chooses the President.

**B. Method of Selection**

The Senators gather candidates from among themselves to produce a President. Both the candidates and the electors are Senators. Ordinary citizens recorded in the register take no part in this selection.

**C. Life Tenure**

The President serves for life. Once chosen, the President holds the office without a fixed term.

**D. Comparison with the Prince-Electors**

This system can be compared to the way the prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire chose the emperor. A small number of landed lords choose the supreme ruler from among themselves, and the one chosen remains in office until death. Because each of the lords holding a vote governs his or her own domain, the President holds office only on the strength of their support.

| Position | Unit | Chosen By | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| President | The Government as a whole | Senators | For life |
| Senator | One district (one duchy) | That district | Not defined |

**E. Citizens and Hierarchy**

Since the collapse, the Government has recognized as citizens only those recorded in its own register. They are effectively serfs. Scarcity led children to inherit their parents' work, and so a rigid vertical hierarchy formed. The selection of the President takes place only at the very top of this hierarchy, among the Senators who hold duchies. The Government regards the land south of the Han River as a place where barbarians live, and the factions there have no share in this selection.

## Government: The Register and the Citizenry

The Government rules the 14 districts north of the Han River. On paper, it maintains ministries representing the three branches of the Republic of Korea and a multiparty system. Yet over nearly a century since the Great Blackout, the actual order has hardened into modern feudalism. What supports this system is the register.

**A. Those on the Register**

Since the collapse, the Government has recognized as citizens only those recorded in its own register. In the words of Chapter 1, only those born and educated in Seoul count as citizens. Being entered in the register confers eligibility for the Government's protection and supplies. At the same time, the register is the basis on which the Government keeps hold of that person. Citizens are bound to a set livelihood and position and have little room to choose their own work. In name they are citizens, but in condition they are effectively serfs.

Which ministry manages the register, and whether people outside it can be newly entered, has not been defined.

**B. Supplies and Defenses**

Chapter 1 depicts Government territory as a place rich in supplies and strongly defended. For citizens on the register, these supplies and defenses are reasons to remain within the system. The Government likewise uses them as grounds for guarding the boundaries of the register.

**C. Views of the Far Bank**

The Government treats the entire area south of the Han River as barbarian land beyond the wall. Chapter 1 records that "they think the other side of the river is where barbarians live." The Free City in the 7 southwestern districts and the Arc in the 4 Gangnam districts lie outside the Government register. Accordingly, people there are not citizens by the Government's standard. How the factions across the river actually interact with the Government is not covered in this article.

**D. Hereditary Livelihoods and Vertical Hierarchy**

Scarcity removed the luxury of choosing one's work. Chapter 1 records that scarcity "made people hand down the work they did to their children." With people in short supply and goods precious, the one best suited to a trade was the child who had grown up watching it done. As livelihoods passed down the generations, a person's place came to be fixed by the work of the household into which he or she was born. This is the rigid vertical hierarchy of Government society.

| Tier | Position | Character |
|---|---|---|
| President | Chosen from among the Senators | For life |
| Senator | National Assembly member elected in each district | Holds that district's duchy |
| Registered citizens | Carry on their parents' livelihoods | Effectively serfs |
| Outside the register | People south of the Han River | Not counted as citizens |

The President is produced by the Senators, who gather candidates from among themselves. This system has been compared to the prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire. The form of elections remains, but those who hold votes are the few who possess duchies. Whether a path exists for registered citizens to become Senators has not been defined.

In the Government, the register and hereditary succession are two faces of the same system. The register determines who is a citizen, and hereditary succession determines what that citizen will do in life.

## Free City: Cities Without a King

The Free City is not a single power. The cities in the 7 districts southwest of the Han River are each independent city-states, like the poleis of ancient Greece. Each city is its own power and holds its own duchy. No common king stands above these cities. There is no hierarchy like the Government's, in which Senators produce a President for Life, nor clan rule like the Arc's, with a Hierophant at its apex.

**A. A Society of Skills**

Most residents are engineers. The cities live by trading goods and skills with one another. Chapter 1 explains the absence of a leader this way: "There was no way to tell whose skills were greater, so they couldn't choose a leader either." In a society where skill was livelihood, there was no common yardstick for measuring the relative standing of skills. As a result, no position arose to represent the Free City as a whole.

**B. The Name "Union"**

As of 2126, the collective name for the Free City is the Union. Go Ye-jin, who grew up in the Free City, recalls in Chapter 1: "Now they lump them together and call it the Union, but when I was growing up it was just the Free City." The appearance of a collective name does not mean a king has come to stand over the cities. Each city remains an independent power.

**C. Duchies and Titles**

Under the rule that each district is one duchy, the Free City's share is seven duchies. Those who hold the duchy of each city are placed in the Duke tier in this article's classification. I Ilseop is the Duke of Gwanak. Some cities call the person in charge of a work unit within the city a "Depot-Master" (provisional).

**D. Relationship with the Arc**

The Arc split off from the Free City. In 2112, while repairing the Free City power grid, Go Ye-jin followed a dead cable and found a station that was not on any map. Later, as the 4 Gangnam districts were united as the Arc, the Free City and the Arc became separate factions.

This system contrasts with the other two factions. The Government has raised Senators and a President for Life atop a vertical hierarchy hardened by scarcity, and the Arc is ruled by the Hierophant's clan. The Free City is held together by relationships of trading skills. The canon contains no mechanism for ranking the cities against one another.

## Free City: The Duke of Gwanak

I Ilseop is the Duke of Gwanak and holds the duchy of Gwanak-gu. In the system of twenty-five duchies, each district is one duchy, and the seven southwestern districts belong to the Free City. Gwanak is one of those seven duchies.

**A. Position Within the Free City**

The Free City is not a single power. Like the city-states of ancient Greece, each city stands on its own and each holds one duchy. There is no king. Accordingly, there is no higher sovereign above the Duke of Gwanak to invest or depose him. Chapter 1 gives the reason this way: "There was no way to tell whose skills were greater, so they couldn't choose a leader either." Most residents are technicians who trade goods and skills.

As of 2126, these cities are collectively called the Union. Chapter 1 records: "Now they lump them together and call it the Union, but when I was growing up it was just the Free City." The name is a way of referring to the cities together and does not denote an office standing above them.

## Arc: Clan Rule

The Arc is the faction that occupies the 4 Gangnam districts. It split off from the Free City and took shape as a faction around 2122, when the Gangnam shelters were united under the name Arc. Since each district is one duchy, the Arc has four duchies. The four districts are Seocho-gu, Gangnam-gu, Songpa-gu, and Gangdong-gu.

**A. The Hierophant and the Clans**

Go Ye-jin is both Messiah and Hierophant. Unlike the Government's President, the position is not chosen by Senators, nor is it like the Free City, where each city stands as a separate power. The Arc is ruled by the Hierophant's clan and the clans that stand beside it. This can be compared to clan rule in the Shia tradition. Positions are not decided by vote or by comparison of skills but are passed on through bloodlines and relationships among clans. Who succeeds to the position of Hierophant, and in what order, has not yet been defined.

Clan leaders are called Clan-Heads (provisional). Those in charge at the station level are called Keepers of the Machines (provisional).

The Arc's titles do not borrow the clerical titles of old religions, because the Arc has replaced all earlier religions. Among canonical terms, only Hierophant and baptism are used.

**B. Baptism and the Counting of Faith**

Believers replace parts of their bodies with machines. This is baptism. Chapter 1 records that "in the Arc, faith is counted by the number of implanted parts." Baptism is a mark that shows through the body that a believer belongs to the Arc. Because machine parts are precious goods in an age of scarcity, baptism is also proof that the Arc can command technology and supplies. Whether the number of implanted parts affects rank within a clan or the appointment of Clan-Heads or Keepers of the Machines has not been defined.

## Arc: The Hierophant

Hierophant is the highest title in the Arc. As of 2126, the holder of this position is Go Ye-jin. The Arc is the faction occupying the 4 Gangnam districts, and the Hierophant represents the faction as a whole.

**A. Forms of Address**

Hierophant is a title. Messiah is a name given by the believers. Both refer to the same person. Go Ye-jin did not choose this name for herself. In Chapter 1, she writes:

> The believers call me the Messiah. I see no reason to correct them, so I let it be.

**B. Background**

Go Ye-jin grew up in the Free City. The Free City is now collectively called the Union, but when she was growing up it was simply the Free City. In 2112, while repairing the Free City power grid, she followed a dead cable and found a station that was not on any map. She then spent five years reading the manuals there.

Around 2117 she performed the first baptism. Twelve people were baptized at that time. Baptism is the replacement of body parts with machines. In the Arc, faith is counted by the number of implanted parts. Around 2122 the Gangnam shelters were united under the name Arc. With this, the Arc became a faction split off from the Free City.

## Succession

Succession in Seoul in 2126 differs by faction. All three factions, however, rest on the same condition of scarcity.

**A. Succession of Livelihoods**

Scarcity led children to inherit their parents' work. Chapter 1 records this as scarcity having "made people hand down the work they did to their children." As livelihoods hardened across generations, a vertical hierarchy formed. Because only those recorded in the Government register are recognized as citizens, the work and status of residents under the Government are handed down together, effectively as serfs.

**B. Government**

Each district elects a National Assembly member by vote, and the one elected holds that district's duchy. The title is Senator. On paper there is a multiparty system, but in reality only two parties remain. The President is elected by the Senators, who gather candidates from among themselves. The President serves for life, and this structure has been compared to the election by the prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire.

**C. Free City**

The seven districts of the Free City are not a single power. Each city is an independent city-state with no king, and the mode of succession differs from city to city. Most residents are engineers, so, as Chapter 1 puts it, "There was no way to tell whose skills were greater, so they couldn't choose a leader either." The Duke of Gwanak is I Ilseop.

**D. Arc**

The Arc governs the four Gangnam districts through clan rule. At its apex is Go Ye-jin, both Messiah and Hierophant, and authority passes along her clan. Believers' faith is counted by the number of implanted parts, but how this number bears on the succession of rank has not been defined.
