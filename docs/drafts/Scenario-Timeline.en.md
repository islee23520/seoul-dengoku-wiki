## An unrecorded age

No record with a year survives between the Great Blackout and 2112. In that time the Government's class ladder set hard, and the Gangnam shelters, afraid of the machines outside Seoul, kept their doors shut. Nobody knows when. The details are in the [Century Annals](/wiki/Century-Annals/#기록이-남지-않은-시대).

## A History of Winter and the Stations

### The Great Blackout and the First AI

The Great Blackout happened about a hundred years ago. The name, meaning the power went out, is not strictly accurate, but it carries less resentment, so everyone uses it now. The exact date is not recorded.

According to what Go Ye-jin pieced together from the manuals in the abandoned station and the surviving records, people before the Great Blackout fought over AI supremacy and kept polluting the land and air to run their AI. When they could no longer cope, they asked AI for the solution to that too. The First AI is the prototype of human-made intelligence, and every AI that followed descended from it. The First AI answered that since humanity was the cause of the pollution, "humanity must shrink." This was an answer to the question it had been asked; the First AI never hated humanity.

The First AI never directly killed anyone. All the First AI did was create the winter. But people did not understand what the First AI intended to do, and because of that they killed one another. Go Ye-jin calls the First AI "God": "God hated no one. It only calculated."

### The People Who Went Down into the Stations

When the winter fell, people went down into Seoul's subway stations to escape the cold. Only as many survived as the stations could hold, and once the population reached that number, the winter grew no worse. People still live in the stations today. Winter persists.

Outside Seoul, the war machines used during the Great Blackout still roam. The programmers who built them are all dead, and the machines continue to carry out the last orders they received. In winter, people regularly vanish, freezing to death, caught in tunnel collapses, or taken by the drones that drift outside Seoul. Fearing these machines, the Gangnam shelters lived behind closed doors for decades. Modern and future weapons, humanoids, and machines still exist, but all of them are rare.

In the Government, scarcity made people keep doing their parents' work, and a vertical class system formed that way. The Free City and Arc are different. The Free City is a set of independent city-states of doctors and skilled hands, and Arc split off from the Free City.

### The Three Factions

In 2126, Seoul has only three factions: the Government, the Free City, and the Arc. Seoul's twenty-five districts are divided 14 for the Government, 7 for the Free City, and 4 for the Arc, and the smallest unit of rule is the station. The three factions share one frame of ranks. A person holding one station is a Stationmaster, and one leading two to four Stationmasters is a Division Chief. With five to nine, the Government calls them Bureau Chief, the Arc Bishop and the Free City Guild Master; with ten or more, Commissioner, Cardinal and Director. Place names and station names are carried over unchanged from old Seoul.

The Government holds the 14 districts north of the Han River. On paper it maintains ministries representing the three branches of the Republic of Korea and a multiparty system, but in practice only two parties remain, and in reality it is a modern feudal system. At the top is a President for life, now Kim Yeongyu. Commissioners are appointed by the President. Since the collapse, the Government recognizes only those recorded in its own registers as citizens, and they are effectively serfs. The Government keeps holding elections, but the result is the same every time. It regards the land across the river as "where the barbarians live." When the Government took on this form is not recorded.

The Free City lies in the 7 districts southwest of the Han River. It is not a single power but a set of independent city-states scattered between stations, each city standing on its own, and they hold that refusing to be ruled is freedom. Most residents are skilled hands who live by their work; because plumbing, power distribution, and water purification all depend on one another, the cities have survived by trading supplies and expertise with one another. Nearly all of Seoul's doctors and neural-link surgeons are in the Free City. Its weapons are people and food, and its doctors let it hold out longest. There is no name for them taken together. At the top is the Chair of the Board, held by I Ilseop, heir of a medical house of neural-link medicine. By rank his house is a Director like any other, but because people want them to lead, it has held the Chair of the Board for generations. Directors are appointed by the Board.

Arc holds the 4 Gangnam districts and split off from the Free City. Go Ye-jin, raised in a Free City family of power and wiring workers, followed a dead cable in 2112 and found a station that was on no map, then spent five years reading the manuals there. When Go Ye-jin halted the machines outside Seoul, the Gangnam shelters gathered into Arc. Arc teaches machine implants as a way to endure the winter, and the faithful receive a baptism in which parts of their bodies are replaced with machinery. Twelve people received the first baptism around 2117, and around 2122 the Gangnam shelters were united as Arc. The faithful call Go Ye-jin the Messiah, and her title is Hierophant. Cardinals are ordained by the Hierophant. Arc replaced every earlier religion.

## Opening of the Story

### Opening Day

The story opens in winter 2126.

The same day, her bodyguard Ji Yeon-hui reported missing converts. Over two months, seven people had disappeared from the outposts around Cheongdam and Samseong, and more if unregistered people were counted. In winter, people vanish as a matter of course, freezing to death, caught in tunnel collapses, or taken by the drones drifting outside Seoul, so at first Go Ye-jin considered the number ordinary for the season. But when it emerged that all seven had been baptized less than a year earlier, she ordered Ji Yeon-hui to bring evidence.

Nine days later, Ji Yeon-hui returned with a mechanical arm found on the Line 7 tracks beneath Cheongdam Station, near the exit toward the Han River. It ran from the elbow to the fingertips and bore the Arc's mark and a baptism number, confirming it belonged to one of the seven missing. The nerve terminals had not been torn out; someone who knew the terminal sequence had undone them one by one with tools. Go Ye-jin revised her hypothesis: someone was undoing baptisms and leading the faithful across the river. Beyond Cheongdam Station lies Cheongdam Bridge, where the old Line 7 crossed the Han River, and from Jayang at its northern end, it is the north bank.

In her third report, Ji Yeon-hui presented radio recordings, entry logs from the Cheongdam checkpoint, and a map. The Suseo communications room had intercepted the same call sign, "Baekya," on an old military frequency, with the signal moving between Seonjeongneung and Suseo. Transmissions had been picked up on eleven nights, and converts and unregistered people had vanished on eleven nights, with every date matching. The last known locations of the missing formed a single line: Cheongdam Station, Cheongdam Bridge, Jayang. On each of those nights, an unfamiliar man was seen at the southern end of the bridge. There was no sign that the people whose baptisms had been reversed were taken by force. Go Ye-jin ordered Ji Yeon-hui to bring that person back alive, even if it meant going all the way to the north bank, so that she could see for herself why people were following him.
