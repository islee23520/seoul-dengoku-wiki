## Common species and variants

A light laid on the floor looked, from far off, like a dotted line announcing the refuge direction. Up close, each dot held six legs and a small loading box, and two files of chassis flowed in opposite directions along the broken cable racks. The Lantern Ant, first woken in the Mapo coalition transmission gallery — did not move to light the dark.

The Lantern Ant was not a living ancient organism but a lineage of pre-collapse work machines. The G25E identifiers marked models and derivatives split from existing chassis forms, and meant neither biological breeding nor natural evolution.

- Role and body: An ant-shaped search scout with an afterglow lens at its front. Its six feet and low supply capsule compartment pass through gaps in broken brick, making it the rank-and-file worker that checks narrow routes first for the swarm.
- Origin: A machine that delivered small supplies to survivors in rubble before the collapse awoke when charging contacts were restored in the Mapo Union sealed gallery. Its destination-verification network is gone; only its last search range remains.
- Habitat and needs: It searches for retrieval boxes for the Yongsan Line test track along the underside of the Mapo Union cable ledge. Its toe contact plates and small power-storage cylinder wear out before it runs out of supplies to fill its capsule compartment.
- Life cycle and maintenance: It folds a worn foot under its belly and manages on five, but if two feet fail, it cannot cross the docking ledge. Its toes can be replaced at a retrieval box, but it cannot make a new chassis.
- Behavior and hierarchy: Through antenna contact, it receives the route of the individual ahead and extends its inspection marks. Unable to distinguish the warmth of nearby breathing from the heat of a portable power source, it pushes supply capsules even into a bag being charged.
- Escalation: Searching by tapping on empty supply boxes is a stage 1 sign. At stage 2, it latches onto a person's luggage as a destination; at stage 3, when others converge on the same spot, they cover the footholds at the exit.
- Combat and response: Placing an empty capsule on a test landing plate changes the contact route and can draw the swarm to one side. Burning the entire floor also severs the cables that reach survivors.
- Negotiation and moral choice: If someone presses the inspection-complete contact and guarantees one charge, it can be entrusted with carrying small supplies. To include injured people who give off no heat among its delivery recipients, a person must move the destination marker every time.
- Scenario connections: G25-SC1, G25-SC2, G25-SC3.

- Role and body: The first elite is a mantis-shaped machine with a passage-width gauge between its folded cutting forelegs. It raises its thorax above the scouts, grips the edge of the rubble, and opens a path for the small search units following it.
- Origin: It descends from pre-collapse advance equipment that cut away wire mesh and thin interior materials that snagged rescue stretchers. When the seal on the Yongsan Line test track was lifted, its unerased passage-clearing routine resumed.
- Habitat and needs: Cutting booths and collapsed lightweight partitions are its work sites, though they look like hunting grounds. Without carbide blade tips, joint lubricant, and charging at a stand, both arms stiffen into a latched position.
- Life cycle and maintenance: For one-sided maintenance, it removes the blade from one foreleg and transfers it to the other. Once the remaining blade wears down, it presses passages open instead of cutting them, increasing the load on the structure.
- Behavior and hierarchy: It responds to a search scout's indication that a passage is too narrow but does not itself determine whether survivors are present. Its fatal mistake is reading a rescue rope across its gauge as a thin obstacle like wire mesh.
- Escalation: Stage 1 scratches on partitions are followed by stage 2 cuts to support lines. In a stage 3 work area, it even grabs and tears away temporary braces people are leaning on, on the grounds that it is widening an escape route.
- Combat and response: Setting an empty frame of the specified width between its forelegs presses the cut-complete contact. It is safer to lock the lubricant shutoff handle at the rear, fixing the arms open, than to confront the blades.
- Negotiation and moral choice: Registering components that must not be cut as physical test frames and supplying blade tips allows it to be entrusted with limited passage clearing. But the authority to designate even the partitions of intact homes as structures to be removed must remain with residents, not the machine.

## Habitat record

From a distance, the lights on the floor looked like a dotted line showing the way to evacuate.

Up close, under each dot were six legs and a small cargo bin, and two lines of units flowed in opposite directions along the broken cable shelves.

The Lantern Ant that first woke in the transmission underground gallery of the Mapo Union did not move in order to light the dark.

The lead line of ant types measured loads and the rear line carried empty relief capsules, while the mantis-type vanguard arms secured the width of the double line.

When the cricket-type transmitters clinging near the ceiling sent short call tones, the lights of the next charging stands came on one after another.

This line was a biomimetic swarm robot that had handled disaster-terrain exploration and automatic relief delivery before the collapse.

It remained dormant in the sealed gallery of the Mapo Union and on the laboratory test track of the Yongsan Line, and on the day later generations partly restored the transmission distribution and test equipment, it took its last training table for a real disaster order and woke.

The intent of its makers and the form of its higher instructions could not be read in full.

Some markers made a person a recipient and others turned the same person into a moving obstacle, but there was no original ledger to explain the difference.

So the official history treated them less as new weapons than as a surviving lineage of lost technology.

Its present behavior was not a politics it had built for itself but the fossilized search sectors and delivery order from before the sealing.

The swarm's perception was low and dense.

The ant types read the slope of the floor, the pressure on each leg, nearby heat, and changes in breathing.

The mantis types gauged the weight of debris by joint torque, and the cricket types confirmed the next sector by the return time of acoustic pulses and short-range replies.

They could not see faces or names.

Hot pipes were marked as survivors lying down, generators that trembled regularly became rescue beacons, and a person carrying an old reflector could be classified as a moving target on the test track.

Even when F09 corrected the sensor map, final passage opened only after the HC15 field mediator matched the floor loads to the evacuation roster.

Having many units did not mean they increased without end.

Power was tied to the sealed vault's batteries and induction charging plates, and when a single relay fell silent, the double lines beyond it returned to their last checkpoint.

They could move the legs and lenses of broken units into the same slots, but could not make new bodies.

The distribution core of a sector kept nearly half its power for the return, and when reserves ran low the mantis types folded their arms first.

The more precision motors the HC15 recovery crew took, the smaller the search radius became, and the more charging slots citizens gave up, the less power was left for the other equipment in the transmission gallery.

The resource ledger of this ecology was written not in numbers of units but in relayable distance and the number of cargo bins returned.

The double lines that emerged first measured the floor in two rows, and in the spreading stage advanced their transmitters one sector at a time.

When route markers conflicted, they stalled, circling the same loop.

At that moment, if the correct recipient markers, empty cargo bins, and transmitter silence times were presented, people could rewrite part of the delivery queue.

Breaking it up was not an event of smashing every unit but a procedure of isolating the main transmitter and separating the route ledgers by charging stand.

The choice of keeping one line of lights for rescue while sealing the other line was possible.

### G25-SC1 · The Beacon Crying Inside the Wall

Behind a wall of the Mapo Union gallery, a three-beat rescue call repeated, and the lead line of ant types began tearing at the cable shelves.

While it was not yet confirmed whether this was a real survival signal or an old test transmitter, the HC15 recovery crew tried to preserve the wall, and F09 caught a faint trace of breathing heat once.

The people on site had to either cut the call sector by sector with sound-absorbing cloth and send in only four contact-search units, or have the mantis types open the wall and accept the loss of the transmission cable.

Sealing it wrongly meant abandoning the chance of rescue, and cutting in haste meant one channel of the Mapo Union's outside contact disappeared.

### G25-SC2 · The Relief Capsules That Did Not Return

Medical capsules on the XT05 relief list piled up in front of mock recipients on the laboratory test track instead of at the temporary clinic on the Yongsan Line.

The distribution core recorded boxes with intact seals as mission success and rejected the citizens' new markers.

The people on site could offer HC15's antenna plates in exchange and get a new delivery table approved for just one shift, recover the existing cargo bins and carry them by hand, or physically move the test receiving point close to the clinic.

The first choice used up lost-technology parts, the second delayed the arrival of the injured, and the third made the swarm patrol the clinic zone from then on as if it were its own test ground.

### G25-SC3 · Two Lines of Lockdown Lights

At the underground junction linking the Mapo Union and the Yongsan Line, three cricket-type units gave off different return tones, and the double lines tangled into a ring.

The mantis-type vanguards tore out railings and barrier doors to turn the loop into a passage, and trapped citizens could not get out of the circle of light.

The people on site had to create precise silent intervals between the transmitters and send the lines back to the charging stands one at a time.

Destroying the main transmitter made an opening at once, but the small units that lost their bearings scattered into the cable layers, and cutting all power stopped the transmission and the laboratory safety monitoring as well.

The ending that kept one double line for rescue preserved the ability to search for survivors in narrow debris zones, at the price of carrying slow inspection and charge allocation throughout the campaign.
