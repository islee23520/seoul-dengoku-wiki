## Feral Dogs in the Winter

Feral dogs are dogs that people once kept and lost, and their puppies. After the Great Blackout, dogs that lost their homes, dogs left behind after guarding building sites and warehouses, and dogs separated from their owners during evacuation met in the ruined streets and formed small packs. They are not mutated and do not send signals; they are ordinary dogs living while avoiding people. Memories of having been tamed remain, so they come close to people, but they fear people as much as they trust them.

A pack is a small family of two or three adults and their young. The dog that has lived longest walks ahead and starts a move first, but it has no authority to command. A family with puppies guards its own spot. In winter, with food short, several families sometimes gather loosely.

The danger comes in three forms: raiding food stores, food-waste yards, and livestock pens; hunting down people who go outside the stations at night in a group and biting them; and rabies and parasites passing to people and livestock. Rabies in particular is easy to notice too late after a bite.

## Ecology

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Winter movement | When the cold comes, they gather where they can shelter from the wind: under elevated roads, in the car parks of surface buildings, and in closed guard passages. They move in search of food around the market alleys near Yongsan and Noryangjin Fish Market, and by day hide in drains and under elevated roads. |
| Food | They eat fish offal, food scraps, small animals, and carcasses. They depend on food people throw away, on grain, and on soup-kitchen waste. When water freezes they eat snow and gather near drains where drinking water does not freeze. |
| Breeding and life cycle | Females give birth in spring and autumn. In winter, with less food, puppies find it hard to survive. A lone wandering dog sometimes joins a family, and when people take some away the rest gather again at the same place. |
| Senses | Smell is sharpest. They tell human scent, disinfectant, and thawing meat apart. By hearing they recognize cart sounds and engines idling, and they remember when the feeding cart comes. The jingle of collars and tags against each other is a marker that tells individuals apart. |
| Pack behavior | The dog that has lived longest decides a move first, but some dogs do not follow. A family with puppies guards its own. When a few bark, nearby dogs rush in and in an instant look like a large pack, but they soon scatter by family. |
| Disease and danger | They carry rabies, canine distemper, and parasites. Rabies appears weeks after a bite, so it is noticed late, and once symptoms start it is hard to save the person. On the Government side with no doctors, the White Cross gives only first aid for bites. At night, when several dogs corner one person, the injuries are serious. |
| Response principles | Seal the food firmly, stay away from families with puppies, and do not go out alone at night. If a person is bitten, wash the wound at once and arrange rabies vaccination. If all the dogs are taken, the rest go deeper in search of food. Results are judged not by how many were caught but by the number of people bitten and the number of store intrusions. |

### G03E01 · Family pack

- Size and place: Around the market alleys at Yongsan and the unloading yards toward Noryangjin, and under elevated roads.
- Winter behavior: The dog that has lived longest goes first and the puppies wait in a safe spot. At night they circle the feeding grounds.
- Danger: They raid grain stores and collection yards. They drive down and bite people who come out alone at night.
- Response: Seal the food and do not walk alone at night. Do not approach the puppies.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0034"></a>
<a id="g03e02"></a>

### G03E02 · Stray dog

- Size and place: It wanders along roadside drains and under elevated roads.
- Winter behavior: A dog that people lost goes alone, still wearing a collar ring. It looks for old feeding places by smell.
- Danger: When hungry it will rush a person carrying a bag of food.
- Response: Do not hold a food bag in front of you or run; back away slowly.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0149"></a>
<a id="g03e07"></a>

### G03E07 · Mother with puppies

- Size and place: Dry corners in the car parks of surface buildings and under elevated roads.
- Winter behavior: She hides the puppies and fetches food. When it is cold she moves them.
- Danger: A person who comes near the puppies is attacked fiercely.
- Response: Go well around the puppies’ place. Do not touch the puppies.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0337"></a>
<a id="g03e13"></a>

### G03E13 · Old dog

- Size and place: It lives at the edge of the pack.
- Winter behavior: Its teeth are worn and it cannot eat hard food, so it clings to soup kitchens near people.
- Danger: It follows people but may be sick.
- Response: Do not feed it. If illness is suspected, do not approach and report it.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0304"></a>
<a id="g03e12"></a>

### G03E12 · Dog with a stuck collar

- Size and place: It moves about with its collar ring caught on wire.
- Winter behavior: It goes about wearing a rusted collar and tag. The caught collar slows it down.
- Danger: An injured dog is quick to bite.
- Response: Do not try to free the collar alone. If it must be freed, do it with two people.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0438"></a>
<a id="g03e16"></a>

### G03E16 · Noryangjin Fish Market population

- Size and place: Around Noryangjin Fish Market and its unloading yards. This is Free City land in Dongjak-gu.
- Winter behavior: They gather in the early dawn when the market opens and scavenge fish offal and ice scraps.
- Danger: They surround unloading workers and rummage through their loads.
- Response: Split the unloading hours and keep fish offal in lidded containers.

## Habitat and Winter Response

A feral dog’s territory is set by food and shelter from the wind. In the north it is the market alleys of Yongsan and closed guard passages; in the south, Noryangjin Fish Market and its unloading yards and the undersides of the elevated roads around them. Yongsan to the north is Government land, and Noryangjin is Free City land in Dongjak-gu. In other parts of the Free City south of the Han River they circle workshop back alleys and the surroundings of depots, and in Arc land in Gangnam they look for rubbish heaps around station exits.

Dogs get in through shutter gaps, vents, and under bars. The deep underground levels can be sealed with shutters, but people who live on the ground floor and above must rely on a single closed door, and workers who go out at night carry the greatest risk. In the Government the White Cross gives only first aid to bitten people, while in the Free Cities there are doctors who can give vaccination and wound treatment.

Good response means sealing the food and separating the times and routes where people meet dogs. Close food-waste yards and grain stores, and fix the times that unloading and feeding carts run, and you will know when dogs gather. Rounding them up pushes the remaining dogs deeper in, so it is a last resort. When a person is bitten, wash the wound at once and move them to a place that can vaccinate.

Results are not measured by the number caught. Look at how many people were bitten, how many times stores were entered, and how long unloading was blocked. If intrusions fall but dog traces vanish with them, that may mean not stability but deepening hunger.

## Linked scenarios

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0539"></a>

### G03-SC1 · The Rusted Name Tag

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: On a snowy morning in the Yongsan market alleys, a dog wearing a rusted collar tag snatches a food sack and bolts.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Yongsan, White Cross, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: A dog that people once kept remembers the place and time of the soup kitchen and returns to the same spot.
- Choices: Hang food sacks high / Fix one gathering place for the dogs / Read the tag to find the owner
- Outcomes: Hanging sacks moves the dogs to other alleys. Gathering them in one place fixes where people get hurt. Reading the tag takes time but the dog may be released.
- Moral cost: Safety of people and keeping the dogs alive collide over which to settle first.
- Source dossier: G03

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0541"></a>

### G03-SC2 · The Band That Remembers the Feeding Cart

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: At the unloading yards of Noryangjin Fish Market, a pack of feral dogs gathers at the hour the feeding cart arrives each time, delaying unloading.
- Actors: Free City stationmaster in Noryangjin, unloading workers, fish-market traders
- Ecological mechanism: Dogs remember the cart’s engine sound and the smell of thawing together with the hour, and when that hour comes the pack gathers.
- Choices: Change the cart’s hour / Keep offal in lidded containers / Move the unloading place
- Outcomes: Changing the hour leaves the dogs wandering for a few days. Using containers reduces food and the dogs go elsewhere. Moving the place costs money.
- Moral cost: Decide who bears the safety of the unloading workers, the burden of market labor, and the risk spreading to the next alley.
- Source dossier: G03

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0545"></a>

### G03-SC3 · Rebinding on the Transfer Stairs

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: On a snowy night, dogs that had scattered gather again under the transfer stairs at Yongsan Station and block the stairs.
- Actors: Government stationmaster of Yongsan Station, White Cross, ordinary citizens transferring
- Ecological mechanism: In the cold several families gather under the stairs, where there is shelter from the wind, and their barking calls still more dogs.
- Choices: Block under the stairs with a temporary partition / Leave blankets and food there to lead them to one place / Cut off night passage
- Outcomes: A partition drives the dogs elsewhere. Gathering them in one place fixes where people get bitten. Cutting off passage slows transfers.
- Moral cost: The safety of people transferring and a living place for dogs shivering in the cold cannot both be provided.
- Source dossier: G03
