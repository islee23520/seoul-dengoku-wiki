## Common species and variants

They were small families of abandoned dogs going back and forth between the vacant lots outside the walls of the Taeuk Group on the Yongsan Line and the night loading docks of the Central Intelligence Bureau on the Noryangjin Line. Their name came from the clink of rusted collar rings striking identification tags, and protein leftovers, feeding packs, sealed water, and shade set their density. The longest-lived medium-sized adult proposed moves, but there was no permanent leader, and families with pups refused even those proposals.

From the empty-ring dogs to the cart-side dogs, these are ordinary dogs made by neglect and social learning. The Transfer Rebinding Core is not the dictatorship of one animal but a phase in which the sounds of several families overlap on the cold-storage approach, and the Strait Unloading Dog is a regional branch of the same species tuned to the salt wind. A torn tag and a slow lead dog are accident and aging, not a new biological stratum.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0014"></a>

### Role and Body

It was the controller, the longest-surviving medium-sized adult. The rusted ring on its collar clinked with iron at every step, and its nose told apart human scent, disinfectant, and the smell of thawing frozen goods. It held no right of command and only proposed movement first, and the passive tag on its collar could be read by H03 Ido only within about 2 meters and was often missed because of wet fur and the metal ring.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0016"></a>

### Origin

After the collapse, an individual that went back and forth between private shelters that had closed their doors and construction-site guard posts joined other wandering families in the vacant lot by the outer wall of the Taeuk Group on the Yongsan Line. As the clink of iron became a sign that gathered its companions, people came to call its gait the lead, but it had never been chosen as chief.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0018"></a>

### Habitat

By day it rested in the closed guard passages of the Yongsan Line and under the overpasses, and when unloading leftovers increased, it went down to the edge of the night unloading yard of the Central Intelligence Bureau on the Noryangjin Line. On nights when the XT03 logistics vibration grew, its proposed routes stretched as far as the freezer ramps.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0020"></a>

### Needs and Economy

Protein leftovers, discarded meal packs, and water sealed after filtering set the density. When the HC03 Magok Life House sealed its discarded protein too tightly, this gait proposed a detour through the market alleys, and when it sealed it loosely, crowding built up at the unloading yard and contact with people grew.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0022"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

When feeding was stable it proposed territorial defense before breeding, and when forced capture scattered the pack, it gathered again where the identification tags remained. When the ring broke, the clink of iron disappeared and regrouping came late, so winding a broken ring with wire was not decoration but survival maintenance.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0024"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

The two adults beside it accepted or rejected its proposals, and the families with pups kept a separate protected area. Even when H03 reported them together as an aggressive cluster, the HC03 mediators separated them again by each individual's identification tag, scent, and companions. Even if the leading individual fell silent, the families only returned to their last sleeping places and did not wait for new orders.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0026"></a>

### Escalating Threat

In the first stage it responded only to particular identification tags and feeding trucks and kept to the edge of the vacant lot. In the second stage, a familiar engine sound and unloading leftovers gathered several families into one passage and made them follow the escort carts. In the third stage, after feeding stopped or after a deceptive capture, the families occupied the markets and the transfer stairs at the same time.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0028"></a>

### Combat and Response

Direct fire and wholesale capture only pushed the families out of their living area. Barrier fences, detour feeding points, noise reduction, and flags for the pup zones came first, and capture was limited to injured individuals or individuals that had actually led attacks. Success was zero bites on citizens, fewer minutes of unloading delay, and the share of families regrouped.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0030"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Delaying the unloading until the shelter-code checks were finished reduced misjudgments but let the food thaw. Registering present companions and pup care first stabilized the pack, but clashed with belated return claims by former guardians. Removing only the leading individual on the pretext of its abandonment history left not 48 hours but days of scattered occupation and shut down the citizens' reporting network.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G03-heading-0032"></a>

### Scenario Links

The incomplete reading of the rusted name tag in G03-SC1 overlapped with this gait's proposal, and the engine sound of G03-SC2 and the freezer encirclement of G03-SC3 took place after the proposal was rejected.

## Habitat record

The Feral Dog was a pack of dogs that lived around the laboratory walls, closed guard passages, and weed-covered vacant lots on the outskirts of the Yongsan Line.

At night some went down as far as the fish loading docks of the Noryangjin Line, and by day they hid in drains and under overpasses.

Rusted collar rings and corroded identification tags struck one another with every step and made a clink of iron.

This sound was heard in the transfer passages before footsteps, giving citizens the unease that old abandonment and neglect were coming back all at once.

But they were not a single attacking group.

It was an alliance of loose families joined by animals from private shelters that closed after the collapse, construction-site guard dogs, and pets that lost their people in the evacuating crowds, following food and familiar smells.

The UHF collar tags and the regrouping mediated by those tags were an order observed only in this region, and gave no grounds for blaming any real shelter or business.

The core territory was the vacant lots outside the Yongsan Line walls, but there was no fixed boundary.

In seasons when fish guts and frozen leftovers increased, they moved to the night loading axis of the Noryangjin Line, and when the logistics vibration from the XT03 Korea Strait Theater grew, they extended their territory to the cold-storage approaches and loading-dock entrances, while conversely they withdrew to the drains behind the market or under the overpasses when the daytime crowds thickened.

Protein leftovers, discarded feeding packs, uncontaminated water, and shade decided the density of the pack.

When HC03 Magok Life House sealed its waste protein too tightly, hungry families scattered into market alleys and children's passages, and when it managed it too loosely, they crowded into a single loading dock and contact with people increased.

For water, filtration and disinfection were not enough; sealed storage was needed to prevent recontamination.

Removing food and creating safety were not the same thing.

Animals that had wandered alone found companions they had met at old feeding stations by scent and the clink of iron, and formed small families of two or three.

When loading-dock leftovers became plentiful, several families regrouped into an alliance that used the same paths while keeping different eating orders and resting places.

The longest-surviving medium-sized adult and the two adults beside it proposed moves first, but there was no permanent leader or chain of command.

Families with pups kept separate protected ranges and, depending on the situation, refused even the lead animal's proposals.

When feeding times stabilized, territorial defense grew stronger rather than breeding exploding, and when forced capture scattered the pack, it gathered again around the animals that still had tags.

So a report that the pack had been dispersed once did not mean a long-term solution.

Their rapid changes came from learning rather than bodily mutation.

They told apart human hand gestures, the wheel sounds of feeding carts, idling vehicles, disinfectant, and the smell of thawing frozen food, and remembered individually the people who had been safe and the people who had threatened them.

A tendency among the young to keep away from people might persist over many generations, but changing passages within days or following particular vehicles had to be seen as social learning within the family.

Smell read body odor and degrees of decay, and hearing separated familiar collar sounds from engine noise.

H03 Ido could read passive UHF RFID tags within about 2 meters, but because of wet fur, metal rings, and tag orientation it counted the same animal twice or missed it altogether.

The electronic record was only a clue to past registration, not proof of present family relations, and people had to check appearance, behavior, and companionship again.

The danger grew in three stages, depending less on numbers than on the way trust broke down.

At first they reacted only to particular tags and feeding vehicles and kept to the edges of the vacant lots.

Next, familiar engine sounds and loading-dock leftovers drew several families into a single passage and set them trailing the convoy carts.

Finally, when feeding stopped or a deceptive capture took place, they scattered by family and occupied the market entrances, transfer stairs, and freezer approaches all at once.

At that point gunfire or mass capture only pushed the scattered animals into a wider living sphere.

Barrier fences, feeding detour points, noise reduction, and visual markers for the pup-protection zones came first, and capture had to be limited to injured animals or those that had actually led attacks.

Success was judged not by the number removed but by 0 bites to citizens, reduced loading delays, and the rate at which families regrouped.

G03-SC1 ‘The Rusted Name Tag’ began when H03 made incomplete reads, at the edge of the loading dock, of three tags carrying different shelter codes.

The Yongsan Line and HC03 could delay the loading until the record check was finished, could split the families with feeding carts to confirm them, or could register first the relationships of those who now slept together and looked after the pups, rather than the old codes.

The first choice reduced misjudgment but delayed the food unloading.

The second was fast but could separate pups that failed to follow the cart from their families.

The third made the pack most stable but clashed with the return claims of former owners who appeared later.

What had to be decided here was not only the accuracy of the data.

The heart of it was which relationship to give greater right: the old ownership records, or the animal families formed on their own after the collapse.

In G03-SC2 ‘The Pack That Remembers the Feeding Truck,’ the engine sound of an old feeding truck rang through a closed guard passage, and the families scattered across the Yongsan Line and the Noryangjin Line gathered on the loading route.

Finding the original driver to clear the passage worked at once but left a weakness in which one person's absence could collapse the whole agreement.

Accustoming a new driver step by step to the smells and the feeding procedure meant cutting loading volume for several days, but it could spread dependence in the long run.

Introducing a mobile feeding station without engine noise did not damage the existing trust signals, but needed staff and refrigeration to change its position every day.

Animals that had lost a particular person could refuse food or grow more aggressive, so building a structure in which trust did not concentrate on one person mattered more than restoring passage at once.

G03-SC3 ‘Regrouping on the Transfer Stairs’ was a situation in which the logistics vibration of XT03 and a halt in feeding coincided and several families gathered at the cold storage.

They surrounded its approach road.

Opening feeding zones for each family could divide the passage without harming the dispersed regrouping network, but used up ration staff and sealed stores for a long time.

Capturing only the injured animals by non-lethal means limited the damage to trust but slowed the restoration of passage.

Moving night unloading to a different freezer preserved the animals' social memory but increased the transport distance and freezing costs of the Noryangjin Line merchants.

The HC03 mediator could keep the agreement only by trading the feeding times, passage-opening windows, and capture limits as a bundle, and presenting the former owners' scent markers together with the familiar carts.

Tag data was to be used only to find missing families or owners, never turned into a punishment ledger or a speculative asset.

Coexistence with the Feral Dog lay not in believing they were gentle.

It lay in admitting the possibility of attack while never treating trust as a consumable.

Using the clink of iron as a false rescue signal made every later rescue call useless, and cutting off feeding made the danger vanish only by moving it to alleys walked by weaker citizens.

Extermination could clear the transfer routes in the short term but collapsed the ethics of care and the citizens' reporting network together.

Territorial markings could be kept alongside detour lines and used as mutual rules of passage rather than being erased without condition.

Whether the animals' present families and memories could be deleted for the sake of securing human passage, or whether the boundaries would be divided again at extra cost and inconvenience, was in the end a choice humans had to bear.

The responsibility for caring for the Feral Dog over the long term lay in binding the outer management of the Yongsan Line, the waste control of the Noryangjin Line, and the water and veterinary care of HC03 into one ledger, while keeping trust from concentrating on any single feeder.

Every season they had to record together each family's sleeping places and whether the pups survived, the rate of duplicate reads of the same animal, bacteria redetected in the sealed water tanks, time spent on the loading route (in minutes), citizen bites and chase reports, and the share of injured animals that rejoined their original companions after treatment.

A simple drop in numbers left out the families pushed into the alleys, so it could not serve as an indicator of stability.

When deceptive capture or a sudden halt in feeding collapsed the trust network, the engine sounds and carts that had been used were retired for a time, and keepers with different smells and walking habits were paired to accustom the pack again, starting with water at a distance.

Recovery was judged not at the moment they took food but by whether adults came out of the passage leaving the pups behind, and whether the family did not scatter in all directions when rescue staff approached.

After deaths or disappearances, regrouping was not forced by following the remaining tags alone; night companionship was observed for several days and the new family boundaries redrawn.

Meanwhile, if travel times and feeding locations were disclosed too precisely, theft gangs could read the dogs' gatherings as the work timetable of the cold storage and find empty warehouses and bypasses.

So location records were shared with delay and only to the extent needed for rescue and disease control, and market guards were not to consume the animals' regularity as a surveillance device.

Only then did the clink of iron remain a promise of safety and not become the signal of yet another exploitation.
