**A. How stations and tunnels are laid out**

The Seoul of the time was not a flat administrative map but a graph of stations and tunnels. Even in 2026 people crossed gu by subway, and the depots sat as the heavy nodes of that graph. On the night of [The Great Blackout](/wiki/World-Unbinding/), when remote control died, stations became fortresses and faction strongholds, tunnels became licensed passage. A century on, the three factions of 2126 read those points and lines as living spheres, and territory is divided by station rather than by gu.

The [Seoul Station Catalog](/wiki/Seoul-Station-Catalog/) records 334 observed-name rows for 315 stations. [The Structure of the World Map](/wiki/World-Map-Construction/) records 25 gu and their assembly order; [Seoul and the Subway Layers](/wiki/World-and-Subway-Layers/) records six layers. Building use is recorded in [River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse](/wiki/Building-Reuse-Geography/).

## Stations

**A. Station strongholds**

A station brought people, facilities, and connections back into use. In a dong with a subway station, the station building was the core facility; distribution, passage tolls, lodging, and refuge were handled there. Schools, community centers, and hospitals supported it. From 2106, passes for Jongno and Junggu carried the Government Seoul Complex stamp from Gwanghwamun. Sindorim was the bottleneck between Yeongdeungpo and Guro, while Suseo held a southern depot.

A station has six layers: the surface stronghold, the concourse, platforms and tracks, transfer corridors, deep tunnels, and depot adjacency. Markets, homes, and offices occupy the concourse; people and freight move through the platforms; toll collectors hold the transfer corridors. The facilities adjoining each station are recorded in [Entering the Station Interior](/wiki/Station-Interior-Construction/). The terms by which a stronghold holds its territory are recorded in Strongholds and Territory.

**B. What a station covers and what it is worth**

A "station" means the subway station together with the surface stronghold above it. One station is the smallest unit of territory, and a person who holds one station is a stationmaster. A station is worth more the more transfer lines meet in it and the deeper it goes. Surface and elevated stations are worth the least, and the stationmaster of a surface station ranks lowest among stationmasters. The Government holds 153 station strongholds in the 14 districts north of the Han, the Free City 77 in the 7 southwestern districts, and the Arc 77 in the 4 Gangnam districts.

Depth also divides standing. The deeper the level, the higher the people who live there. Deep levels keep intruders out behind shutters, make the exits easy to control and watch, and are warmed by the ground. Above ground, the higher the floor, the lower the people: high floors are cold and windy and lie open to watching and attack from the air, drones included.

**C. Five stages**

A station stronghold grows through five stages under shocks and the pressure of population. The stage names and figures are provisional: stage 1 Entrance Post, stage 2 Concourse Stronghold, stage 3 Station Precinct Defence, stage 4 Surface Perimeter Line (for the Arc, Deep-Level Seal), and stage 5 Inter-Station Defence Network. Of the 307 strongholds in 2126, 250 start at stage 3, 50 at stage 2, and seven large interchanges (Seoul Station, Cheongnyangni, Wangsimni, Dongdaemun History & Culture Park, Express Bus Terminal, Sadang, Sindorim) at stage 4. Surface and elevated stations and line ends start at stage 2. A stronghold that loses 30% of its people or runs out of reserves drops one stage. The figures for each station are in [Station Strongholds](/wiki/strongholds/).

In 2106, Jongno and Junggu passes and commercial contracts received a Government Seoul Complex stamp. A counterfeit medicine box appeared at a Seoul Station auction in 2124.

## Tunnels

**A. Track and repair status**

Between station and station lay lines that were never erased. Only the states changed — normal, hazardous, controlled, blockaded, flooded, collapsed, provisional repair — and cause and repair conditions followed. Yeongdeungpo–Sindorim and Sindorim–Guro were adjacencies the current map already knew, and no line skipped Yeongdeungpo–Guro in one cell. The full catalog's adjacencies went up as design data as 435 drawn from map points.

A transfer station cut from power stopped the traction freight flow even with both tunnels sound. Provisional repair passed only foot and light freight; heavy freight and armed passage took separate approval. The deeps were the seat of covert movement and maintenance, but flooding and communications loss came first. The formulas of throughput, isolation, and overreach were locked in Logistics and Infrastructure.

Since 2026, machines have repeated their last search and guard routines on surviving tracks, while rat colonies nest in cable trays beneath the transfer shops. Machines working or moving beside people are recorded in [People and Machines](/wiki/People-and-Machines/). The tracks retain their 2026 connections.

## Depots

**A. The eight depots**

Depot area reflects the rolling-stock storage and maintenance scale of 2026. Eight depots lie within Seoul: Sinjeong, Cheonwang, Banghwa, Gunja, Godeok, Changdong, Sinnae, and Suseo. Each depot belongs to the faction that holds the station strongholds beside it. Jichuk, Dobong, Moran, and Gimpo lie outside the Seoul slots.

A depot was the seat of repair, bulk storage, and siege. Sinjeong's unloading and Godeok's train maintenance became a faction's arms of legitimacy because the hands that moved a stopped train again or sent a convoy were there; Changdong's wheels and brakes lost their owner and remained only as leases in neighbouring stationmasters' ledgers. The drainage ponds became the water surface where tank aquaculture and migratory birds overlapped, and in the deep bays of maintenance sheds looping chassis are sealed. The charging terminals and hydraulic hoses were the repair layer of [Lost Technology](/wiki/Lost-Technology-Lineage/).

The industrial blocks of Guro and Geumcheon clung to the depots and used schools for drying parts.

## Waterfront and Flooding

**A. The waterfront**

The first floors north and south of the Han, and the tributaries Jungnang, Anyang, Tan, Cheonggye, and Hongje, decided which dongs got wet. Yeouido, Ichon, Banpo, Jamsil, Mangwon, Hapjeong, Amsa, Guui, and Jayang were read as main-channel waterfront. Habitation pushed above the third floor and the ration windows sat inside the embankment. The details of the waterfront rules were already written by [River, Gu, and Dong Building Reuse](/wiki/Building-Reuse-Geography/).

## Passages

**A. Transfers and evacuation**

Passages were the narrow lines where transfer, maintenance, and refuge overlapped. The transfer corridors were the hubs of politics and logistics between lines, and blockades, transit tolls, and congestion came first. The junctions where the Gangdong and Amsa ration corridors met the Eunpyeong and Seongbuk evacuation stairs became the warm nests of the rat colonies, and the Sindorim junction split the main freight door from the maintenance passage that took only people and packloads, using siege as its stage. This junction is not among the catalog's 334 observed-name rows. The Sindorim incident's location is recorded in [the Scenario Timeline](/wiki/Scenario-Timeline/).

The vertical connections were stairs, elevators, and ventilation shafts. Flooded floors showed cause and repair conditions and kept people out — that was the design. The Bukhansan Bogukmun mountain refuge roads, the Jegi-dong herb unloading, and the lower passages under the Noryangjin elevated joined the same graph from outside the stations. Connections were never deleted. Their states changed, and those states changed the times of rice, medicine, and convoys.

Garak Market, Gangseo Market, Yanggok Market, and Noryangjin Fish Market are facilities around station hubs. Garak handles price discovery rather than production. Noryangjin relies on refrigeration and ice credit, so it cannot keep goods frozen when power fails. Convenience stores and clinics remain trades around the stations.
