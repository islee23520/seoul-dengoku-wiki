## Brown Rats in the Winter

The brown rat lives wherever people live. Where there is food, warmth, and a hole to hide in, it spreads. After the Great Blackout, when people went underground to live together, rats also gathered beneath the floors of interchange shopping arcades in stations, in cable trays, and around heating pipes. In the artificial winter the surface is frozen and the stations are warm, so rats target the same places as people. There are no mutated rats and no leader that commands them.

Rats keep two or three breeding cores in a section where food and heat meet. If one core disappears the animals simply crowd into the remaining ones and the colony does not collapse. Removing one visible nest only makes them shift for a while. If the same poison or the same ultrasound is repeated, rats learn to avoid that sequence.

The danger comes in three forms: gnawing grain stores and food depots so that food is lost; gnawing cable insulation and causing short circuits and lighting outages with droppings and damp; and spreading diseases such as plague, hantavirus, leptospirosis, and salmonella to people and food. Shutter gaps, vents, and drains are the routes by which rats come and go.

## Ecology

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Winter movement | When the surface freezes, rats move down into the warm underground. The spaces beneath the floors of the interchange shopping arcade in Gwangjin-gu, the cable trays, and the areas around heating pipes are the large habitats. When more people evacuate, branches extend into the cables of evacuation stairs and behind the walls of temporary soup kitchens on the Yeouido side. |
| Food | They eat anything: grain, food crumbs, insects, carcasses, even soap and wrapping. A colony is sustained by crumbs people have dropped alone. They also eat thawing drip and packaging scraps left after unloading. |
| Breeding and life cycle | A female gives birth several times a year. If there is food and heat they breed even in winter. If a breeding core is exposed often to disinfection and lights, some move their breeding place to a tray on the opposite side where there is less heat. |
| Senses | With whiskers and smell they read air flow and gaps. Through their feet and bodies they tell the vibration of a train, a cart, or a person approaching. They cannot pick out distant heat sources and cannot read electronic signals. They avoid bright places and move at night. |
| Colony behavior | There is no fixed leader. A core with heavy traffic looks like the center for a while, and when food changes the center moves. When a path-finding animal opens a hole first, the rest follow that path. |
| Disease and danger | Plague is carried by rat fleas, hantavirus by dust from droppings, leptospirosis by contaminated water, and salmonella by food. In stations where people live packed underground, once an outbreak spreads, the Government side with no doctors is in the most danger. When cable insulation is damaged, lights and guide displays go out and people’s movement is blocked in an emergency. |
| Response principles | Collecting leftovers and keeping things dry is the basis. Block shutter gaps, vents, and drains, and keep cable-tray lids closed. Poison and ultrasound lose effect if the same sequence is repeated, and heavy poison use harms children, pets, and other animals first. Fire burns cables and is not used. Results are judged not by how many were caught but by short-circuit counts, food contamination, and harm to other animals. |

### G05E08 · Main colony

- Size and place: Cable trays and areas around heating pipes under the floors of the interchange shopping arcade in Gwangjin-gu.
- Winter behavior: They get through the winter on pipe heat and leftover crumbs. Branch nests extend along the trays.
- Danger: They gnaw grain and food and damage cable insulation.
- Response: Clear leftovers daily and keep the trays dry.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0057"></a>
<a id="g05e09"></a>

### G05E09 · Secondary colony

- Size and place: A cooler place such as the opposite tray or behind the walls of a soup kitchen.
- Winter behavior: To avoid disinfection and lights, they move their breeding place to a quiet spot. They hardly show on thermal imaging.
- Danger: If only one place is blocked, they crowd into this one. They show only through traces of young.
- Response: Before eliminating one place, check every core.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0080"></a>
<a id="g05e10"></a>

### G05E10 · Cable-tray habitat

- Size and place: Cable trays under the interchange shopping arcade in Gwangjin-gu.
- Winter behavior: The metal run laid beside the heating pipes stays warm even in winter and becomes a sleeping place.
- Danger: Droppings and damp damage insulation and cause short circuits.
- Response: Keep the trays dry and close lids starting from the back. Driving them out by filling with water causes short circuits first.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0103"></a>
<a id="g05e11"></a>

### G05E11 · Habitat behind closed shutters

- Size and place: The shutter line of the interchange shopping arcade in Gwangjin-gu.
- Winter behavior: They use the space behind shutters of shops where traffic has stopped as storage. Warm air leaking through shutter gaps becomes a sleeping place.
- Danger: If the soup-kitchen shutter is closed first, the rats behind are driven up toward the emergency stairs.
- Response: Do not lower all shutters at once; close shops from the back. Move leftover sacks off the rails.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0126"></a>
<a id="g05e12"></a>

### G05E12 · Young

- Size and place: Inside cores and branch nests.
- Winter behavior: They grow on crumbs the mother carries in and weak heat. Most of the young born die in their first winter.
- Danger: Footprints are a sign that a hidden core exists. The young are weak to disinfection.
- Response: Mark only the sections with signs of young and check other places first.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0149"></a>
<a id="g05e13"></a>

### G05E13 · Old individual

- Size and place: They avoid places often treated and stay in the shade of stair cables and unloading ledges.
- Winter behavior: Animals that have gone through the same poison and the same ultrasound know to avoid that sequence.
- Danger: The remaining animals follow the sequence in avoiding it, so treatment stops working.
- Response: Change the sequence, open an exit toward where people do not go, and close from the back.

## Habitat and Winter Response

A rat’s territory is where food and heat meet. The center is beneath the floors of the interchange shopping arcade and the cable trays in Gwangjin-gu on Government land. When people crowd in to evacuate, they extend branches into the cables of evacuation stairs and behind the walls of temporary soup kitchens on the Yeouido side in Yeongdeungpo-gu in the Free City. The underground spaces of Gangnam in Arc land suffer the same under the same conditions.

Rats come in through shutter gaps, vents, and drains. Deep underground levels are easy to seal by closing shutters and blocking gaps, so rats arrive late there, but for people on the ground floor and above, grain sacks and food are at the same height as the rats. The Government has no doctors and finds it hard to stop an outbreak, while the Free Cities have doctors who can treat people but lack medicine and labor.

Good response means cutting off food and heat. Clear leftovers daily, keep the trays dry, and block shutter gaps and vents with mesh. If one colony is eliminated, the animals scatter into neighboring sections, so after checking every core, open one exit toward where people do not go and close from the back. Use poison and ultrasound while varying the sequence, and keep them away from children, pets, and other animals.

Results are not measured by the number caught. Look at short-circuit counts, food contamination, harm to other animals, and the time it took people to pass. If rats suddenly decrease, leftovers in drainage grooves rot and insects can increase, so even after the colony shrinks, also watch how far the leftovers have spoiled.

## Linked scenarios

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0313"></a>

### G05-SC1 · The Second Core Nest

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: Thermal imaging shows only one colony, but traces of young are found on the opposite tray.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Gwangjin, facility maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: With several breeding cores, even if one is eliminated the center of the colony moves to another section.
- Choices: Survey all cores first / Borrow an old cable heat map to cut survey time / Test a one-way exit in one section
- Outcomes: Surveying is safe but opens the passage late. Borrowing a heat map is quick but means handing over passage records. Testing an exit closes one unoccupied section for a long time.
- Moral cost: People who want immediate sanitation and people who want to check there is no harm to other animals split over where to spend the time.
- Source dossier: G05

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0317"></a>

### G05-SC2 · The Error in the Sealing Order

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: When the soup-kitchen-side shutter is closed first, the rat colony is driven up into the cables of the evacuation stairs on the Yeouido side.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free City, facility maintenance workers, evacuating people
- Ecological mechanism: Rats learn the sealing order and open a new path in the direction left open.
- Choices: Close again from the back / Stop recovering leftovers and leave bait / Send people on a surface detour for the night
- Outcomes: Closing again reduces contamination but makes the work longer. Bait accepts food contamination. A surface detour empties the cable level but needs warmth and guards.
- Moral cost: Fast passage for evacuating people and reducing contamination and harm to other animals cannot both be achieved.
- Source dossier: G05

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0319"></a>

### G05-SC3 · The Predator of the Open Courtyard

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: After unloading, rats block the interchange hub and a proposal is made to release predatory birds.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free City, unloading workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Rats increase at a hub where food and heat concentrate, and introducing birds may shrink the colony but bird collisions and droppings become a new problem.
- Choices: Open a courtyard with markers and monitoring / Use only staged sealing / Split unloading schedules to reduce food
- Outcomes: The courtyard needs people to keep it up for a long time. Staged sealing delays rations. Splitting unloading raises cost.
- Moral cost: The responsibility of shifting one ecological problem into another animal’s predation remains.
- Source dossier: G05
