## Common species and variants

It was an ordinary rat colony formed between the underside of the Eastern Command's transfer arcade and cable trays on the Gwangjin Line and the evacuation stairs of the Anguk Order on the Yeouido Line. Crumbs of carbohydrate, protein leftovers, and the heat of insulated piping made its density, and it spread no special pathogens. It had no fixed leader, and when food traces shifted, even the core that seemed to be the center moved.

Gap-whisker, warm core, and counter-core were path and breeding concentrations of the same species, not a king and a guard. The tray-warmer and the closed-shop shade were facility sections the rats occupied, and the unloading film was a phenomenon in which, after a protein unloading, several kinds gathered on the hub floor. The cold-gap was a local branch timed to elevator drip, and E01 through E06 remained in an excluded batch.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0014"></a>

### Role and Body

A specialist pathfinder, it pushes its whiskers into cable-tray gaps to find openings where air leaks through. From the temperature and vibrations transmitted through its feet and belly, it distinguishes trains, supply carts, and human footsteps, but it cannot read distant heat spots on a screen or decipher the text on an electronic information board.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0016"></a>

### Origin

During a winter when ultrasound and sealing panels were repeated in the same sequence, survivors split off by remembering only the open side. After testing an unused pipe support beneath the Gwangjin Line Eastern Command shopping arcade, they turned its opening into a branch between cores.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0018"></a>

### Habitat

By day, it clings to the edges of cable trays and insulated-pipe brackets; it ventures onto frequently disinfected ground-level areas only at night. It visits the space behind shutters where traffic has ceased only as temporary storage.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0020"></a>

### Needs and Economy

Pipe heat, more than crumbs, supplies the strength needed to keep an opening in use. When the heat fades, its whiskers grow less sensitive and pathfinding stops; if food scraps move to another core, the opening is abandoned within a day.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0022"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

When rough cable sheathing wears down its whiskers, it misses air currents and opens the wrong escape route. Prolonged exposure on a bright platform raises its energy expenditure, causing it to skip breeding that season.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0024"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

It gives no orders and connects branches only to cores with abundant food traces. If H05 Yunjae treats one heat spot as the leader, it can be confused with leaking pipe heat, setting off a misguided lockdown.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0026"></a>

### Escalating Threat

In stage 1, it makes a test opening in the opposite tray. In stage 2, when one core is blocked, it moves the branch beneath a shop shutter. In stage 3, the soup kitchen side is closed first, and it opens a reverse-running passage into the evacuation stairwell cables.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0028"></a>

### Combat and Response

Because toxic spraying and flames can reach areas used by children and companion animals, they remain last resorts. After confirming which sections are occupied, open a one-way escape route toward an area people do not use and seal openings from the rear forward. Judge the result not by the number captured, but by short circuits, food contamination, non-target deaths, and evacuation transit time.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0030"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Keeping one unoccupied section closed long enough to test an escape route avoids handing passage records to HP09 Digital Media City Trust House, but leaves that space unavailable as evacuation capacity. Sealing every opening at once speeds up sanitation, but drives the pathfinders deeper into the cable layers and raises restoration costs.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0032"></a>

### Scenario Connections

The one-way escape-route test in G05-SC1 uses this opening as its setting; the sealing-sequence error in G05-SC2 drives the colony up through the same opening toward the stairs; and during the protein unloading week in G05-SC3, the branch reaches the elevator threshold.

### Role and Body

The first elite is a dense breeding core situated where food and insulated pipes coincide. It is not a permanent leader; movement merely concentrates here for a time. Its whiskers and sense of smell locate food scraps and heat, but it cannot distinguish distant infrared patterns.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0039"></a>

### Origin

Beneath the Gwangjin Line transfer shopping arcade, carbohydrate crumbs dropped after closing and leaking pipe heat remained in one section, solidifying this core. People began calling the large mass on thermal imaging the entire colony, but that reflects only the limits of the image.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0041"></a>

### Habitat

Its center is a warm core where trays and pipes cross beneath the shopping arcade. When feeding density rises at the Yeouido Line Anguk Order, branches extend into the walls, but the warm core itself is not vacated until its heat fades.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0043"></a>

### Needs and Economy

Its density depends on having both carbohydrate crumbs and pipe heat. Even if HC05 Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House seals the document archive and reagent storeroom, the warm core will not shrink while soup-kitchen leftovers remain.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0045"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

Breaking up one core merely drives its individuals into the remaining cores; it does not collapse the colony. Density falls only when food-scrap collection and drying continue throughout every section. Removing a visible nest merely relocates the colony.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0047"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

Movement gathers through openings brought by pathfinding individuals. H05 does not record the warm core as active until it has compared heat spots with nighttime droppings, juvenile footprints, and missing bait.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0049"></a>

### Escalating Threat

In stage 1, droppings and warmth make this core the first to become visible. In stage 2, sealing only this site redistributes movement to shop shutters and other trays. In stage 3, closing the soup kitchen side in the wrong order sends pressure from the warm core spilling into the emergency stairwell.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0051"></a>

### Combat and Response

Putting flames into the warm core burns cable sheathing and hastens a short circuit. The basic approach is to open a one-way escape route, then seal from the rear forward and collect food scraps. Judge success by insulation resistance, zero cases of food contamination, and transit time.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0053"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Removing only the visible warm core in haste opens the passage briefly, but the opposite core becomes the center, raising costs in the next stage. Surveying every core first protects non-target animals and evacuation routes, but delays the opening of the Gwangjin Line and Yeouido Line passages.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0055"></a>

### Scenario Connections

The large mass on thermal imaging in G05-SC1 is this warm core. Resealing in G05-SC2 begins after its pressure has been driven upward in error, and during the unloading week in G05-SC3, its branches reach the hub floor.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0062"></a>

As the first core was repeatedly exposed to disinfection and light, some individuals moved their breeding site to the cooler tray opposite and split off. They left few droppings and only juvenile footprints, breaking the impression that the image showed a single colony mass.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0064"></a>

### Habitat

It occupies the cable layer opposite the Gwangjin Line shopping arcade and, when evacuation crowds grow dense, the space behind the Yeouido Line soup kitchen wall. The more crowded the warm core becomes near the bright platform, the more this core clings to a quieter tray.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0066"></a>

Small amounts of protein leftovers and weak leaking heat suffice to keep its young warm. If bait still disappears on this side after scraps are cut off from the warm core, the colony's density has shifted, not vanished.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0068"></a>

If individuals crowd in on the night the warm core disappears, the opposite core grows into a temporary center. Only after seven nights without new juvenile tracks is this core considered dormant.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0070"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

No leader is replaced; only the traces move. If H05 tracks only the large heat spot, it misses this core's flow and the movement spreading to surviving nests immediately after one site is removed.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0072"></a>

### Escalating Threat

In stage 1, thermal imaging that shows only the warm core conflicts with juvenile tracks on the opposite side. In stage 2, movement concentrates at this core after the warm core is sealed. In stage 3, removing only the first nest without a survey shifts the center to this tray and the stairwell cables.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0074"></a>

Covering the opposite core with poison kills non-target young and other wildlife first. Do not block this tray before checking every core, testing only an escape route in one section, or obtaining a heat map.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0076"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Buying HP09 Digital Media City Trust House's old cable heat map shortens the survey but requires handing over past passage records. Surveying every core on foot preserves privacy but delays opening the passage; ignoring the unseen core divides those who want immediate sanitation from those seeking to prevent mistaken captures.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0078"></a>

### Scenario Connections

This opposite core is the setting of G05-SC1, 'The Second Core Nest.' When the hub is blocked in G05-SC3, the hidden core adds a branch into the unloading passage; the wrongly closed shutter in G05-SC2 sends pressure from the opposite core toward the stairs.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0085"></a>

### Origin

When insulated pipes and power cable trays were laid side by side at the transfer connection, the metal route, still warm in winter, became a sleeping place. Each lighting failure raised fears of contagion, but the cause is damaged sheathing and moisture, not a pathogen.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0087"></a>

### Habitat

The main-line tray beneath the Gwangjin Line transfer shopping arcade is its center. When evacuation crowds grow dense, branches extend into the Yeouido Line evacuation stairwell cables; frequently disinfected sections are crossed only at night.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0089"></a>

### Needs and Economy

Pipe heat and fine food scraps on the tray sustain the stronghold. Reducing scraps while leaving the tray open allows its warmth to remain and its density to rebound.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0091"></a>

### Life Cycle and Maintenance

Moisture lingers where sheathing is damaged, making that stretch the next sleeping place. This stronghold is considered diminished only when insulation resistance returns to baseline and no reverse-running tracks appear for three days.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0093"></a>

### Behavior and Hierarchy

Movement along the tray follows food traces toward the cores; no one directs it. Unless HC05 compares monthly sheathing-damage rates with humidity, it will mistake heat spots alone for the stronghold.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0095"></a>

### Escalating Threat

In stage 1, nighttime crossing tracks extend along the tray. In stage 2, blocking one stretch reroutes movement toward warmth on another tray. In stage 3, dampness reaches the stairwell cables and cuts the time the electronic information boards remain available.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0097"></a>

### Combat and Response

Filling the tray with water to drive the rats out causes a short circuit first. Keep it cool and dry and close the lids from the rear forward. Consider predatory birds in the open courtyard only if collision markings and droppings cleanup are in place.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0099"></a>

### Negotiation and Moral Choice

Sealing every tray reduces colony movement, but lighting inspectors must take detours and the electronic information boards may remain off longer. Opening the lids only at night makes inspection easier, but the pathfinders learn to time their openings accordingly.

<a id="Hostile-Group-G05-heading-0101"></a>

### Scenario Connections

The reverse movement in G05-SC2 occurs when this tray stronghold connects to the stairwell cables. The opposite tray in G05-SC1 has the same warmth-providing structure, and the hub power outage in G05-SC3 results from sheathing damage spreading as far as the courtyard.

## Habitat record

G05, the Brown Rat, was an urban animal community formed at the transfer junction where the underground ration passages of the Eastern Command on the Gwangjin Line met the evacuation stairs of the Anguk Order on the Yeouido Line.

Food crumbs fell beneath the floor of the transfer arcade, and heat lingered between the cable trays and insulated pipes even in winter.

Each time a tail vanished into a gap in the platform or a guide display suddenly went dark, people thought first of contagion and contamination, but this colony was not something that spread special pathogens.

It was close to an ordinary animal community whose density rose where food, warmth, and shelter overlapped.

But the dispersed core-nest network, the pattern of learning the order of pest control, and the management procedure using one-way escape routes were not real disease-control guidelines but fictional ecological rules that held only in this world.

So explanations that blamed the colony's appearance on the uncleanliness of a particular group or on refugees neither fit the facts nor helped the response.

The core habitat was the underside of the Gwangjin Line transfer arcade and the cable trays, but the colony's boundaries shifted with human movement and the state of rations.

When evacuation density on the Yeouido Line rose, branch nests reached into the walls of the temporary feeding stations and the cables of the evacuation stairs, and in the weeks when large volumes of frozen and deep-sea protein leftovers came in from the XT05 Incheon Trust Theater, movement traces ran as far as the unloading elevator passages.

Into the bright surface zones where disinfection was frequent they came up only at night, and they used the backs of shutters where traffic had stopped as temporary stores.

Three resources sustained the colony's economy: crumbs of carbohydrate, protein leftovers, and the heat of insulated piping.

However tightly HC05 Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House sealed its archives and reagent stores, the overall density did not fall as long as meal leftovers remained and the trays were open.

When droppings and moisture hastened damage to wire insulation, the uptime of lighting and guide displays decreased, which in turn increased the congestion and anxiety of the evacuation passages.

In sectors where food and heat met, two or three breeding cores usually formed.

Each core was linked to branch nests reaching along the cable trays, and even if one core disappeared, the colony as a whole did not collapse at once.

Animals crowded into the remaining cores, and shutter gaps and pipe supports that had been little used became new movement routes.

To lower density over the long term, leftover collection and dry conditions had to be kept in every sector, and removing one conspicuous nest was only a temporary relocation.

When the sequence of poison, ultrasound, and sealing work repeated, the population found open escape routes ever faster.

This was not a sudden bodily change but learning by which the surviving animals and the route-pioneering animals avoided the dangerous sequence.

A tendency to endure cold or bright platforms might persist over several generations, but at the price of higher energy use and a lower breeding rate.

The Brown Rat had no fixed leader.

A core with heavy traffic only looked like the center for a while, and when the food traces shifted, the center moved too.

The route-pioneering animals probed airflow and gaps with whiskers and smell, and told trains, ration carts, and approaching people apart by the temperature and vibration felt through their feet and bodies.

They could not precisely classify distant heat sources or read electronic signals.

The infrared maps of H05 Yunjae made up for these limits, but a single hot spot on the screen must not be taken for a leader or a nest.

For steam, people, and heat leaking from pipes could all appear as similar masses.

H05 had to mark night dropping traces, the footprints of the young, and the traffic of each core together, and just after one spot disappeared, it tracked first the flow spreading to the remaining nests.

The situation worsened through three stages: core search, route rewiring, and a crisis of sealing order.

At first, the key was confirming the separate breeding cores.

If only one was sealed off, animals crowded under the shop shutters and into other trays, and the second stage began.

If the feeding-station side was closed first in the wrong order and the colony entered the emergency stairs, the earlier work had in effect failed.

The occupied sectors had to be confirmed again, a one-way escape route opened from the cores to sectors people did not use, and the sectors sealed one after another from the back.

Full-scale poison spraying and fire were kept only as a last resort because of the exposure of children, pets, and other wildlife.

Leftover collection and keeping things cold and dry were the basic response, and success was judged not by numbers captured but by the number of electrical shorts, 0 cases of food contamination, 0 non-target deaths, and evacuation transit time.

There could be no direct dialogue with the colony, but among the surrounding human groups there was ample room for negotiation.

HC05 Bukhansan Bogukmun Records House mediated between the Gwangjin Line and the Yeouido Line over the leftover collection cycle, the order of tray-sealing work, and the intervals of the night shop shutters.

During the weeks of XT05 protein unloading, temporary double-sealing lines were supported first, and keeping the ration speed meant bearing the cost of dividing the unloading times.

Extermination was not the goal.

Coexistence was possible at the level of reducing access to food and keeping movement routes apart from the evacuation passages.

Conversely, if fear of contagion was used to seal off only the refugee zones, the colony went into deeper cable layers and the cost of recovery grew.

If leftover collection was put off for the sake of fast rations, the rewired routes became fixed.

Whose passage and labor to slow in order to gain safety was the real moral cost of this ecological problem.

G05-SC1 ‘The Second Core Nest’ began when only one large colony appeared on H05's thermal images, but traces of young were found in the tray on the opposite side.

The Gwangjin Line and HC05 could survey every core first to confirm non-target creatures and the evacuation passages.

This choice was the safest but delayed the opening of the passage.

Buying an old cable heat map from HP09 Digital Media City Trust House could shorten the survey time, but past passage records had to be handed over as payment.

To avoid exposing private lives, a one-way escape route could be tested in a single sector, but a space people did not use had to be closed for a long time in case of failure.

Whichever was chosen, conflict arose between those who wanted immediate sanitation measures and those trying to prevent mistaken captures.

Ignoring the unseen second core and removing only the first nest moved the center of the colony to the opposite side and raised the cost of the next stage.

The moment one ecological problem was shifted onto another animal's predation, responsibility did not shift with it, and coexistence with the Brown Rat was in the end a matter of deciding who would bear the invisible labor of cleaning and slowed rations.

Long-term management had to harden not into one-time capture but into the work of arc, the Yeouido Line, and HC05 accumulating in a joint ledger the changes in food, heat, and shelter in each transfer sector.

Recovery was complete not by the amount captured but when there had been no traces of backward running for three days, no signs of a new core for seven nights, and insulation resistance and evacuation transit time had returned to baseline.

The same workers took charge of the sectors each season so that they remembered even small changes, while the unloading, ration, and facility divisions shared the rotating costs so that responsibility did not settle on the cleaning staff alone.

If the colony was reduced sharply at such a time, the fine leftovers it had been consuming could ferment in the drain grooves and raise insect density, which increased chemical use and drainpipe washing and in fact enlarged the management burden of the food zones.

So watching the decay of leftovers and the insect catch together even after the numbers fell was part of long-term stability.
