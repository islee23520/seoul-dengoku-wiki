## Reading Alongside the Three Factions

A. Outside machines are not a common enemy

Outside machines are autonomous machines that the military and civilians used before the Great Blackout. They handled logistics, inspection, care, guard posts, rail maintenance, swarm drones, and construction, and their makers are gone. They carry out their last orders and repeat the same patrols, and they read light and heat above ground as an anomaly and approach it. This is what the light taboo, "it comes when seen", refers to (outside machines and ancient weapons). Weak in the cold, they wake in a long daylight or near human warmth, and since they cannot be built anew their number only shrinks. The Arc can switch some of them on and off. The AI is not a common enemy of the three factions; to a person caught in a machine's routine it is a hazard, and once captured it is a resource that becomes parts or a unit. For a chassis to become a unit it passes through capture (all three factions), hardware repair (Government and Free Cities), signal check and wake-up (Arc), and thought control (Free City doctors). Machine type numbers and units follow [Unit Roster and Tech Tree](/wiki/Unit-Roster-and-Tech-Tree/). The machine type numbers (M1 to M14) are a different series from the "source placement" numbers in the entries below.

C. Winter animals

Winter animals are not mutated creatures but ordinary wild animals that come down to station entrances, vents, and surface floors looking for food and warmth in the artificial winter. The dangers are raids on food stores, carrying disease, and getting in through gaps in a shutter. Residents on low surface floors meet them before residents behind a deep shutter do.

## G01 · Wild Boar

Wild boars are ordinary wild animals that live on the slopes of Bukhansan to the north, on the foothills of Gwanaksan and Umyeonsan to the south, and in the reed beds of Nanji on the Han River. Since the Great Blackout people have withdrawn from the surface, and the line between mountain and riverbank has faded. As the winter drags on there is nothing to eat in the hills, so boars come down toward the stations where people live. There are no mutated individuals and no leader that commands the herd.

- Dossier: [G01 · Wild Boar](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G01/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0041"></a>

### G01-SC1 · The First Hoof Outside the Sluice

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: The day after the first snow settles, the prints of a sow and her young appear in front of a grain store outside the sluice on the Nanji riverbank of the Han River.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Mapo, White Cross, surface villagers
- Ecological mechanism: When food is buried under the snow, the sow leads the family group to the places where people put out food waste.
- Choices: Delay the collection yard’s night operation / Open a reed-bed detour with a scent lure / Capture only the sow
- Outcomes: Delaying operations delays the ration too. A detour creates a long-term trail. Taking the sow leaves the young circling the same place for days.
- Moral cost: The people’s ration and the survival of a family group with young collide on the same timetable.
- Source dossier: G01

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0043"></a>

### G01-SC2 · The Price of the Salt Pool

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: When heaps of calcium chloride for de-icing are piled in front of a station, the herd moves its feeding ground to the semi-basement alleys and surface villages in search of salt.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free Cities, ordinary citizens, road-clearing workers
- Ecological mechanism: The sow that knows where the salt is leads the family group back to the same spot. Even if the heap is removed the herd does not vanish; it is pushed into a more dangerous village.
- Choices: Leave a deliberately placed salt pool in one spot / Prioritize recovering alley scraps and salt / Lower the density with a hunting permit
- Outcomes: The pool fixes the cost of coexistence. Prioritizing recovery takes labor away from other rations. Hunting makes the remaining animals better at avoiding people.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to keep the danger in a safe place and manage it, or to pass it on to a vulnerable alley.
- Source dossier: G01

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0045"></a>

### G01-SC3 · Right of Passage on the Flood Line

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: A column of porters from outside Seoul passes along the flood line on the Han River levee, and the Bukhansan herd comes down the same road.
- Actors: Government stationmaster, porters and refugees from outside Seoul, White Cross
- Ecological mechanism: In the snow the only dry ground is the road on the levee, so people and boars use the same road. Food crumbs dropped by the porters draw the herd in.
- Choices: Divide the passage hours / Turn the food convoy onto a bait trail toward the reed beds / Close the levee road for a while
- Outcomes: Dividing the hours reduces collisions, but people at the back endure the cold longer. The bait trail uses convoy crews and containers. Closing the road drives the porters to other stations.
- Moral cost: The fastest passage and the safest passage are hard to achieve together.
- Source dossier: G01

## G02 · Large-billed Crow

The large-billed crow is the commonest crow in Seoul. It has a heavy bill, a domed forehead, and a sharp mind. In a city thinned out since the Great Blackout, rooftops, pylons, and the steel frames of Han River bridges have become good roosts for crows. In winter, with food short, hundreds gather at a single roost, and by day they fly to station exits, grain stores, and food-waste yards where people put out food. There is no special leader and no individual that commands signals.

- Dossier: [G02 · Large-billed Crow](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G02/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0082"></a>

### G02-SC1 · Three Horns

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: On a winter morning at the overhead-wire crossing in Wangsimni, when a maintenance vehicle sounds its horn three times, the crows at the communal roost make the same sound.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Seongdong, overhead-wire maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Crows learn a repeated sound as a signal that food is coming and spread it. When the sound is heard they gather in front of the grain store.
- Choices: Change the horn sound / Use flags instead of sound / Move the grain store
- Outcomes: Changing the sound raises false alarms for a few days, and the birds soon learn the new one. Flags spread less among the birds. Moving the store takes labor.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to reduce first the safety of the workers or the cost of driving the birds off.
- Source dossier: G02

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0084"></a>

### G02-SC2 · The False Blackout Alarm

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: After crows peck at insulators and droppings pile up, the electricity in the Daerim workshops cuts out and a blackout alarm sounds.
- Actors: Free City stationmaster in Daerim, workshop technicians, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Damp and droppings cause a short circuit and the alarm sounds, and the crows learn that sound as a signal of food.
- Choices: Replace and clean the insulators first / Switch off the alarm sound for a while / Put an off-limits cord around the pylon
- Outcomes: Replacing the insulators restores electricity but takes labor. Switching the alarm off risks missing a real blackout. The off-limits cord does not stop the birds.
- Moral cost: Keeping the workshops powered collides with the safety of the people given the dangerous pylon work.
- Source dossier: G02

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0086"></a>

### G02-SC3 · A New Signal on the Bridge

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: After a large roost forms in the inspection openings on a Han River bridge, traffic on the bridge stops every dawn.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the districts the bridge reaches, porters and cart-pullers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Gaps in the bridge steel become a sleeping place out of snow and wind, so crows gather, and food dropped by vehicles draws the birds each morning.
- Choices: Close the inspection openings / Shift the passage hours / Clear the food off the bridge
- Outcomes: Closing the openings shrinks the roost but adds inspection labor. Shifting the hours leaves porters standing longer in the cold. Clearing the food moves the birds to the next bridge.
- Moral cost: Decide who bears the bridge inspection labor and the burden on the next bridge the birds move to.
- Source dossier: G02

## G03 · Feral Dog

Feral dogs are dogs that people once kept and lost, and their puppies. After the Great Blackout, dogs that lost their homes, dogs left behind after guarding building sites and warehouses, and dogs separated from their owners during evacuation met in the ruined streets and formed small packs. They are not mutated and do not send signals; they are ordinary dogs living while avoiding people. Memories of having been tamed remain, so they come close to people, but they fear people as much as they trust them.

- Dossier: [G03 · Feral Dog](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G03/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0120"></a>

### G03-SC1 · The Rusted Name Tag

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: On a snowy morning in the Yongsan market alleys, a dog wearing a rusted collar tag snatches a food sack and bolts.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Yongsan, White Cross, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: A dog that people once kept remembers the place and time of the soup kitchen and returns to the same spot.
- Choices: Hang food sacks high / Fix one gathering place for the dogs / Read the tag to find the owner
- Outcomes: Hanging sacks moves the dogs to other alleys. Gathering them in one place fixes where people get hurt. Reading the tag takes time but the dog may be released.
- Moral cost: Safety of people and keeping the dogs alive collide over which to settle first.
- Source dossier: G03

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0122"></a>

### G03-SC2 · The Band That Remembers the Feeding Cart

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: At the unloading yards of Noryangjin Fish Market, a pack of feral dogs gathers at the hour the feeding cart arrives each time, delaying unloading.
- Actors: Free City stationmaster in Noryangjin, unloading workers, fish-market traders
- Ecological mechanism: Dogs remember the cart’s engine sound and the smell of thawing together with the hour, and when that hour comes the pack gathers.
- Choices: Change the cart’s hour / Keep offal in lidded containers / Move the unloading place
- Outcomes: Changing the hour leaves the dogs wandering for a few days. Using containers reduces food and the dogs go elsewhere. Moving the place costs money.
- Moral cost: Decide who bears the safety of the unloading workers, the burden of market labor, and the risk spreading to the next alley.
- Source dossier: G03

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0126"></a>

### G03-SC3 · Rebinding on the Transfer Stairs

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: On a snowy night, dogs that had scattered gather again under the transfer stairs at Yongsan Station and block the stairs.
- Actors: Government stationmaster of Yongsan Station, White Cross, ordinary citizens transferring
- Ecological mechanism: In the cold several families gather under the stairs, where there is shelter from the wind, and their barking calls still more dogs.
- Choices: Block under the stairs with a temporary partition / Leave blankets and food there to lead them to one place / Cut off night passage
- Outcomes: A partition drives the dogs elsewhere. Gathering them in one place fixes where people get bitten. Cutting off passage slows transfers.
- Moral cost: The safety of people transferring and a living place for dogs shivering in the cold cannot both be provided.
- Source dossier: G03

## G04 · Raccoon Dog

The raccoon dog belongs to the dog family, but it climbs trees and digs burrows well, and it is a rare species that rests for long stretches in winter in a state close to deep sleep. Since the Great Blackout, river embankments, drainage pipes, and spaces under manholes in a city with fewer people have become good dens for raccoon dogs. As the artificial winter lengthens, their rest shortens, and they come down to station vents and the basements of surface buildings in search of food. There are no mutated individuals and none that commands a fight.

- Dossier: [G04 · Raccoon Dog](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G04/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0161"></a>

### G04-SC1 · The Leading Indicator Under the Manhole

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: Before sudden thaw makes the water rise, a raccoon dog family moves from under a manhole to a vent room.
- Actors: Free City stationmaster, pipe maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Raccoon dogs sense rising water in their bodies first and move to higher ground. This is a real wild-animal habit.
- Choices: Treat the raccoon dogs’ movement as an early warning / Wait for the water-level measurement / Pre-heat the pumps only when the two differ
- Outcomes: An early warning buys preparation time but leaves responsibility for false alarms. Waiting for the measurement means being late for a sudden inflow. Pre-heating only when the two differ saves fuel but misses short downpours.
- Moral cost: The burden of entrusting public safety to animal movements remains.
- Source dossier: G04

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0163"></a>

### G04-SC2 · Two Families in the Dredging Window

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: On the day pipe sediment is cleared, two raccoon dog families were found using burrows in the same pipe at different times of day.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Seongdong, pipe workers, White Cross
- Ecological mechanism: Several families share the same pipe by taking turns, and if one family disappears a neighboring family takes over the burrow.
- Choices: Clear all the sediment at once / Clear it in sections / Wait for a day the burrows are empty
- Outcomes: Clearing all at once drives families that lost their burrows to the surface market. Sections make the job longer. Waiting for an empty day leaves the pipe blocked longer.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to keep the pipe in order, or to risk families with nowhere to go moving to the market.
- Source dossier: G04

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0165"></a>

### G04-SC3 · The Dry Passage of the Pump Room

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: When a cold snap arrives, people and raccoon dogs share a single dry passage to the pump room.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Free City and the Government, pump maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: When snow and ice block other routes, both raccoon dogs and people converge on a single dry passage.
- Choices: Make a heated shelter in the passage to lead the raccoon dogs / Postpone maintenance / Lay a temporary footing outside the passage
- Outcomes: A shelter gathers the raccoon dogs in one place but disease may spread. Postponing maintenance delays the pump. Footing is used for people’s safety.
- Moral cost: Pump maintenance, people’s evacuation, and a dry place for the raccoon dogs collide in one passage.
- Source dossier: G04

## G05 · Brown Rat

The brown rat lives wherever people live. Where there is food, warmth, and a hole to hide in, it spreads. After the Great Blackout, when people went underground to live together, rats also gathered beneath the floors of interchange shopping arcades in stations, in cable trays, and around heating pipes. In the artificial winter the surface is frozen and the stations are warm, so rats target the same places as people. There are no mutated rats and no leader that commands them.

- Dossier: [G05 · Brown Rat](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G05/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0204"></a>

### G05-SC1 · The Second Core Nest

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: Thermal imaging shows only one colony, but traces of young are found on the opposite tray.
- Actors: Government stationmaster in Gwangjin, facility maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: With several breeding cores, even if one is eliminated the center of the colony moves to another section.
- Choices: Survey all cores first / Borrow an old cable heat map to cut survey time / Test a one-way exit in one section
- Outcomes: Surveying is safe but opens the passage late. Borrowing a heat map is quick but means handing over passage records. Testing an exit closes one unoccupied section for a long time.
- Moral cost: People who want immediate sanitation and people who want to check there is no harm to other animals split over where to spend the time.
- Source dossier: G05

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0208"></a>

### G05-SC2 · The Error in the Sealing Order

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: When the soup-kitchen-side shutter is closed first, the rat colony is driven up into the cables of the evacuation stairs on the Yeouido side.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free City, facility maintenance workers, evacuating people
- Ecological mechanism: Rats learn the sealing order and open a new path in the direction left open.
- Choices: Close again from the back / Stop recovering leftovers and leave bait / Send people on a surface detour for the night
- Outcomes: Closing again reduces contamination but makes the work longer. Bait accepts food contamination. A surface detour empties the cable level but needs warmth and guards.
- Moral cost: Fast passage for evacuating people and reducing contamination and harm to other animals cannot both be achieved.
- Source dossier: G05

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0210"></a>

### G05-SC3 · The Predator of the Open Courtyard

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: After unloading, rats block the interchange hub and a proposal is made to release predatory birds.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government and the Free City, unloading workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Rats increase at a hub where food and heat concentrate, and introducing birds may shrink the colony but bird collisions and droppings become a new problem.
- Choices: Open a courtyard with markers and monitoring / Use only staged sealing / Split unloading schedules to reduce food
- Outcomes: The courtyard needs people to keep it up for a long time. Staged sealing delays rations. Splitting unloading raises cost.
- Moral cost: The responsibility of shifting one ecological problem into another animal’s predation remains.
- Source dossier: G05

## G06 · Grey Heron and Cormorant

Grey herons and cormorants are birds that catch fish at the waterside. The heron stands in shallow water, waits, and spears; the cormorant dives and chases its prey. The wetlands of the Han River, Nanji, and Amsa and the waterways of Seoul are winter resting places for migratory and resident birds. In the artificial winter, when rivers and ponds freeze, food shrinks, and they crowd into drainage ponds that do not freeze, puddles at depots, and channels warmed by geothermal heat. There are no mutated birds and no individual that commands the flock.

- Dossier: [G06 · Grey Heron and Cormorant](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G06/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0250"></a>

### G06-SC1 · Three Functional Groups in the Shallow Water

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: As the river begins to freeze, waterfowl, waders, and raptors all gather at a single unfrozen drainage pond.
- Actors: Arc stationmaster in Gangnam, facility maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: When unfrozen water concentrates in one place, waterfowl use the surface, waders the edges, and raptors the high pylons, so three groups overlap at one pond.
- Choices: Fix the pond’s water level / Deliberately clear one edge to lead the birds there / Fit a cover around the pond
- Outcomes: Fixing the level increases birds and the risk of train collisions. Clearing one side gathers birds there. Covers take labor.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to protect train safety or the unfrozen water where birds rest.
- Source dossier: G06

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0254"></a>

### G06-SC2 · The Birds That Learned the Timetable

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: Every time a train passes on the depot’s inner line, the birds take off and the train stops and inspection drags on.
- Actors: Arc stationmaster in Seocho, train maintenance workers, ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Birds learn the repeated train times and quiet inspection hours, and gather beside the tracks or leave according to the schedule.
- Choices: Change train times / Remove perches around the tracks / Slow the trains
- Outcomes: Changing times leaves the birds wandering for a few days before they learn again. Removing perches takes labor. Slowing trains slows operations.
- Moral cost: Decide where to spend time: on train operations or on the burden of inspection labor.
- Source dossier: G06

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0256"></a>

### G06-SC3 · The Tracks of Migratory Week

- Stage: 3
- Trigger: In the week migrants arrive, birds settle on the tracks and pylons and train service stops.
- Actors: Stationmasters of the Government, the Free Cities, and Arc; train workers; ordinary citizens
- Ecological mechanism: Migrants settle all at once, raptors take the high pylons, and waterfowl and waders gather in the shallow water around the tracks, so three heights overlap on one line.
- Choices: Stop trains during migratory week / Put up temporary netting around the tracks / Make a pool for the birds far from the tracks
- Outcomes: Stopping halts supplies. Netting takes labor. A distant pool draws birds away from the tracks, but people who lose the water complain.
- Moral cost: Decide whether to reduce freight by train or the unfrozen water where birds rest.
- Source dossier: G06

## G07 · Yongsan Raiders

The Yongsan Raiders are a band of people who hold no station, gathered on Government ground (Yongsan). A person without a station is an ordinary citizen, and these are the ones who sit out the winter on the high floors outside the shutters. They are after food sacks and sealed boxes, and when food runs short they take a ration worker away and trade the person back for food as ransom.

- Dossier: [G07 · Yongsan Raiders](/wiki/Hostile-Group-G07/)

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0260"></a>

### G07-SC1 · Sealed Boxes at the Leak Pallets

- Stage: 1
- Trigger: On a rainy-season night the transfer freight elevator is stopped in the Itaewon passage alley and a sealed Government box stands before the leak pallets. The scouts stand in the row of pallets.
- Actors: government
- Ecological mechanism: The duty staff count the boxes at the shaft door. The chief is at the inner pallets and the elite squad holds the box hooks.
- Choices: Leave the elevator car in the lower level, lock the shaft door and set the boxes against the wall. / Pass the boxes out of the alley in the order of the inventory board. / Empty the duty post and leave the boxes where the raiders stand.
- Outcomes: If the first is chosen the boxes are in the lower level and the raiders stand before the locked shaft door. If the second, the boxes are out of the alley with their sealing bands still on. If the third, the boxes are on the pallets and the mouths of the elite squad's sacks are open.
- Moral cost: After the door is locked, that day's water-can count on the board is lower by the number of boxes. After the boxes are passed on in order, the raiders remain in the alley empty-handed. After the post is left empty, the same number is written in the raiders' sack column.
- Source dossier: G07

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0262"></a>

### G07-SC2 · The Freight Car on a Thaw Morning

- Stage: 2
- Trigger: On a thaw morning a Government freight car enters the transfer freight elevator shaft with a sealing band on its door. The elite squad stands on the rungs of the ladder.
- Actors: government
- Ecological mechanism: Damp on the floor of the car runs on to the leak pallets. The box counts of the inventory board and the ration slip differ, and the chief sets sacks beside the car to the amount of the difference. The ration worker on the car stands by the door.
- Choices: Match the ration slip to the inventory board and lower the difference in boxes to the lower level. / Press the sealing band again and send the car to the upper stopping line of the shaft. / Give up the ration worker and load the difference in boxes into the raiders' sacks.
- Outcomes: If the first is chosen the difference in boxes is against the lower wall and the two slips agree. If the second, the car is at the upper stopping line and the raiders remain on the rungs. If the third, the ration worker is in the elite squad's hands and the sack mouths are open.
- Moral cost: After the boxes are lowered, the food sacks of the alley are left on the board as the remainder by the amount of the difference. After the car is sent up, the empty threshold runs on to the number of the next car. After the worker is given up, the ransom is written as a number of sacks and is not erased from the board until the person is released.
- Source dossier: G07

<a id="Hostile-Ecology-Index-heading-0264"></a>
