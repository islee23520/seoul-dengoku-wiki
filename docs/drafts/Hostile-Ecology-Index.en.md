## G01 · Wild Boar

- Modern anxiety: The anxiety of wild-boar bands coming down after a flood into the lowland scrap yards and the semi-basement alleys, competing with people over refuse and crops
- Fictional origin: Right after the collapse, when the flooding of the western Han River cut the green axis outside the Yeouido and Yeongdeungpo sluices, wild boar herds that came down from the Bukhansan and Anyangcheon corridors settled at the food-waste collection yards on the edge of the Sluice.

Real-world sources supported only the basic ecology of urban incursions, food sources, and blocked movement; the behavior of learning the sealing hours and the relationship with HC01 were fiction.

The cooling-waste heaps sealed by HC01 Yeouido Computing House came to serve the wild boar as a winter salt source.

- Territory: The core habitat was the wetland and waste slopes outside the sluices of the Sluice.

In the dry season and winter they moved along the green fragments toward the Government of the Republic of Korea at central Seoul Station and along the axis of night-market leftovers, and the northwestern edge stirred in response to the food traces of the refugee columns pushed down from the XT01 Imjin River Gate Theater.

When the flood level crossed the sluice warning line, the herd was pushed up the upland stairs step by step and cut across the walkways.

- Economy: The main resources are organic scraps, salt, and shallow-rooted crops, and as secondary resources they use the soft plastic of flood debris and the pools of cooling water.

When the HC01 recovery trough delays the scrap-sealing cycle, the band's density rises, and when the seal is drawn too tight the band diverts to household-alley scraps.

The energy cycle runs from feeding to dung to the wetland microbial film, and unsanctioned hunting for meat and hide, instead of a short-term gain, makes the remaining band more aggressive.

- Life cycle: Before the monsoon rains, family groups split by sow scatter and scout the feeding grounds, and right after a flood they join the high-ground scrap axis for a time.

A family group splits only when the survival rate of the young stays high for two seasons in a row, and when the winter salt sources and the mud baths shrink they choose long-distance movement over breeding.

- Long-term adaptation: Over a short span they learn the positions of fences and the sealing hours, but that memory is not treated as a genetic change.

Across many generations nocturnal activity and avoidance of people can be selected, but heavy capture removes the bold individuals first and causes a reverse selection in which the more secretive individuals remain.

The more secretive they become, the longer the search for a feeding ground takes, and the growth rate of the young falls.

- Senses: They read together the low-frequency vibration of sluice opening and closing and of vehicles, carried through the hooves, and the smells of scraps, people, and disinfectant, and at close range they react to warmth carried on the wind and to the flow of air.

They cannot distinguish a distant heat source or decipher electronic noise.

Only Hannuri's (H01) infrared observation compares the heat sources of people, generators, and wild boars, and on days of heavy rain, wind, and cooling fog a person reads the family groups and the lone individuals again.

- Hierarchy: One adult female with young starts the move first, and the subadults scout the feeding ground and the escape route ahead.

Lone males overlap the band only loosely and do not follow the decision to move.

H01 marks the central individual of a family group and the lone males as separate heat-source clusters, and when the central female disappears the group tracks the young's scent for 48 hours and then disperses by family.

- Escalation 1-3: Stage 1, warning appearance: night tracks increase at the collection yard outside the sluice and on unsanctioned fields, but they do not meet people.

Stage 2, infrastructure disturbance: drains, sealing tape, and the water-supply inspection passages are damaged, and scrap convoys are delayed.

Stage 3, flood-line collision: the rising flood level overlaps refugee leftovers, and family groups occupy the sluice stairs.

At that point a response that is nothing but removal by force scatters the band into the alleys, so only a division of time and a feeding corridor work.

- Combat: Noise screens and flares push back only the scouts, and the main body is turned by a feeding detour, by moving the salt pools, and by path fencing.

On days of heavy rain and wind the error in H01's infrared observation grows, so a human scout party confirms the escape route first.

Culling by firearm scatters the remaining band into the alleys and increases harm to citizens, so under the operating rules it is limited to a last resort.

The measure of success is not carcasses but the number of intrusions into the collection yard and the time until passage over the sluice resumes.

- Negotiation: Through HC01 mediation they trade the scrap sealing hours, the night window for passage over the sluice, and a cultivation buffer strip.

There is no condition of extermination, and capturing the central female is paid for with 48 hours of searching behavior.

The leftovers of the refugee column flowing in from XT01 are taken out by a separate recovery, which reduces the lure on the band.

The agreement document takes the form of an ecology-management log, and it carries no clause assigning blame to the current regime or to a real company.

- Moral cost: Indiscriminate removal collapses wetland purification and downstream vegetation together, and putting scrap sealing ahead of the citizen ration increases alley raiding.

The moment a hunting gain is taken, the passage-safety data is distorted.

Choosing coexistence leaves short-term threat reports behind, but the sluice workers' movement lines are kept.

## G02 · Large-billed Crow

- Modern anxiety: The anxiety of cluster interference, as bands of large-billed crows gather on transmission towers, wires, and bridge railings and touch signal tones, the school run, and evacuation routes at the same time
- Fictional origin: After breeding pairs settled on the factory pylons of the Daerim–Garibong Korean-Chinese corridor and the cables of the Han River bridges, the Large-billed Crow formed as the waste heat of the Yeongdeungpo Line Gauge factories and the sparks of the Guro industrial-park trolley wires gathered night insects.

Real-world sources supported only the breeding-season territorial behavior of large-billed crows and the range of human responses; the rule by which they learned signal interference as alarms and spread it was fiction.

- Territory: The core habitat is the group of factory roofs and pylons at Daerim and Guro on the Yeongdeungpo Line.

Arc 1 sits on top of the Daerim and Garibong Joseonjok corridor, and in the breeding season the flock circles over bridges, school passages, and evacuation stairs.

In the non-breeding season, matching the sea wind and the insect density pushed in from the West Sea Granary Theater (XT02), it moves for a time to the western waterfront.

When wire-repair work begins, part of the band moves along the work basket.

- Economy: The food is insects, leftover market protein, and small carcasses, and for nest material they pick fine wire with the sheath stripped and fiber scrap.

The shorter the cycle on which the Sinjeong Wheel House (HC02) replaces wire sheathing, the less nest material there is and the more territorial attacks increase, and leaving the sheathing untended increases the risk of a short.

The cycle in which droppings contaminate the insulators connects directly to communications uptime.

- Life cycle: In spring a breeding pair defends one pylon, and in the non-breeding season several families join a communal roost.

Repeated safety signals and food rewards, more than nest success, decide the return site the next year, and after a forced removal the young individuals test a new roost on the adjacent school route.

- Long-term adaptation: Sound shapes repeated like three horns, two inspection bells, and a steady transformer start-up are socially learned as food and danger signals and spread at the communal roost.

When the signal changes, false alarms increase for several days, and the flock learns again through a new reward rule.

No genetic change is set, and the more specialized they become in a familiar sound shape, the later they react to an unfamiliar real alarm.

- Senses: By sight they read helmets, rods, and flashlight reflections, and by hearing they distinguish the order of high-voltage noise, horns, and the crying of infants.

They do not decipher the content of an electronic signal; they connect the sounds of sparks and transformers to the appearance of insects and workers as a cue.

Seorin (H02) records the source of a sound shape and the number of repetitions, but whether it is a real alarm is checked by a person against the marks on site.

- Hierarchy: A breeding pair divides nest defense and the carrying of food, and the young helper individuals join the alarm calls and the dives.

A communal roost in the non-breeding season shares only sound shapes and has no command structure.

H02 records nest radius, flight direction, and call order separately, and the human control officer of HC02 decides only whether to stop the work.

When one breeding individual disappears, the adjacent pair tests the empty roost and territorial collisions increase.

- Escalation 1-3: Stage 1, sound-shape gathering: scout individuals on the pylons and railings follow the horns and the inspection bells.

Stage 2, alarm contamination: food signals and safety alarms mix, and dives on workers, detours off the school route, and transmission stoppages chain together.

Stage 3, signal renegotiation: when false alarms accumulate three times at a bridge and a transmission inspection hatch, the repair hours, a new inspection bell, and the residents' alarm custom have to be reset together, not the nests torn down.

- Combat: Instead of the shortest straight breakthrough, the movement line is bent with sight-blocking cloth without nets, an umbrella column, and alternate stairs.

During the wire-repair hours, insect lure lamps and the inspection bell are moved to another pylon to separate the learned signals.

Gunshots and drone pursuit push the band into the residential alleys and make the flock distrust the alarm sound shapes themselves.

There is no condition of extermination. The custom by which residents read an intruder from an alarm call is left in place, but a nest on the wire is moved only with a relocation rod.

Putting transmission recovery ahead of the ecology lengthens the night blackout stretches and enlarges conflict with the other houses.

## G03 · Feral Dog

- Modern anxiety: The anxiety of bands of abandoned and neglected dogs, with the remains of iron collars and identification tags, moving through transfer passages and vacant lots while marking territory
- Fictional origin: After the collapse, animals separated from private shelters, construction-site guard dogs, and crowd evacuations joined together between the vacant lots outside the Yongsan Line and the night loading docks of the Noryangjin Line and became the Feral Dog.

Their iron collar rings and corroded tags strike one another as they walk.

- Territory: The core habitat is the vacant lot by the outer wall of the laboratory on the Yongsan Line and the closed guard passage, and depending on the season and the unloading cycle they move to the fishery and frozen-leftover axis of the Noryangjin Line.

When the logistics vibration from the Korea Strait Theater (XT03) grows, the band extends along the unloading-yard entrance, and in the daytime hours when the density of people is high they hide in drains and under the elevated roads.

- Economy: Leftover protein, fish guts, and discarded ration packs are the main resources, and scraps of collar, wire, and identification tag are markers that connect individual identification to the memory of a past feeding station.

When the Magok Life House (HC03) strengthens the sealing of waste protein, the band seeps into the market alleys, and when it loosens the seal, concentration at the unloading yard rises and contact with people increases.

Water that has been filtered and disinfected and then stored sealed so it is not recontaminated, and shaded resting places, are the upper limit of density.

- Life cycle: A lone wandering individual finds the companions of a past feeding station by smell and by the sound of identification tags and makes a small family, and when unloading leftovers increase, several families rebind into a loose alliance.

When the feeding schedule stabilizes, territorial defense increases over breeding, and after a forced capture they gather again around the individuals whose identification tags remain.

- Long-term adaptation: They learn human hand signs, the engine sound of the feeding cart, and the sound of each individual's identification tag, and distinguish a safe human from a threat.

Avoidance of people in some young individuals can undergo generational selection, but the setting's rapid changes are explained by social learning and rebinding.

The more finely they distinguish people, the greater the food refusal and the aggression when a particular protector is lost.

- Senses: By smell they told apart human body odor, disinfectant, and the smell of thawing frozen food, and by hearing they read the iron of collars and engine idling.

H03 Ido read the passive UHF RFID tags attached to the collars within about 2 m, but metal collars, wet fur, and tag orientation caused missed and duplicate reads.

Humans used them only as clues to past registration, checked against records of appearance and behavior.

- Hierarchy: One medium-sized adult that has survived the longest, and two adults that go with it, propose a move first, but command is not fixed.

A family with pups keeps a separate protection range, and the sound of the identification tags becomes the marker that rebinds scattered companions.

Even when H03 bundles them as an aggression cluster, the HC03 mediator divides them again by each individual's identification tag, smell, and companionship.

Stage 2, family rebinding: unloading leftovers and a familiar engine sound gather several families into one passage and make them follow the convoy cart.

## G04 · Raccoon Dog

- Modern anxiety: The anxiety of sewer and underground emergence at night, as raccoon family groups occupied storm drains, covered waterways, and underground passages and came up onto the surface
- Fictional origin: When the Jungnang, Tancheon, and inner storm-water trunks were pushed out of the inspection cycle after the collapse, raccoon family groups that had moved following aquatic food, trash, and carcasses settled in the pipelines beside the pumps of the Arc in the Ttukseom Union and in the underground cable galleries of the Yeouido Investment Federation in the Mapo Union, and became the Raccoon Dog.

Real-world sources were consulted only for the range in which raccoons appeared in cities and streams, and the rule of sensing the water level before the pumps was fiction.

- Territory: The core habitat was the pump-house connecting pipes of the Ttukseom Union and the dry refuge cavities under the workshops.

When flood stage rose they dispersed into the Mapo Union's transmission underground galleries and vents, and when a cold wave and a logistics break from the XT04 Duman River Far East Theater changed feed density they moved briefly onto the eastern branch lines.

By day they patrolled the pipes, and by night the surface alleys within 50m.

- Economy: Aquatic prey, organic refuse, and carcass remains were the main resources, and dry hiding places and low flow were the upkeep cost.

When HC04 the Ttukseom Heat-Source House skipped dredging the pipe deposits, the food film thickened and density rose, and when it over-dredged, the family groups were pushed out into the surface markets.

Droppings and biofilm masked the water-quality sensor readings and delayed pump judgment.

- Life cycle: In the dry season each family group used its own dry hiding place and feeding ground, and before the rain they moved their kits into the high ventilation rooms along trunks where the water rose first.

After a flood, when the food film thickened, several families shared the same pipe by time slot, and when hiding places flooded in succession they dispersed into the surface alleys.

- Long-term adaptation: Individuals learned the pressure changes before a pump start and the safe dredging timetable.

Across generations a smaller body and a nocturnal habit favorable to pipe life could be selected, but the more they specialized for narrow pipes, the more vulnerable they became to surface crossings and cold waves.

Moving a hiding place by following a leading indicator is distinguished as family-group learning, not a genetic change.

- Senses: With their whiskers they felt the current and the air moving through narrow gaps, and through pressure changes in their feet and bodies they sensed the water rising before a pump start.

Scent separated food, disinfectant, and rot, and hearing separated the pumps from human footsteps.

H04 Gangbyeol's pipe-robot logs easily confused raccoons, floating debris, and people, so confirmation by human eye was required.

- Hierarchy: Loose family groups and shared hiding places were all there was.

The elder females held only movement priority, and had no combat command.

Several family groups used the same trunk by time division.

When H04 summed them as a single colony, the HC04 drainage watch split them again by hiding place.

- Escalation 1-3: Stage 1, leading pressure signal: before the rain, the family groups moved into the high ventilation rooms.

Stage 2, sensor mismatch: droppings and biofilm masked the official water-level sensors, and inspection robots and family groups collided in the same pipe.

Stage 3, refuge contest: when a cold wave or a flood left only one dry pipe, pump maintenance and family-group refuge demanded the same space, and the dredging timetable and the substitute hiding places had to be rebuilt together.

- Combat: Instead of driving them out with poisoned bait or a full flood, partial flood-barrier closure, substitute refuge pipes, night surface-passage limits, and sensor washing were the baseline.

Culling spoiled the water further through carcass rot.

The success measures were pump-restart recovery time, zero surface bites, and sensor reliability.

- Negotiation: Through HC04 mediation they traded dredging hours, surface-passage ban windows, and substitute refuge pipes.
