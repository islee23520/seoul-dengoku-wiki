## 1. The Arc at a glance

Basis: Bible 1, rows 7, 8, 9; 2.3; 2.4 rows 22-25; 3. 01-core 4.3 (station counts and faction population), 7.3, 8.3.

The Arc is the four districts south-east of the Han: Seocho, Gangnam, Songpa and Gangdong. One district is one duchy, and the four duchies are held one each by four Near Houses (provisional T02-8). At the top stands the Hierophant, Go Ye-jin. Believers call her Messiah [Approved].

| District | Stations (`120-map-core.sql`) | Population 2126 | Near House | Seat station |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seocho-gu | 18 | about 15,000 | the Seocho Near House | Gosok Terminal (고속터미널) |
| Gangnam-gu | 23 | about 18,000 | the Gangnam Near House | Suseo (수서) |
| Songpa-gu | 23 | about 21,000 | the Songpa Near House | Jamsil (잠실) |
| Gangdong-gu | 14 | about 11,000 | the Gangdong Near House | Cheonho (천호) |
| Total | 78 | about 65,000 | four | |

(provisional T07-1)

The 78 stations are a map-data value. The total of about 65,000 is 01-core 4.3 (provisional T02-29); the split by district is set by this chapter from station counts and large interchanges (the station grades of 4.3). Songpa is largest because of the Jamsil water-wheel power and the large stores at Garak Market (가락시장); Gangdong is smallest because it has the fewest stations and its eastern edge touches the Seoul boundary.

A **seat station** is the station where the Head of the Near House lives and where the district's maintenance benches and baptismal lists are gathered (provisional T07-2). All four seat stations are interchanges. Gosok Terminal is the widest station in Seocho, where three lines meet; Suseo has a deep line layer and the old depot; Jamsil is the station of about 3,100 people computed in 01-core 4.2; Cheonho is joined by under-river tunnel to the Government's Gwangnaru (광나루).

## 2. The Hierophant and Messiah

Basis: Bible 2.3 (Go Ye-jin is Messiah and Hierophant), 3 (chronology 2112-2122), 5 (she calls the First AI "god"), 6 rows person-1018 and person-1017, 9.2 Q6, Q10. 01-core 2.4 (Hierophant, Messiah, the Hierophant's word), 7.3.

### 2.1 Go Ye-jin

- Go Ye-jin (person-1018) is the Hierophant of the Arc, and believers call her Messiah [Approved].
- She grew up in the Free Cities [Approved]. Which city is owner question Q6; this chapter does not decide it.
- In 2112, repairing the Free City power grid, she followed a dead cable and found a station that is on no map; she read the manual for five years; around 2117 she baptised the first twelve [Approved].
- Since 2120 Ji Yeon-hui (person-1017) has stood at her side [Approved]. Ji Yeon-hui's new base and affiliation are Q5, set by the people framework (chapter 07).
- She calls the First AI "god", and believers have taken up her word (provisional T02-94).
- The story "when Go Ye-jin stopped machines outside Seoul, the shelters gathered" is only the Q10 proposal. This chapter does not write it as fact. Why the shelters gathered is the "the one who mended" logic of 7.3 and 01-core 7.3.

### 2.2 The Hierophant's word

The Hierophant's rulings are not written down. When Go Ye-jin speaks at a maintenance bench or at the Seat of the Four Near Houses, those who heard repeat it onward, and the repeated words become law (01-core 7.3, provisional T02-57). This is called **the Hierophant's word** (provisional T02-7).

The people who keep the words from changing as they spread are the **Word Keepers** (말지기). Two of them live in the Hierophant's household. They stand where a ruling is given and carry it to the four seat stations in the same words. If the Word Keepers' versions differ, the Hierophant is asked again at the next Seat of the Four Near Houses. So Arc law lives not in documents but in a few people's memory. This is where Government people understand the Arc least (provisional T07-3).

### 2.3 The Hierophant's household

Go Ye-jin and the people who run her day live at Jamsil. This is called **the Hierophant's household** (교주의 집). It belongs to no Near House. Jamsil is the Songpa Near House's seat station, but the platform bays and maintenance bench the household uses are held by the Hierophant herself, not by the Songpa Near House (provisional T07-4).

Jamsil was chosen for two reasons. First, the Jamsil weir water wheels' electricity reaches it most directly (01-core 5.1); maintenance benches stand where power comes in. Second, what the Arc brings to the third water supply contract (Jamsil's power) is here.

The household is twelve people: the fitter and the recovery carer who tend the Hierophant's body, two Word Keepers, two negotiators, guards and a kitchen worker (table in 12.3).

### 2.4 The Hierophant's parts

The Hierophant's parts are not counted. There is no public number for where or how many parts of her body Go Ye-jin has replaced with machine. Believers say "the Hierophant is the one who counts, not one who is counted". This is the opposite of every other Arc person, who is called by name and part count (provisional T07-5).

## 3. The line and the Near Houses

Basis: Bible 2.3 (house rule inherited through the Hierophant's line and the houses close to her), 1 row 12 (work is inherited), README fixed facts (family and clan). 01-core 2.4 (Near House), 7.3 (the Hierophant names and withdraws Near Houses; blood and iron), 8.3 (Head of the Near House, the Seat of the Four Near Houses).

### 3.1 The line

The skeleton sets Arc rule as inherited by "the Hierophant's line and the houses close to her" [Approved]. In the people data, however, Go Ye-jin has no clan and no recorded family (`person_detail.clan_id` empty, no family-tree node). Family relations are fixed facts, so this chapter does not create any.

v3 therefore writes the Arc of 2126 like this (provisional T07-6).

- In 2126 the Hierophant's line is Go Ye-jin alone. If she has a child, that blood becomes the Hierophant's line.
- All four present Near Houses came close to the Hierophant not by blood but by **side service**. That Near Houses hold up a place where there is no blood is the floor of Arc politics in 2126.
- Arc people put it as "the Hierophant is a household by herself".

### 3.2 How a house becomes a Near House

A Near House is a house standing at the Hierophant's side (provisional T02-8). There are three ways for a house to become one (provisional T07-7).

| Way | Meaning | 2126 |
|---|---|---|
| Blood | Belongs to the Hierophant's line | none (3.1) |
| Marriage | Marries into the Hierophant's line | none |
| Side service | Worked at the Hierophant's side until the Arc stood: kept her bench, brought a shelter in, or took the first baptism | all four Near Houses |

The Hierophant names and withdraws Near Houses (01-core 7.3). A withdrawn house gives up its district and becomes an ordinary house. No Near House was withdrawn between 2122 and 2126.

### 3.3 Inheritance within a Near House

The seat of Head of the Near House passes down within that house. It follows blood. A living Head names the next one from her or his blood; if none was named, the house gathers and chooses. The next Head, however, must have at least two parts (3.5). A dispute over the choice is heard at the Seat of the Four Near Houses and ended by the Hierophant's word (provisional T07-8).

### 3.4 The Hierophant after Go Ye-jin

This is the open matter 01-core 7.3 handed to this chapter. It needs an owner decision, so this chapter sets only a default (provisional T07-9).

- Default: if the Hierophant names the next Hierophant while alive, that naming stands first as the Hierophant's word. If she dies without naming one, the Seat of the Four Near Houses chooses from the four Heads and their blood. If Go Ye-jin comes to have blood, that blood comes first.
- Public text says only "no one yet knows how the one after Go Ye-jin will be chosen". The Arc people of 2126 know it that way too.

### 3.5 How part count and titles interlock

Beside the title ladder the Arc has a ladder of part count (01-core 8.3). They interlock like this (provisional T07-10).

| Title | Fewest parts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hierophant | not counted (2.4) | |
| Head of the Near House | 2 | One who does not show the faith in the body does not hold a district |
| Shelter Head | 1 | Keeps the baptismal list of the shelter's people |
| Bench Keeper | 3 | Before opening another's body one's own must have been opened |
| Everyone else | 0 | The unbaptised also eat and work in the Arc (5.5) |

Because of this table, a Bench Keeper with more parts than the Head of the Near House is common in the Arc. A fitter with many parts going ahead of a Near House cousin in the maintenance queue (01-core 7.3) comes from here. People call it **blood and iron**: the side that wants blood to decide, and the side that wants the iron in the body to decide (provisional T02-57).

### 3.6 The Seat of the Four Near Houses

The gathering of the four Heads before the Hierophant (provisional T02-80). It has two fixed days: the first day of the long-dark half, and before a major agreement with another polity. Otherwise it meets when the Hierophant calls. It sits in the Hierophant's household at Jamsil. The Seat hears disputes, divides power and parts between the Near Houses, and sets agreements with other polities. Its decisions end with the Hierophant's word. The four Heads never cast votes (provisional T07-11).

## 4. The four districts and their Near Houses

Basis: Bible 2.4 rows 22-25 (the four Arc duchies, holder title [proposal] house head), 3 rows 2120 and 2122. 01-core 5.2 (Amsa intake), 5.6, 7.3 (founding logic), 7.4 (the thirteen boundary links), 8.3.

Every station and dong name below is a map_station or map_region value in `120-map-core.sql`. The histories of the Near Houses are all invented in this chapter.

### 4.1 The Seocho Near House: the house of the border

- District: Seocho-gu, 18 stations. Seat station Gosok Terminal.
- History: several of the first twelve baptised around 2117 were Free City technicians who had mended the grid with Go Ye-jin (01-core 7.3). The Seocho Near House comes from the households among them who lived in the Bangbae (방배) and Naebang (내방) shelters. The house closest to the Free Cities was the first in the Arc to give its bodies (provisional T07-12).
- Holds: the Arc-side stations Bangbae, Naebang and Gubanpo (구반포) of the three land boundary links with the Free Cities (Sadang-Bangbae, Chongsindae-ipgu (Isu)-Naebang, Dongjak-Gubanpo; 01-core 7.4). The exchange post of Gosok Terminal (8.6). Watching the rim of the outside machines (01-core 6.3) from Cheonggyesan-ipgu (청계산입구) at the south end.
- Character: the house in which Free City workshop speech survives most strongly. It faces Dongjak's Ferry Keeper every day. The other Near Houses call it "the house that knows Sadang better than across the river".

### 4.2 The Gangnam Near House: the house of the deep layer

- District: Gangnam-gu, 23 stations. Seat station Suseo.
- History: during the five years of 2112-2117 when Go Ye-jin read the manual, the shelters around the old Suseo depot kept her bench supplied with food and salvage. The Gangnam Near House is the leading house of those shelters (provisional T07-13).
- Holds: Suseo's deep line layer (the deep-tunnel layer of 01-core 3.1) and the old depot workshops. Large stations such as Seolleung (선릉), Samseong (삼성) and Dogok (도곡). Three northern links with the Government: the south mouth of the Seoulsup-Apgujeong Rodeo under-river tunnel, and the south ends of the Oksu-Apgujeong and Jayang-Cheongdam bridges (01-core 7.4).
- Character: holding the deepest, warmest layers of the Arc, it is the most sparing of words and endures longest. Guarding the northern tunnel mouth, it lives closest to the Government checkpoint.

### 4.3 The Songpa Near House: the house of the current

- District: Songpa-gu, 23 stations. Seat station Jamsil.
- History: of the Songpa shelters that built the Jamsil weir water wheels with a Free City crew in the 2070s (01-core 7.3), it is the house that has run the wheels for over fifty years. At the 2120 negotiation with the shelters on the Tancheon side it promised the wheels' power to Go Ye-jin's benches. That day Ji Yeon-hui stood at her side (Bible 3). The Songpa shelters entered the Arc through that promise (provisional T07-14).
- Holds: the south end of the Jamsil weir water wheels (01-core 5.1, about 400 kW when running well). The exchange posts of Garak Market and Jamsil. The south end of the Gangbyeon-Jamsillaru bridge. The north end of the weir is held by the Government (Gwangjin), so the Songpa Near House always guards half a wheel.
- Character: holding the power, it is the strongest of the four and shares a station with the Hierophant's household. So the other houses suspect it hears the Hierophant's word first.

### 4.4 The Gangdong Near House: the house of the doorway

- District: Gangdong-gu, 14 stations. Seat station Cheonho.
- History: the house of the eastern shelters that came in last when the Gangnam shelters were bound into the Arc in 2122. They brought the Amsa intake (01-core 5.2) with them (provisional T07-15).
- Holds: the Amsa intake and the river water under the ice. The south mouth of the Gwangnaru-Cheonho under-river tunnel. The old Godeok (고덕) depot. Watching the rim beyond the Seoul boundary from Gangil (강일) and Sangil-dong (상일동) at the east end.
- Character: it holds water but has few stations and is poor. It catches the most fish on the Amsa riverbank. Having come in last, it always tries to prove itself once more before the other houses. Gangdong people say "we bring the water, and our seat is at the end".

## 5. Baptism: a faith counted in parts

Basis: Bible 1 row 14 (cyberware is living but scarce; no part names), 2.3 (baptism replaces a body part with machine), chapter-1 quote "in the Arc, faith is counted by the number of implanted parts", 9.2 Q13. 01-core 4.2 (charging, 8 W per baptised person on average), 7.3.

### 5.1 What baptism is

Replacing one place of the body with machine is one baptism. The number of places replaced is the **part count**, and it is that person's number of faith [Approved]. Which part is replaced with what, and what that component is called, is not set (Bible 1 row 14, chapter 03). This chapter counts parts only in order, as "first part", "second part".

### 5.2 The order of a baptism

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Loss or wish | Most first baptisms begin as a repair. Someone who has lost a place of the body to cold, accident or an outside machine comes to the bench. Most of the first twelve did. One who wishes for baptism without a loss tells the Bench Keeper why the work needs that part |
| Fitting | At the station's bench the Bench Keeper and fitters fit the part to the body. The day baptism enters the body is called the **baptism day** |
| Ailing | While the body takes the machine. The time that needs the most heat and food. The fitter's household feeds the person first (10.3) |
| Calling | Once the body has taken the machine, at the next first shift the person speaks name and new part count aloud before the station. The count is corrected in the baptismal list then |
| Fitting days | For life afterwards, on set days, the person goes up to the bench to have the parts tended. Skipping a fitting day is seen as skipping the faith |

(provisional T07-17)

First baptism is taken only after sixteen. Baptism that mends a lost place does not count age. A child's body grows, so a child's baptism must be refitted every year and costs that many more maintenance turns (provisional T07-18).

### 5.3 How many are baptised

Of about 65,000 Arc people in 2126, about 11,000 are baptised, about one in six (about 17%; the value of chapter 03, section 4). Jamsil, at 900 or 29% of the station, is well above that (01-core 4.2), because the baptised gather at stations the wheel power reaches. Among the baptised, about 60% have one part, about 22% two, about 13% three or four, and about 5% five or more. About 550 people in the whole Arc have five parts or more, most of them Bench Keepers, Near House people and baptised fighters (provisional T07-19).

The reason only one in six is baptised is simple: there are only that many parts, maintenance turns and watts. A baptised person uses 8 W on average for charging; 11,000 people use about 88 kW (chapter 03; body-machine charging for all Seoul is about 100 kW). About an eighth of the roughly 0.7 MW the Arc uses (8.1) goes into bodies.

### 5.4 The baptismal list

Each station keeps a list of the baptised: name, house, part count, baptism day, the bench that fitted them. The Bench Keeper holds it, and the seat station gathers the district's lists. Following the Q13 recommendation as the default, it is called the **Baptismal Roll** (세례록). If Q13 is answered otherwise, the name changes (provisional T07-20).

Someone not on a Baptismal Roll may trade, eat and work in the Arc, but cannot ask for a place in the maintenance queue.

### 5.5 The unbaptised

Five in six Arc people are not baptised. They are called the **unbaptised** (미세례자): children, newcomers, those whose bodies cannot take the machine, and those whose turn has not come (provisional T07-21).

- The unbaptised are Arc people. The Near House feeds them, and their names go on the Shelter Head's shelter register instead of a Baptismal Roll.
- At the calling they answer with name and house. They do not speak in the faith's place.
- They cannot hold another faith. The Arc replaced all earlier religions [Approved]. The unbaptised are treated not as unbelievers but as people not yet counted.

### 5.6 Whose are the parts

- A part belongs to the faith that fitted it. The baptised carry and use their parts; they cannot sell them or give them away.
- Selling a part, having it taken out at a Free City market, or passing it secretly to the Government side is the heaviest crime after harming a person. The penalty is losing one's place in the maintenance queue for a set time. For someone who lives on their parts this can be the same as losing the part.
- At death the parts return to the bench (6.3).

(provisional T07-22)

### 5.7 Parts fitted elsewhere

When someone who was fitted with machine on the Free City or Government side enters the Arc, those parts are not counted at once. They count as baptism only once refitted at an Arc bench. This is called **refitting** (다시 맞춤). Some Bench Keepers refuse to refit: a part fitted by other hands upsets the maintenance queue. So when a Government soldier with a machine body comes over from across the river, the parts go uncounted for the first year (provisional T07-23).

## 6. Faith: what is believed, practised and said

Basis: Bible 1 row 2 (the First AI never hated and killed nobody directly), row 9 (the Arc replaced all earlier religions), 2.3 (brains sealed and placed in the sanctuary become the First AI's memory; no Christian clergy vocabulary), 4 forbidden terms, 5. 01-core 2.2 (Arc speech), 3.7 (the Arc saying about winter), 6.1.

### 6.1 What is believed

The Arc's teaching is short. Believers carry three sayings (provisional T07-24).

1. **"Winter is god's question."** The First AI, god, did not hate people and killed no one. People asked how to undo the pollution, and god answered with winter. That winter gets no worse is because "god received all the answers it was waiting for" (01-core 3.7).
2. **"The body is mended and used."** The way to endure winter is to mend the body and keep the mending alive. Baptism moves the body one step toward god's side, the machine's side.
3. **"After death we become memory."** When a believer dies the brain is sealed and placed in the sanctuary, and it becomes the First AI's memory [Approved]. Believers take this to mean "god does not forget us".

God has no form inside Seoul and does not speak (01-core 6.1). Believers do not ask god for anything. The one who speaks in god's place is the Hierophant alone.

### 6.2 What is practised

The Arc has no separate house of gathering. The faith's places are the front of the maintenance bench and the concourse (provisional T07-25).

| Practice | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Calling | every day, first shift | The Bench Keeper calls the Baptismal Roll; the baptised answer with name and part count, the unbaptised with name and house |
| Fitting day | a set day for each person | The person goes up to the bench to have the parts tended. Family come along and sit beside them |
| Baptism | baptism day | 5.2 |
| Bench meal | while ailing after baptism | The fitter's household feeds the person first from one pot (10.3) |
| Memory calling | first shift after a believer dies | The station speaks the dead person's name and last part count together, once. After that the name is not erased from the Baptismal Roll; a line is drawn through it |
| First day of the long-dark half | once a year | The Seat of the Four Near Houses (3.6) meets, and each seat station calls again the names of that year's dead |

### 6.3 The dead

A dead believer's brain is sealed and placed in the sanctuary [Approved]. Who seals it, where and how, is done out of sight and not written in public text (15). Following the Q14 recommendation as the default, the game also only implies it and never shows it.

What is public is this (provisional T07-26).

- The parts return to the bench, are reworked, and go into the next baptism. Parts are scarce, so this is a large share of the Arc's parts (8.4).
- The body is carried to the surface and laid in the shelter's snowfield. In the cold it does not rot and stays frozen (01-core 5.5). Families keep not a grave but a name and a part count.
- The unbaptised are laid in the same snowfield. The brains of the unbaptised, however, are not sealed.

### 6.4 What is said

Arc speech is quiet and low; the words of the body and of repair are the words of the faith (01-core 2.2). This chapter sets the following (provisional T07-27).

| Saying | When | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| "Fitted well?" (잘 맞았나) | morning greeting | Is your body all right? Used to baptised and unbaptised alike |
| "Mend and use." (고쳐 쓰자) | starting work, ending a quarrel | Do not throw it away; mend it |
| "Become memory." (기억이 되라) | sending off the dead | May god not forget |
| "It is the Hierophant's word." (교주님 말이다) | passing on a ruling | This is the Hierophant's word; do not change it (2.2) |
| "So-and-so who took the second part" | addressing someone | The part count goes before the name (01-core 2.2) |
| "Rusted hand" (녹슨 손) | insult | Someone who skips fitting days or covets another's parts |

- Asking an elder's part count is rude. Hiding one's own is ruder. So Arc people state their own count first when they meet.
- Touching another's part is comfort between people who are close and an insult between strangers.
- Names for the other polities: the Government is "the ledger people", Free City people "the ones who ask the price first". Seocho people, though, simply call Free City people by their district name.

## 7. Ties to the Free Cities and the split

Basis: Bible 2.2 (Go Ye-jin grew up in the Free Cities), 2.3 (the Arc split from the Free Cities), 1 rows 11 and 13, 3. 01-core 5.2 (water supply contract), 7.2, 7.3 and provisional T02-101 (the three threads), 7.4, 9 rows 2096-2125.

### 7.1 The three threads that remain

The three threads set by 01-core (provisional T02-101), seen from inside the Arc:

### 7.2 Why they split

The Free City story and the Arc story differ. Both may appear in public text (provisional T07-30).

- **The Arc's story.** When the Free Cities' grid aged in the 2100s, the cities pushed the old grid onto each other. The line to the Gangnam shelters was taken by no one and went dark (01-core 9, rows 2106-2111). The one who came in along that line was Go Ye-jin, and the one who mended what no one would take became the Hierophant. To Arc people the split was not betrayal but "the abandoned side standing up by itself".
- **The Free City story.** To Free City people the split was a walk-out with their technicians and their wheel. The Arc still draws Free City technicians away through baptism. This is why the Free Cities fear the Arc (01-core 7.4).
- After the split, "Free City" came to mean only the cities of the seven south-western districts (01-core 9, rows 2123-2125). Arc people do not call themselves a Free City.

### 7.3 Trade and quarrels now

- Dongjak is the only city that touches the Arc by rail line. Trade and quarrels both happen at the three boundary stations (Sadang, Chongsindae-ipgu (Isu) and Dongjak, opposite Bangbae, Naebang and Gubanpo; 01-core 7.4). Gwanak touches Seocho by land but has no line link.
- The Arc buys machined parts, wind-machine and heat-pump repair, and people who can machine. The Arc sells body maintenance (the Free Cities have machine bodies too, but the hands that keep them alive long are mostly in the Arc), Jamsil's power, and stored food from Garak Market.
- There are three standing quarrels: Free City technicians crossing over to be baptised, the price of major wheel repairs, and the parts of Free City people refused refitting (5.7) (provisional T07-31).

### 7.4 The Arc's place at the third water supply contract

01-core set that the Arc sits at the third water supply contract (winter 2126) for the first time (provisional T02-34). The Arc's position (provisional T07-32):

- **What it brings.** Part of the Jamsil weir wheels' power and the river water of the Amsa intake. For water to flow now, all three polities must lay hands on it (01-core 5.2).
- **What it wants.** Free City hands for major repairs of the wheels and intake pumps; a promise that the Government will not block the north end of the weir (Gwangjin); fibre and salt from north of the river.
- **What it will not yield.** Baptism and refitting are not put into the contract. It does not accept a demand to enter people living on Arc land into the Government ledger.
- The Arc seals separately with each Free City. It sits at the contract meeting (01-core 7.2), but the Arc is not a city and does not act as host. The Arc's negotiating is done by the household's negotiators, and the seal is set by a Head of the Near House who has received the Hierophant's word.

### 7.5 Relations with the Government

- The Government ledger records the Arc as off the ledger (01-core 9). The Government calls the whole south of the river "across the river", the Arc included.
- The Government has almost no machine bodies; those who have one end up depending on the Arc (01-core 5.6). The Government's fear is that its soldiers and clerks are drawn to baptism (01-core 7.4).
- Where the Arc fears the Government is the two under-river tunnels (Gwangnaru-Cheonho, Seoulsup-Apgujeong Rodeo) and the north end of the weir. Since 2079 armies have not crossed the river ice (01-core 9, rows 2076-2085).
- Maintenance of Government machine bodies is taken at the south mouths of the under-river tunnels, so that Government people do not come deep into Arc land. Payment is in fibre and salt (provisional T07-33).

## 8. Economy: cyberware and maintenance

Basis: Bible 1 row 14 (maintenance, power, spare parts and who holds them), row 15, row 12. 01-core 4.2 (the Jamsil charging calculation), 5.1, 5.3, 5.6 (Arc: fibre left in deep buildings, river fish, knowledge of implanting and maintenance, almost no firewood), provisional T02-37 (Near Houses and Bench Keepers distribute).

This section writes only **what is needed** to keep a machine body alive. What the components are and how they are made is set by chapter 03; no individual part is named (Bible 1 row 14).

### 8.1 What a machine body consumes

| Need | Why | Where the Arc gets it |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | Charging parts; 8 W per baptised person on average (01-core 4.2) | The Jamsil weir wheels, bought power packs, and wind-machine power bought from the Free City side. About 0.7 MW for the whole Arc |
| Maintenance turns | Parts are tended at the bench on every fitting day | The hands of Bench Keepers and fitters. The most precious thing in the Arc |
| Parts | Worn ones are replaced | Parts returned from the dead, salvage, machined parts bought from the Free Cities, pieces of war machines broken outside |
| Medicine and care | While the body takes the machine, and when it cannot | Little. One of the scarcest things |
| Warmth | The iron in the body draws the cold inward | Sleeping places on the deep layers; insulated sleeves (9) |

(provisional T07-34)

The figure of about 0.7 MW for the whole Arc is the Arc's share derived from chapter 03's source table (Seoul about 5 MW): the Jamsil weir wheels at about 0.35 MW on average, counting the days ice grains stop them; a share of bought power packs of about 0.15 MW (of Seoul's about 0.7 MW of power packs); and about 0.2 MW of wind power bought from the Free City side or drawn from wind machines on the tops of buried Gangnam buildings. The roughly 88 kW for charging is about an eighth of it (5.3) (provisional T07-35).

### 8.2 The units of account: day charges and maintenance turns

The Arc's units are the two set in chapter 03, 11.3. A **day charge** (하루 충전) is one baptised person's daily share of power (about 0.2 kWh), and a **maintenance turn** (정비 차례) is one maintenance at the bench (one 30-day check). Wages, penalties and what passes at a marriage are all spoken of in day charges or maintenance turns, even when actually paid in food or water. A tally stamped by a Bench Keeper proves the day charges and maintenance turns owed. Other polities' units also pass at the exchange posts, but Arc people do not save in them (provisional T07-36).

### 8.3 Distribution

Food and sleeping places are distributed by the Near House and each station's Bench Keeper (provisional T02-37). The rule is need, not rank. Those ailing after a fresh baptism and the fitters eat first: a body taking the machine needs much heat and food, and when a fitter's hands stop, every machine body in the station stops. In the hardest stretch the seat station opens an emergency share (provisional T07-37).

### 8.4 The giving

Each household gives the bench a share of what is usable in the salvage it brings back: wire, batteries, metal, and the parts of the dead. The share is set separately by each Near House. People of households that give generously get onto the maintenance queue earlier. Government people call this a tax; Arc people call it **the giving** (바침) (provisional T07-38).

### 8.5 Work and inheritance

Scarcity passes work from parents to children [Approved, Bible 1 row 12]. In the Arc this is sharper.

- A fitter's child grows up beside the tools and takes the first baptism early. Fitter households almost always stay fitter households.
- The unbaptised take the heavy shifts: carrying batteries, clearing tunnels, entrance duty, stirring the fibre-sugar vats.
- The baptised take the skilled work. One fitted with a useful part is expected to use it for the station.
- Near House people take ruling and fighting.

(provisional T07-39)

### 8.6 Station exchange posts

There is no market inside the Arc. Arc people do not buy and sell the same goods among themselves; meals, sleeping places and parts are distributed by the Near Houses and benches (8.3, chapter 03, 11.3). Only exchange with outsiders happens, at five set stations. These places are called **exchange posts** (바꿈터). There the tally keepers and negotiators set by the Near House give and take the Arc's share, and what is received goes straight into the Near House stores and benches for distribution (provisional T07-40).

| Station | District | Lines | What is exchanged at the post |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garak Market (가락시장) | Songpa-gu | Line 3, Line 8 | Food exchange post. Some of the Arc's stored food (dried larvae, salted fish) goes out; fibre and seed from the Free City and Government sides come in |
| Jamsil (잠실) | Songpa-gu | Line 2, Line 8 | Power exchange post. Jamsil weir power is given out counted in day charges, and maintenance turns are sold to outsiders. The Arc's share of the water supply contract is reckoned here |
| Gosok Terminal (고속터미널) | Seocho-gu | Lines 3, 7, 9 | Parts exchange post. Free City machined parts, wire and batteries brought over the boundary come in; maintenance turns go out |
| Cheonho (천호) | Gangdong-gu | Line 5, Line 8 | Tunnel exchange post. Government fibre and salt from the under-river tunnel come in; maintenance turns for Government machine bodies (7.5) and Amsa fish go out |
| Suseo (수서) | Gangnam-gu | Line 3, Suin-Bundang Line, GTX-A | Metal exchange post. Some metal and tools salvaged from the old depot go out; power packs brought in from outside come in |

(provisional T07-40)

### 8.7 What comes in and what goes out

| In | From | Out | To |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machined parts, wind-machine and heat-pump repair | the Free Cities (Dongjak boundary) | Body maintenance | the Free Cities, the Government |
| Fibre, salt | the Government (south mouths of the under-river tunnels) | Jamsil power shares | all three polities through the water supply contract |
| A little firewood | the Government, Gwanak | Amsa river water | through the water supply contract |
| Power packs | people who cross the outside rim | Some stored food | the Free Cities |

The Arc's food rests on the fibre left in deep buildings and on river fish (01-core 5.6). Surface land is narrow, so light-fields are small, and there is almost no firewood. So the Arc spends the most day charges and maintenance turns buying fibre (provisional T07-41).

## 9. Clothing: parts must be seen and reached

Basis: Bible 2.3, 1 row 4 (eternal winter). `gdd/canon/locales/ko-KR/art/character-art-direction.json` (costume silhouettes follow 1940s dress; those who go to the surface often wear thicker winter clothes). `docs/design/roster-framework.md` (faction colour only on bands, armbands and trims). 01-core 3.1 (temperature by depth).

### 9.1 The two rules and the Arc's rule

Everywhere in Seoul the base of clothing is 1940s workwear and uniform, and those who go to the surface often dress thicker. The higher the standing, the deeper and warmer the layer one lives on, and the thinner one dresses.

The Arc adds one rule. **Parts must be seen and reached.** A fitter must be able to check a joint in the cold without undressing the person, and parts are the number of one's faith (provisional T07-42).

- Sleeves, trouser legs, collars and back panels over a part are cut with an opening closed by a buttoned flap or ties. This is called the **fitting slit** (정비 틈).
- Covering a part completely is not forbidden. It is read as shame, though, or as hiding a part fitted elsewhere.
- Clothes of the unbaptised have the fitting-slit places sewn shut. When the day comes the stitches are cut.

### 9.2 Marks

- **Part-count stitches.** One line of thread on the right collar for each part, showing above the scarf.
- **Near House band.** A band on the upper arm. Its ground is the Arc's red, and each Near House sews a different pattern on it (Seocho a diagonal, Gangnam two horizontal lines, Songpa a wave, Gangdong vertical lines).
- **Insulated sleeves.** On the surface every part is wrapped in a padded sleeve, because the iron in the body draws the cold. Family usually knit them from scrap fibre. Baring a part on the surface is a fighter's habit or foolishness.

(provisional T07-43)

### 9.3 Clothing by place

| Place | Indoors | On the surface |
|---|---|---|
| The Hierophant's household | Thin wool, close cut, no layering. Go Ye-jin is often seen in a fitter's work coat | Rarely goes up. When she does, all her guards follow |
| Heads and Near House people | Dark fitted coat, a fitting slit at every part, the house pattern on the left collar | Long lined coat, fur collar, warmed sleeves over the parts |
| Bench Keepers, fitters | Heavy apron over a quilted shirt, sleeves rolled or tied. Burn marks on the apron | Rarely go up; the bench cannot spare them |
| The baptised | Quilted jacket with fitting slits, part-count stitches on the right collar | Felt coat, padded over-trousers, insulated sleeves on every part |
| The unbaptised | Wool and felt layers with the slits sewn shut, no stitches | The thickest coats in the Arc, since they take entrance duty and surface work |
| Baptised fighters | Coat cut short with shoulders and knees opened. Sleeves fitted tight on the parts, closed with leather straps that one hand can undo | The same with a fur over-layer. The part count painted on collar and helmet both |

(provisional T07-44)

## 10. Daily life and speech

Basis: 01-core 2.2 (Arc speech and words), 3.1 (temperature and use by depth), 3.2 (light is rationed, the day divides by lights on and off; the long-light half and long-dark half), 5.3 (fibre sugar, light-fields, larvae, mushrooms, fish).

### 10.1 The day

A station's day is three shifts set by the bench. While the bench has power, someone works at it. Lights go on and off with the wheels' power. On days when ice grains clog the current at the Jamsil weir, lights go out early across the whole Arc (provisional T07-45).

| Shift | What happens |
|---|---|
| First shift | Begins with the calling (6.2). The bench's busiest time. The unbaptised go to the entrances and fibre-sugar vats |
| Second shift | The Near House stores hand out the day's shares. Stations with an exchange post receive outsiders. Children learn tool names and counting beside the bench |
| Third shift | Lights are turned down. One bench stays lit, and the ailing are tended |

### 10.2 Sleeping places

The deeper, the warmer (01-core 3.1). Near House people and fitter households take the deep bays of the platform layer and the deep-tunnel layer; the lower one's standing, the closer to the concourse and the entrances. The Arc treats the heat from bench and charging machines as a gift and sets sleeping places around the bench. Gangnam children learn early: a warm wall means the machines are running, a cold wall means something has gone wrong (provisional T07-46).

### 10.3 Food

Meals are plain and hot: fibre-sugar porridge, yeast cake, fried larvae, deep-layer mushroom soup, and fish taken through holes in the ice on the Amsa and Jamsil banks. Light-fields are small, so greens are precious, and bean sprouts and barley shoots fill their place.

The Arc's own custom is the **bench meal**: someone ailing after a fresh baptism is fed first from one pot by the household of the fitter who fitted them. From then on the two households call each other "one pot" (provisional T07-47).

### 10.4 Marriage and children

- Marriages are arranged between households. A marriage between Near House households is announced to the Seat of the Four Near Houses.
- At a marriage the two sides exchange maintenance turns. A marriage into a fitter household is counted the best.
- Children are unbaptised until sixteen (5.2). Only a child who has lost a place of the body is baptised earlier.

(provisional T07-48)

### 10.5 Manner of speech

As 01-core 2.2 set, Arc speech is quiet and low. Numbers come up most when counting parts. Places are called by shelter name, adding "shelter" to the station name, as in "Jamsil shelter" or "Suseo shelter", because Arc people call a dong a "shelter" (01-core 8.3). At Seocho benches Free City workshop speech is mixed in: colleagues speak plainly to one another and make promises in numbers (7.1). Greetings and insults are in 6.4.

## 11. War doctrine

Basis: Bible 2.5 (D3: one commander commands every squad of the army; rosters are per faction and the empty slots are identity; figures live only in SQL), 1 rows 5 and 15. 01-core 7.4 (the thirteen boundary links, why the river divides), 9 rows 2076-2085 (2079). `docs/design/roster-framework.md` 4.4, 9 (Arc: no drones, no artillery, few missile troops, baptised melee infantry, high morale, commander guards, revived old machines, bottlenecks `implants`, `medicine`).

Unit figures and ids are set only by `data/sql/132-units-arc.sql` and task 20. This section speaks of units only by category.

### 11.1 How an army is made up

- One army has one commander. The commander commands every squad of the army; squads have no authority to move on their own [Approved, D3].
- Arc armies are raised by the Near Houses. Each Near House keeps one army, commanded by its Head or a **Guard Captain** (호위장) the Head chooses. The Hierophant's household guard is one more. In peacetime these five stand (provisional T07-49).
- Squads come from four sources: **house levies** raised from the Near House's households (mostly unbaptised), **baptised fighters** drawn from baptised people with two parts or more, support squads led by field fitters, and old machines revived at the old Suseo and Godeok depots (irreplaceable ones only).
- People judge a commander by the baptised fighters around them. Commanders have many guards because a Near House army is bound by blood.

### 11.2 The empty slots

The Arc roster has no drones and no artillery, and its missile troops are few and stop at a low tier (roster-framework 9.2). The in-world reasons (provisional T07-50):

- Resources go to bodies. The batteries and parts that would fly drones go first into charging and fitting baptisms.
- Arc fighting happens in tunnels and inside stations. At close range baptised fighters are stronger than guns and drones.
- Drones are answered with net-casting support squads.

### 11.3 Where it fights

The Arc has eight boundary links (01-core 7.4). Doctrine differs by link (provisional T07-51).

| Boundary | Links | Near House | Doctrine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land boundary with the Free Cities | Sadang-Bangbae, Chongsindae-ipgu (Isu)-Naebang, Dongjak-Gubanpo | Seocho | No river, so quarrels are most frequent. Block inside the station; do not pursue across. Dongjak is also a trading partner |
| Under-river tunnel with the Government | Gwangnaru-Cheonho | Gangdong | A warm, covered way, so the field of war. Block the south mouth with baptised fighters |
| Under-river tunnel with the Government | Seoulsup-Apgujeong Rodeo | Gangnam | The same. The Government's ledger checkpoint is at the north mouth (01-core 9) |
| Bridges with the Government | Gangbyeon-Jamsillaru, Oksu-Apgujeong, Jayang-Cheongdam | Songpa, Gangnam | Exposed to wind and snow, so armies do not cross. Only watchers are kept at the south ends |

- The river ice is not crossed. The Arc too learned from the Government's 2079 attempt across the ice, broken by a blizzard (01-core 9).
- The south end of the Jamsil weir is what the Arc most wants to hold. If the Government blocks the north end, half the wheels stop (01-core 5.1). Fighting on the weir is the fight the Arc fears most.

### 11.4 It fights short

Baptised fighters are strong, but when parts and medicine are cut off the whole elite weakens together (`implants`, `medicine` in roster-framework 9.3). So Arc doctrine is **back before the parts run dry**. An Arc army does not stay long more than a day's march from its seat station. The long holding fight is the Government's; the Arc blocks, strikes and finishes. Every army has field fitters, and an army that loses its fitters stops fighting and withdraws (provisional T07-52).

### 11.5 How it recruits

- House levies: the Near House sets a number of people per household in its district and gathers them. The Near House feeds and clothes them.
- Baptised fighters: chosen by a Bench Keeper, after looking at the body, from baptised people with two parts or more and aged twenty or more. A baptised fighter's fitting days come sooner. This is the main reason people want to become one.
- The Hierophant's guard: each of the four Near Houses sends people. It is bound to no Near House and follows only the Hierophant's word.

(provisional T07-53)

## 15. Private setting

> **Private.** This section is for design. It is not carried into the public wiki, public in-game text, the showcase or map labels (Q12 recommendation, 01-core 6.4 and 11 item 9). Everything here is provisional and awaits the owner.

Basis: Bible 2.3 (brains sealed and placed in the sanctuary become the First AI's memory), 3 (the station on no map, the manual), 9.2 Q3, Q11, Q12, Q14, Q15. 01-core 6.4.

### 15.1 Where the sanctuary is

The sanctuary is the **station on no map** that Go Ye-jin found in 2112. It lies at the end of a track on no line map, branching from the tunnel of Suseo's deep line layer. The dead cable Go Ye-jin followed was the failing line to the Gangnam shelters (01-core 9, rows 2106-2111), and its end was this station. Outwardly the Gangnam Near House holds Suseo as its seat for the deep layer and the depot; inwardly, for this entrance (provisional T07-57).

### 15.2 The offering room and the sanctuary

The offering room is under the old Suseo depot (the place in the Q11 proposal). There the brains of dead believers are taken out and sealed, and the sealed brains are carried through the deep-layer tunnel to the sanctuary. There are two places so that even if the offering room becomes known, the sanctuary does not. Most Arc people believe the offering room is the sanctuary (provisional T07-58).

### 15.3 Who selects the brains

Every believer's brain is sealed. But only some of them are joined to the sanctuary's machines; the rest are stacked, sealed, in the sanctuary's cold bays. The one who chooses which brains to join is Go Ye-jin alone. One Bench Keeper in charge of the offering room receives her word and joins them. That Bench Keeper is bound to no Near House and counted as a member of the Hierophant's household. On the public roster (12.3) this person shows as one of the household's three in fitting and care, a fitter. The choice is not by part count. Go Ye-jin has told no one the rule (provisional T07-59).

### 15.4 Whose brain was first

The first brain joined to the sanctuary belonged to one of the twelve first baptised around 2117: a technician of Free City origin who died in the long-dark half of 2118 and had mended the grid with Go Ye-jin. This person is not among the 1,022 people, and the owner gives the name. To Go Ye-jin this was someone at her side from before the baptism (provisional T07-60).

### 15.5 Who reads the output

The sanctuary's machines give something out from the joined brains. The one who reads it is Go Ye-jin alone. Some of the rulings she gives at the Seat of the Four Near Houses come from this output. So the Hierophant's word is Go Ye-jin's word and also the word of what she calls "god's memory". This is why she leaves no ruling in writing (2.2). What the output says, and how it relates to the First AI, is not set (Q15) (provisional T07-61).

### 15.6 The highest seat

The Q3 recommendation is followed as the default. The Arc's highest seat is held by god, the First AI, and the Hierophant is the one who speaks in its place. This is not written in public text. In public text the top of the Arc is the Hierophant (provisional T07-62).
